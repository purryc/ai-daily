The cleaning job starts before the robot moves

A scheduled cleaning run may still begin with somebody picking up charging leads, socks and pet toys. Vacuum Wars published a purchased-unit L40 Ultra Gen 2 evaluation on October 27. Its favorable mopping findings sit beside avoidance of only two objects in a 24-object course. Cleaning a surface well and completing a run unattended are separate outcomes. This is fresh independent evidence about existing hardware, not a new product launch. For a household whose floors change throughout the day, obstacle preparation and rescues belong in the same accounting as time saved on mopping. An advertised suction figure cannot answer that whole-job question.

Extending hardware reaches edges; the dock handles follow-up

The product has a concrete operating sequence: choose areas or a schedule in the app, then let the robot follow its map. An extending side brush and mop pad reach beyond the circular body to address edges. Mop lifting helps separate wet cleaning from carpet travel. The dock subsequently handles debris collection, pad washing and hot-air drying. Hot-air drying should not be confused with hot-water washing; the review describes room-temperature washing for this model. Dreame separately lists a detergent-dispenser module and consumables. Owners still refill clean water, empty dirty water and service bags or brushes, so an automated dock removes part of maintenance rather than the entire task.

Similar names do not guarantee the same sensors

L40 Ultra, L40s Ultra, L40 Ultra Gen 2 and AE variants should not simply be ranked by how new their names sound. The review describes structured-light and LDS sensing on Gen 2, a different arrangement from the original camera-assisted L40. That difference does not isolate a single component as the cause of its result. Surface, object placement and software can also affect behavior. A buyer should therefore inspect the full model suffix, included main brush and accessory list. Otherwise a favorable description of another variant can create an expectation that the purchased machine was never configured to meet, especially around small obstacles or tangled hair.

Use a lab result for the question it answers

A laboratory course supplies comparable difficult cases, not the probability of failure in every home. Someone with consistently clear floors and a mopping priority faces a different tradeoff from a household with long hair, scattered objects and pet areas. The review’s summary and detailed table disagree on the precise hair-tangle percentage, so this account retains the direction of the finding rather than selecting a convenient number. In an actual cleaning routine, count preparation, brush servicing and rescue interventions. Frequent extra steps do not erase good mopping performance, but they change what the purchase delivers: useful automated cleaning with recurring supervision, rather than a reliably unattended chore.

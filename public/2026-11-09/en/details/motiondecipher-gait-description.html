MotionDecipher structures gait waveforms before drafting descriptions, without prescribing treatment

The input is a processed movement signal

MotionDecipher takes processed joint-angle and ground-reaction-force waveforms and drafts gait descriptions organized by body part. It is not a patient-facing diagnostic application. For someone adopting a reporting tool, the first practical concern is the integrity of the input: acquisition conditions, missing segments and left-versus-right labels should be checked before prose is generated. That is our implementation judgment, not an additional evaluated feature. Fluent language can make a basic data error harder to notice. A useful review surface would keep the relevant measurement close enough that a clinician can question a sentence without reconstructing the entire analysis.

Domain structure precedes text generation

The pipeline encodes domain features, selects relevant information and retrieves expert examples before language generation. The product lesson is about preparing evidence rather than copying a long prompt. For a statement about one joint, a reviewer should be able to identify the phase and reference used. Our suggested test set would include symmetric movements, boundary cases and incomplete measurements, examining whether confident wording conceals uncertainty. These are evaluation ideas for a reporting system, not instructions for readers to interpret clinical data themselves. The interface should make a questionable statement easier to challenge, even when it sounds technically polished.

[S37]

The demonstrated setup remains laboratory based

The authors evaluated data from 122 patients and 46 controls using optical motion capture and force plates. Lower-cost video and wearable sensing remain future work. A purchasing decision must therefore consider the complete workflow: equipment, calibration, staff, processing and professional review, not just model runtime. We would measure where effort actually moves after introducing automated drafting. Faster text generation could still leave substantial correction work, or change how long a reviewer spends checking unusual cases. Those costs cannot be inferred from a language-similarity score, and laboratory results should not be advertised as proof that a phone can replace the validated sensing setup.

[S37]

Description is not a treatment decision

The present scope is description of existing gait patterns, not personalized treatment recommendations or demonstrated long-term health improvement. Adoption would require clear review responsibility and a visible route back to manual analysis when a sentence lacks support. An unusual but genuine movement should not be flattened merely to resemble a retrieved example, while measurement noise should not become a clinical claim. We would want those two error types reported separately and retested after changing the sensing equipment. Neither model scores nor the authors’ efficiency claims settle cross-device performance or the reliability of a complete clinical workflow. No clinical deployment was tested by this publication.

Annotations become the task boundary

Lea supplies a shared formalization backend for LeaChat and an Overleaf integration. An author can annotate statements and dependencies in the paper, then inspect the corresponding Lean work. For a manuscript under revision, this is a useful place to define the task boundary: identify the intended statement before generating its proof. Our interpretation is that the integration can reduce bookkeeping between a document and separate conversations, but only when the annotations themselves are precise. Missing hypotheses remain a mathematical problem. Putting them in a structured interface does not automatically make them complete or appropriate.

Compilation and human acceptance are different states

The system distinguishes formalization without sorry, additional SafeVerify verification and a human-only audited status. That distinction addresses a practical trap: a perfectly checked proof can concern a weaker statement than the author intended. A useful adoption test would deliberately omit a hypothesis and examine whether the original target and produced statement remain easy to compare. A green status icon alone would not answer that question. This test concerns the collaboration boundary rather than replacing mathematical checking with a subjective score. It should include rejected and disproved outcomes, because finding a wrong conjecture can be a useful result for the author.

Edits should not inherit obsolete review

Artifacts and review evidence are tied to their inputs, and relevant changes make results stale. Sessions can continue across the two clients. For a real paper, the revealing task is changing a definition and then locating the statements that need renewed attention. We would record the cost of human reinspection separately from the quantity of generated code. Excessive invalidation can create review fatigue, while missed dependencies can leave obsolete conclusions circulating. Those errors need separate counts. A completion rate alone would hide the tradeoff, particularly when a manuscript is repeatedly revised rather than formalized once and left untouched.

[S33]

One case does not establish broad performance

The paper reports a Durfee-square case study with a substantive human correction, no controlled comparison, and output still requiring cleanup. Concurrent multi-mathematician work is not supported. A sensible trial would therefore accompany a small part of a paper rather than promise autonomous completion of a research program. We would examine statement correspondence, dependency invalidation and the time spent reviewing the result. The architecture offers a specific collaboration mechanism; it does not establish that every mathematical domain benefits equally. Commercial service access, pricing and all deployment combinations were not verified, and we did not run the software or reproduce its proof.

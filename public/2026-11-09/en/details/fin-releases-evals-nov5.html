Keep related edits in one release

A refund-policy update can require changes to explanatory content, escalation rules and the procedure that takes action. Shipping those separately risks inconsistent answers. Fin announced Releases and Evals on November 5: one groups related configuration changes, while the other exercises conversation behavior. Opening a release changes the interface color and identifies the version; saving there leaves customers on Main Fin. This gives support teams a place to coordinate changes that belong together. It can reduce the separate tracking needed to remember which edits should ship together, but the team must still decide whether its proposed policy is appropriate.

Exercise a conversation before rollout

The team defines an Eval around a topic, supplies simulated customer openings and context, and specifies the checks. A model plays the customer across multiple turns; an LLM judge scores the outcome. Results include pass or fail and a transcript that can be inspected. Running against a release keeps those results separate from Main Fin, and subsequent edits mark the earlier results stale. This supports repeatable checks without assuming that real customers will follow the same script. Ambiguous success criteria still produce weak evidence. Reviewers should inspect both failures and suspicious passes instead of treating a green result as an explanation.

Testing and publishing remain separate decisions

Evals are recommended rather than a mandatory publication gate. Teams therefore need an explicit review owner; the product does not force testing before shipment. Propose or publish permissions govern test creation, editing and execution, while publishing a combined release requires rights for every included change type. A successful handoff check only confirms that Fin transferred a conversation: the downstream workflow is not simulated. An external refund or notification still needs its own verification. Each Eval permits up to fifty simulations, and workspace monthly allowances count runs once started. Operator can help prepare and schedule tests through the Pro add-on; manual Evals do not require Operator. Completed runs currently have no notification, so an assigned reviewer must return to inspect the results.

A limited rollout still needs supervision

Publishers can replace Main Fin immediately, limit a release to an audience and traffic share, or compare it with Main Fin through an A/B test. A running release is locked; changing its percentage means ending the rollout and starting another. Results show resolution-rate effects with uncertainty, so a small apparent difference should not be sold as proven improvement. Ending a rollout returns even ongoing conversations to Main Fin. After merging, reversal relies on item histories, with exclusions including escalation guidance, snippets, internal articles, evals and deleted items. Before adoption, rehearse one real policy change through preparation, review, limited exposure and recovery to establish who owns each step.

Keep the request beside the recipe

NYT Cooking places a customization box above a recipe’s ingredients. A request can concern an available ingredient, a different pan or another practical constraint. Follow-up questions can narrow the change before the user opens a personal version, with alterations shown in orange and a toggle back to the original. The design keeps the result beside the task it modifies rather than leaving instructions in a detached chat. That matters when someone is cooking and cannot repeatedly compare two documents. Color makes a difference easier to inspect; it does not establish that every dependent timing, quantity and preparation step has been reconciled correctly.

A new review finds a downstream miss

The November 8 event is a new WIRED cooking review, not the feature’s original launch. The reviewer found substitutions useful overall but reported a chicken-breast variation that retained a later cooking stage and produced less succulent meat. That is one observed case, not a failure-rate estimate. Its broader design implication is that changing an ingredient creates dependencies across the procedure: when it enters, what accompanies it and how long it remains under heat. A useful review follows that ingredient through every step. Checking only its first highlighted occurrence could miss a later instruction inherited from the original recipe.

Sources and exits are part of the interface

The help page describes an ordered source preference: the recipe, a substitution guide, community comments and then AI applying editorial principles. A Sources control explains the basis, and a request too far from the dish may lead to a different recipe. Each chat maintains one evolving customized version; a fresh chat starts another, and history entries can be deleted. Current versions support printing and Cook Mode but cannot be placed in Recipe Box, rated or shared. Those boundaries affect practical handoff. They also help prevent a generated variation from silently borrowing the tested original’s reputation or mixing its results into the original’s ratings.

[S02]

Treat the result as a versioned draft

Access described in the review is an iOS beta for subscribers; availability elsewhere was not established. No recipe was cooked for this report. A restrained adoption test would use a familiar dish and a low-stakes substitution, then compare the ingredient list with every affected step before cooking. The important control is a reversible, inspectable draft with an original reference, not merely a fluent answer. An allergy, preservation method or special diet should not become a safety guarantee because a generated version looks orderly. The help page also says chat and usage information are collected for improvement, so users should avoid entering personal health details they do not want retained.

The repeated work sits between two records

One historical discussion describes a manager overseeing four projects and trying to reduce repeated checks across submittals, supplier lead times and the site schedule. A separate thread contains a user's report of dates remaining unchanged after schedule revisions, with Excel as the workaround. These are distinct account reports, not a measured market. Only the bodies and visible comments were read; replies also point to training and data maintenance as possible explanations.

What the native product already covers

Procore's September 15 announcement already covers native log generation and the Dynamic Submittal Plan open beta. A generic AI register generator therefore has a strong existing substitute. This week's Groundbreak reporting adds bulk submittal updates and a procurement-log agent still in closed beta. The narrower boundary appears in the current Canadian creation guide: an initial schedule link supplies dates, but later changes to the activity do not automatically update the submittal. Recalculation after editing submittal inputs is a different operation.

Return differences that need a decision

The proposed product is a change checklist. It would read two schedule versions, exported submittals and lead times confirmed by project staff, then show old and new dates, affected records and the origin of each lead-time assumption. Stable activity IDs support deterministic comparison; a model could suggest ambiguous matches for human confirmation. An earlier installation date does not authorize shortening contractual review time, a constraint also reflected in the native in-progress workflow. The output should explain conflicts rather than silently rewrite commitments. This needs no new sensor or replacement construction platform.

Seeking a tool is not buying this service

The second thread's author was open to a one-off purchase or subscription for creating a register. That is adjacent payment interest, not an order for date reconciliation. No corresponding budget, transaction or renewal was verified. Construction-management communities, Procore user groups and contractor associations are plausible discovery channels, subject to their participation rules. Reachable unique people, a deduplicated prospect list and conversion rates are unknown. A buyer might prefer occasional project cleanup to another recurring software subscription.

[S56]

Start with one readonly rehearsal

A first rehearsal can use synthetic files containing changed, renamed and split activities plus missing lead times, with a knowledgeable reviewer preparing the answer key. No production connection is necessary. Check for missed differences that affect procurement decisions, false merges and whether review is easier than the existing spreadsheet. Only then consider permissioned, de-identified exports. First inspect the customer's actual version, beta settings and existing integrations. The enablement guide says some established projects require Procore assistance; an inactive feature is not evidence of an absent capability.

[S58]

Count the support work before continuing

A first rehearsal could be capped at six hours and CAD100 of additional spending; these are proposed experiment limits, not measured costs. Per-project contribution must subtract mapping and review labor, model calls, platform charges, rework and refunds, support and acquisition. With revenue unknown, keep the formula rather than inventing a margin. Stop if the native setup already synchronizes the changes, exports lack usable identifiers, or correcting false alarms takes longer than manual comparison. Further integration requires reliable mappings across successive revisions and an actual willingness to pay for the work removed.

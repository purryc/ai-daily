This week adds compute and safety detail

The October 22 announcement says Digit 5 will standardize onboard compute on IGX Thor and incorporate Halos capabilities into its human-detection and safety architecture. Final third-party certification remains ahead, with early access expected in the first half of 2027. That belongs on a procurement timeline: a clearer architecture enables discussion of interfaces, operating limits and evaluation evidence without establishing general delivery. A common compute supplier cannot by itself validate every workflow. The relevant conditions include particular movements, nearby people and exceptions, not simply the processor’s name.

Who can still stop it after the network drops

The Q&A separates Arc’s fleet orchestration from onboard control and describes an isolated safety computer that can shut down the robot when a rule is violated. Connectivity loss does not remove that function, according to Agility. This division gives a site team a concrete acceptance question: can it distinguish a queued task, interrupted execution and a safety stop? If orchestration disappears while a box remains in the gripper, an operator needs to understand the physical task state rather than see only an offline cloud icon. This is an evaluation scenario, not a network-loss test performed for this article.

The gripper and interfaces define the useful job

The product page describes swappable grippers and fleet software connecting to compatible AMRs, WMS, WES and MES environments. These interfaces indicate that the input is richer than “move that box”: a task also has an origin, destination, material state and upstream release condition. The result should return to the original system so that workers do not manually reconcile a move the robot has already finished. Changing an end effector broadens the jobs worth attempting but does not remove differences between containers, grasping surfaces or loads. A repeatable handoff is a more useful starting unit than a general demonstration.

Include recovery in the workflow being purchased

For a facility using fixed arms, conveyors or manual handling, compare one completed and recorded handoff. A pilot could choose a well-defined material and inspect wrong destinations, failed grips, a person entering the area and loss of system connectivity. Clear stops and understandable recovery determine how much work returns to an operator after the demonstration ends. Pricing, orderable configurations and independently observed recovery outcomes were not established, so a payback period cannot be calculated. This week supplies better questions for a supplier; it does not establish readiness for arbitrary factory workflows.

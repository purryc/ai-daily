A revision should preserve the next edit

A poster may begin as a sketch, acquire a retouched photograph and end with type and production dimensions. The November 2 launch puts those tasks into studios sharing one document. The useful distinction is the object available for the next correction: a node, mask, text frame or page can still be edited after an image operation. Pencil pressure and tilt support drawing, while direct pointing and touch support positioning. For a designer revising work during a conversation, the potential saving is a file handoff and the search for the right version, rather than a promise that the first generated result is finished.

Separate editor access from AI access

Core editing and AI access have separate terms: individuals can use the editor free with a Canva account, while Canva AI Studio requires a premium plan. A free installation therefore does not settle the cost or conditions of AI use. The vendor distinguishes local Affinity files from content uploaded to Canva; that distinction is useful without being an independent privacy audit. Before generating another variation, identify the actual change. Adjusting a mask edge, moving type or correcting a color may be faster through direct manipulation and less likely to disturb details already approved by a client.

Try a round trip before switching tools

The launch FAQ lists absent desktop functions, and complex imported effects or smart objects can flatten. A unified document is not proof of lossless interchange. A practical comparison duplicates an existing project, changes a heading, repairs an image edge, adds a page and sends the export back to its usual collaborator. Count missing objects, type changes and manual repair alongside elapsed time. If the receiving tool requires reconstruction of important structure, the iPad may still be useful for sketching and local revision without replacing the entire production chain. This is a proposed check, not an editor-performed interoperability test.

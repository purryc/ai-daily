APF’s procurement asks people to find content; diagnose navigation before proposing AI search

2026-10-22 RFP / November 9 recheck · Supplementary observation November 9; historical cases are not new events this week. · Observed / rechecked 2026-11-09

The buyer specifies a concrete task

The public brief describes a content-rich site that is difficult to navigate and requests actionable UX recommendations rather than implementation. That is more specific than a request for AI. Readers need relevant research and staff need a maintainable content structure. A proposed diagnostic starts with retrieval tasks on the existing site and records wrong turns, misunderstood terms and missing filters. We have not tested actual visitors.

[S38]

Budget is different from a contract

A published budget is stronger evidence than verbal interest, but no award or payment is established. Revenue cannot simply equal the cap: recruitment, bilingual research, tools, taxes, coordination and rework consume it. The procurement notice is a public route, not permission to contact arbitrary employees. For a broader market thesis, this remains one buyer with no independently repeated procurement or comparable conversion rate.

[S38]

A small proposal should test maintainability

A minimal proposal can manually adjust a small copied taxonomy and compare completion, wrong paths and editorial maintenance effort. Retrieval or generation assistance becomes relevant only if native changes leave a reproducible residual gap. Stop if naming alone resolves it, scope exceeds viable cost, necessary material is unavailable or bilingual validation cannot be supported. The opportunity is bounded diagnosis and handoff, not an asserted empty market for generic AI search.

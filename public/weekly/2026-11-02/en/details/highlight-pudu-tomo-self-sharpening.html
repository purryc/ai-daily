TOMO adds sharpening to docking, making maintenance part of the mowing task

Docking prepares the next job

A mower can follow a route while its owner still replaces blades, cleans the machine and resolves stoppages. TOMO extends automation into the interval between cuts by adding sharpening at the dock. This addresses gradual blade wear rather than another perception benchmark. The announcement does not show that sharpening repairs impact damage or eliminates consumable costs. A purchasing decision therefore needs the grinding mechanism’s service requirements, fault reporting and remaining inspection schedule. Those questions concern the complete recurring job: a machine that starts another run is useful only if it is actually ready to cut.

Check the boundary, not just the sensor list

The product combines several sensing methods with zone, angle and height controls. Avoiding a local RTK base does not remove every network dependency. Trees, flower beds and narrow passages present different boundary conditions, and a complete map may not match the owner’s intended mowing area. A small-area trial can examine missed strips, repeated tracks and the return route before expanding coverage. Sensor variety is an implementation detail, not evidence that every yard works. No field mowing was performed for this edition, and behavior when network corrections disappear remains a question for the actual model and installation.

[S12]

Compare maintenance with another household robot

This week’s MOVA recommendation withdrawal supplies a useful comparison, not evidence of a TOMO defect. A robot can perform well in controlled cleaning tests while routine water delivery or pad attachment still requires repeated intervention. The common design question is how much maintenance remains with the owner. For TOMO, the meaningful benefit would be fewer manual interventions across a growing season, rather than one successful sharpening demonstration. Its planned sales date leaves time for service details and independent evidence to emerge. Until then, the dock is an interesting mechanism whose net labor saving remains unmeasured.

Perplexity separates standing work from queued instructions

A standing responsibility needs a revisable brief

Teammates retains instructions and schedules for recurring work such as collecting material or preparing a familiar deliverable. The owner still needs to name sources, triggers, audience and destination. Watching a channel does not specify which changes deserve a report or when work should stop. Repeated setup may decrease while maintaining the brief becomes a continuing responsibility. A moved source, changed permission or new team priority can invalidate an otherwise well-written recurring task. The useful comparison is total preparation and review effort over repeated runs, not whether the first run can be scheduled.

Queued intent has not executed

Held follow-ups wait until a turn ends and are then consumed in order; they can be edited, removed or sent immediately. That intermediate state avoids interrupting work for every remembered requirement, but the current result may also make a waiting instruction stale. The interface needs to distinguish received intent from executed work. A useful trial introduces two conflicting follow-ups and checks the final artifact and action order rather than relying on conversational acknowledgement. The queue offers a place to resolve intent, not a guarantee that sequential instructions are mutually consistent.

[S24]

Local, hybrid and cloud paths need separate checks

The release describes fully local and hybrid Mac paths as well as cloud teammates. Hardware, app-version and memory conditions for one path do not establish that another is offline. Role controls affect effort and speed, while access still depends on connected services and actual role assignments. Begin with a low-risk recurring responsibility and record missing inputs, refusals, retries and human corrections. Name the person who takes ownership of the result. These operational distinctions make the feature assessable without turning vendor availability into an unmeasured claim of autonomous reliability.

Task and entry point

Promptile targets people who repeatedly explain how they want an LLM to work, such as frequent paper readers. It extracts preference fragments from previous conversations and lets people select, edit and recombine them for a current task. This is an October19 HCI preprint, not verified commercial availability. A useful evaluation task is carrying forward a personal paper-reading method without importing assumptions from the previous paper. PM inference: the likely beneficiary is an experienced user with successful habits but no maintained template library. Someone with only occasional questions may spend more effort curating than they recover. A promising onboarding decision would show one valuable historical preference before asking the person to understand a whole memory system.

Operation and control

The prototype presents candidate fragments in a side panel and filtered suggestions in the system input. Users can inspect provenance, drag fragments into place, delete or rewrite them, reorder them, and invoke Polish. Acceptance and rejection affect later recommendations. PM inference: the meaningful state is which earlier requirements will apply to this request. A product should distinguish a one-time exception from a lasting preference change; removing academic tone today should not silently rewrite next week's research workflow. Provenance should help explain why an instruction worked previously, rather than merely opening an old transcript. A concrete acceptance test would combine a preserve-terminology fragment with an explain-to-children fragment and check whether the conflict becomes visible before submission.

Evidence and adoption

In a within-subject study with16 student participants, manual input was51.38 versus91.63 characters for the normal ChatGPT workflow with global memory. Average input time was145.93 versus137.21 seconds. The result supports less typing, not faster input, and the tasks involved matched paper reading. PM inference: adoption still depends on importing history, handling private material, moving between tools, withdrawing preferences and starting with little history. These should be treated as product questions, not delivered enterprise features. An organizational buyer needs more than a character-count demonstration: who may turn a conversation into a shared rule, and how does that rule stop applying when an employee leaves or a business definition changes?

Alternatives and a falsifiable opportunity

For the same reading task, alternatives include a hand-written template, copying an earlier conversation, or relying on general memory. The first two require finding the right material but make the edited scope explicit. General memory reduces visible organization work but may make the current combination harder to inspect. This is a task-level comparison, not an untested ranking of commercial products. PM inference: the falsifiable opportunity is turning a person's effective method into an inspectable asset. Over two weeks of papers from different fields, compare templates with fragments on inappropriate-preference acceptance, correction time, comprehension and perceived burden. If inspecting suggestions costs more than restating intent, reduce the suggestions rather than adding more memory.

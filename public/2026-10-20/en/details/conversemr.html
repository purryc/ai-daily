Task and interaction loop

ConverseMR addresses changing interface placement during activity: a recipe can follow its wearer while a timer stays behind. On Quest 3, a double pinch activates speech. Meta Voice SDK transcribes it; GPT 5.2 receives MRUK scene context, recent conversation and current policies, then produces schema-constrained JSON. Validation and optimization translate that representation into persistent layout constraints. Direct dragging remains available. [CONVERSEMR1]

Evidence and failure boundaries

Evidence comprises a 19-person formative study and a 24-person within-subject comparison, supporting complementary speech and direct control. Ambiguous interpretations prompt clarification; unsupported requests make no change; users can undo. Higher-confidence interpretations are applied and explained. Self-reported model confidence is not independent correctness evidence. Conference metadata contains template placeholders. This is treated as a preprint, with no verified installable release or public code license. [CONVERSEMR1]

Product analysis: expose the active rule and allow takeover

PM analysis: a sensible first pilot is training or demonstration work in which reference information must repeatedly be recalled while the person moves. The product outcome should be fewer interruptions to the main task. For one small position adjustment, dragging may be cheaper than speaking, waiting and checking. Language becomes more interesting when it expresses a continuing behavior that would otherwise require several settings. A usable product should expose the affected object, the active rule and the last change. Otherwise an unexpected movement becomes hard to diagnose. Manual takeover needs an explicit meaning: does dragging temporarily offset the policy, revise its target, or cancel it? One proposed handoff is to pause optimization for that object during dragging and offer a visible choice about resuming its following behavior afterward. This is a design recommendation, not a claim about the prototype.

Adoption decision and a falsifiable trial

Before adoption, I would turn supported commands and dependencies into a testable contract: which objects can be referenced, what scene information leaves the device, who retains the last layout during a slow response, and whether undo restores both the anchor and its conditions. A fixed anchor with a gesture menu is the simplest alternative. A falsifiable trial would compare manual, speech and combined control on the same moving task, introducing similarly named panels, transcription mistakes and a task switch. Measure time from an expressed need to an acceptable layout, seconds of task obstruction, correct recovery after undo, and whether participants can explain the current policy. If combined control improves preference but increases recovery work, that would argue against expanding deployment. Repeated revisions should remain understandable without the user having to remember the entire conversation.

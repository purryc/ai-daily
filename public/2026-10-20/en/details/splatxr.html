Developer task and workflow

SplatXR serves Unity XR developers by linking Gaussian-splat appearance to proxy meshes, enabling objects such as virtual plants to collide and cast shadows. Rendering, physics, effects and blending components compose virtual assets with passthrough. For reconstruction, headset images and selected points go to an edge computer running SAM 3 and SAM 3D; the result returns to the headset. Four trials averaged 2.38 minutes. This generation step is offloaded. [SPLATXR1][SPLATXR2]

Demonstrations, limits and availability

Evidence includes demonstrations and a three-developer first-use study. Static splats do not respond to environmental relighting; large scenes produced physics instability and a freeze after adding another room. The website describes MIT-licensed open source, but its Code link returned 404 during this review. Availability therefore remains unverified, and paper-image rights must be distinguished from the website’s software-license claim. [SPLATXR1][SPLATXR2]

Product analysis: appearance and interaction need separate acceptance

PM analysis: a useful first pilot is a small collection of manipulable objects for remote teaching, an exhibition or interior planning. Accepting an asset should involve separate checks for appearance, scale, collision and task behavior. A convincing plant image does not tell us whether its base will sit stably on a table or whether a coarse collider turns delicate leaves into an implausible solid block. Authors need a way to inspect proxy geometry and its precision. The deployed experience should distinguish capture, reconstruction, preview and readiness for interaction. A wait of several minutes may fit lesson preparation while breaking an activity that requires immediate capture. I would retain controls to reselect an object, cancel reconstruction and substitute a prepared asset. Returned content should enter a preview before joining the physics simulation. These are proposed acceptance states and controls, not a list of shipping toolkit features.

Adoption cost and a falsifiable comparison

The alternatives are a conventional mesh prepared by an artist or a splat used only for viewing. The first may cost preparation time but gives explicit collision geometry; the second reduces integration work while limiting interaction. A falsifiable comparison would ask the same developers to build the same picking, placing, occlusion and shadow task through all three routes. Count initial integration and the cost of a second asset and second room. Measure capture-to-interaction time, collision mistakes, close-view frame rate, loading interruptions and manual mesh repairs. A quick first object followed by a frozen second room would challenge the platform-readiness claim. Adoption also needs an accessible repository, a reproducible version, dependency information and actual license files. The website’s open-source label cannot substitute for those deliverables. The product hypothesis is that the complete authoring workflow gets shorter while interaction quality remains acceptable, not merely that an attractive render appears sooner.

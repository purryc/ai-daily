The user: bring business definitions into analysis

MirrorShip announced MIP on October 20 as an expansion from its StarRocks-based analytics foundation into a platform connecting data, business semantics and data agents. The immediate users are financial and operational analysts, together with teams building enterprise agents. Data teams still own the definitions and access arrangements. Public material does not identify a universal purchasing role, seat price or general-availability date; the visible entry is a product-demo request, while MIP documentation is marked forthcoming. The practical task is a question such as regional net sales, where refunds, store membership and reporting periods must be agreed before a number can be trusted. MIP's stated proposition is to put those definitions into the query path instead of making each agent maintain a separate interpretation in its prompt. This is a company product claim, supported by its published architecture and demonstration, rather than an independently measured deployment result.

From a question to an inspectable answer

The official sales-and-margin demonstration makes the sequence visible: submit a business question; extract its metric, time period and region; retrieve a certified semantic view; bind the net-sales definition and produce SQL; check permissions, freshness and meaning; then return an answer with evidence. The interface illustration distinguishes semantic retrieval, asset selection, pre-execution checks and the query history. Its amounts, match percentages and execution timing are demonstration values, not performance measurements. Previously an analyst might find tables, confirm a formula, construct joins and reconcile the answer with a finance report. The proposed replacement separates reusable definitions from the choices specific to this query. MirrorData supplies access and computation, MirrorMind organizes semantics and context, MirrorPilot offers the built-in analytical entry, and MirrorControl describes governance across the process. Existing enterprise agents may also connect after integration and authorization; this does not establish that every existing agent works without adaptation.

A successful check does not prove a correct answer

The vendor's technical explanation gives a concrete failure boundary. Joining orders to line items can count an order total repeatedly; declarations of keys and relationships do not clean the underlying records. EXPLAIN INLINE exposes expanded SQL, but is not a query-audit log. PRIVATE controls visibility of semantic elements and does not replace database authorization. A correction therefore needs to distinguish a misunderstood question, a faulty definition, the wrong aggregation grain and bad source data. Rephrasing the question cannot repair every layer. The product page mentions approvals, traces, costs and feedback, but this review did not obtain an operable cancellation, business-action rollback or retry workflow. Those controls remain unverified. Editorially, a buyer should use the demonstration to ask where a rejected check appears, who can alter the chosen definition, and how the revised answer stays linked to its previous version. These are evaluation questions, not claims that the product already implements those experiences.

Alternatives and the cost of adoption

For a team with stable SQL reports, an analyst plus a shared metric dictionary remains a credible alternative. A retrieval-based knowledge system may be sufficient when the task is only to find explanatory documents. MIP addresses the harder case where definitions affect computation and several agents need to reuse them. The available evidence does not establish superiority over all conversational BI products. Migration would involve naming metric owners, reconciling dimensions, preserving historical definitions and effective dates, and checking access paths before comparing existing reports with the new interface. The vendor says existing agent entry points can be retained, which may avoid rebuilding the entire front end. Integration work, commercial terms, runtime allowances and support for the target environment still require confirmation. The adoption burden is therefore the conversion of distributed business knowledge into maintained assets. A useful procurement discussion should budget that work explicitly and ask how a definition changes after release, not assume a natural-language interface eliminates governance effort.

The product hypothesis to test

A bounded pilot could select two net-sales reports that already disagree and preserve a business-approved reference query. Add test cases involving returns, a store crossing regional boundaries and duplicate line items. The useful observation is whether the system locates the disagreement in a definition, time range or aggregation grain and allows the accountable owner to correct it. If each connected agent still needs its own formula, or an analyst cannot trace which data and semantic versions produced an answer, the central shared-semantics hypothesis weakens. Compare the steps from discovering a discrepancy to obtaining a verifiable explanation, rather than borrowing timing from the website animation. The HCI opportunity is to expose meaningful governance state to the person asking the question: which interpretation was selected, what was checked and what remains uncertain. The public demonstration illustrates that direction. Whether it reduces reconciliation work for a real team remains an unanswered adoption question, with no independent result established here.

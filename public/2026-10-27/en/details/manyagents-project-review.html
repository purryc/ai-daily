Finding the decision that stopped work

The maker describes losing track of what numerous agents were doing and which conversation was waiting for a person. ManyAgents collects those questions instead of leaving them buried behind terminal tabs. The useful reduction is in searching for a stalled request; it does not decide a product question on the developer’s behalf. Across several repositories, a decision needs its project, relevant change and likely consequences beside it. A bare approval question still sends somebody back to the original thread to reconstruct context. The public page demonstrates a question queue and recommendations, but offers no measurement of how many missed requests it prevents in a working team.

What separate worktrees actually separate

A coordinator delegates tasks to workers in separate git worktrees, while a reviewer is restricted to reading. With Codex configured, review can use another provider. Worktree separation prevents two workers from directly overwriting one checkout, but it does not settle incompatible interface decisions. A frontend and an API may each pass their own checks after choosing different field names. Read-only review limits the reviewer’s ability to change files; it does not establish the correctness of its conclusions. Developers still need the relationship between the actual diff, the tests that ran and the reviewer’s findings. A completed review status alone is insufficient evidence that the feature works as a whole.

Seeing the result beside the conversation

The embedded browser places the resulting application beside the conversation and allows a person to take control. For interface work, this offers something more inspectable than a terminal summary alone. The successful checkout shown on the site remains a vendor example, not an independent test of every checkout path. Real projects add login state, test data and external operations that cannot simply be repeated. Controlled accounts and predictable data make those checks easier to interpret. The ability to click a button does not by itself mean a developer intended a purchase or a change to a customer record in production.

[S52]

The app license and model use are separate

The website requires macOS 14 or later and Claude Code, with Codex optional. It says completed material remains readable after the trial, while starting new work requires a license. At observation it listed a one-time $49 purchase; an earlier maker comment was still asking about pricing, so purchase-time terms matter. Model use continues through the owner’s Claude Code or Codex account. The absence of another model bill from the application does not make inference free. For people comfortable with terminal panes and manually managed worktrees, the purchase case is coordination, visible previews and the shared question queue. Two unrelated tasks may not need that additional layer.

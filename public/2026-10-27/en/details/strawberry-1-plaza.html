Recognizing the assistant for the job

After several agent conversations, users often have only titles and history entries to distinguish them. Strawberry 1.0 puts companion selection in the new-tab experience through chosen faces, colors and prepared roles. The immediate interaction problem is recognition and starting a conversation, rather than improving model reasoning. For someone alternating between sales research and recruitment preparation, a consistent character could serve as a memory cue. That is an editorial interpretation, not a measured usability result. If the same face changes responsibilities frequently, it could instead carry expectations from an earlier task into the wrong conversation. The current job still needs to remain visible beside the character.

A small space for play

The plaza lets idle characters move around the composer. A person can click one to work or briefly play with it. This gives a work tool room for interaction that is not immediately productive. Compared with a static avatar, it might encourage return visits, but it also competes for attention whenever a new tab opens. For somebody opening tabs mainly to research a question, the practical issue is whether ordinary browsing and companion interaction remain easy to separate. Conversation between characters should not be mistaken for evidence that background work has happened. Likewise, an animation is not a dependable indication that an actual task succeeded.

[S32]

Execution controls have their own date

The October 21 update supplies a different part of the experience: event conditions for routines, results delivered into an existing chat, history filters and a paired WhatsApp connection with permission-dependent sending. Together these give a chosen companion an operational context: which routine belongs to it and which conversation needs an answer. Those capabilities did not first appear on October 26. Filtering can reduce the effort of finding unanswered work across conversations, although users still need to understand which conditions remain active. A person reviewing a draft also needs to know whether the next step is another preview or an actual send.

[S33]

Familiarity needs a task record

A companion may accumulate memories, skills and recurring work. Changing its name is therefore not enough when its responsibilities change; the settings attached to it need review as well. A modest repeated task would reveal whether people choose the intended companion, understand why it started and distinguish a draft from an executed action. Review and correction time matter alongside the ease of starting a chat. If somebody must reopen every source website to reconstruct what happened, the character system has not removed the handoff work. The release does not supply long-term evidence on those outcomes. Subscription terms and available credits also need checking against the actual account before adoption.

Accessible 3D editing needs details the user does not yet know to ask for

This study examines how blind and low-vision makers understand and edit 3D models through language; it is not a released modeling app.

Researchers organize components, geometry, analogies and reference frames, then deliver controlled descriptions while participants ask questions and specify changes.

Recognizing an object is insufficient: editing requires dimensions, relationships and existing details to identify what should change.

The pilot involved twelve sighted participants and the main study ten blind or low-vision participants; even idealized scripts could overwhelm and omit needed detail. This research asks persona-conditioned vision-language models to produce possible readings of a chart for its author to inspect.

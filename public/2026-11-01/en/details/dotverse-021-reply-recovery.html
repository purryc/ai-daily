Returning replies to an understandable role

Dotverse puts an existing Dot conversation into a Mac app with separate personas for activities such as writing, planning and learning. Its October 29 version 0.2.1 addresses where a response belongs after moving between those roles. Responses missing routing information now go to the primary Dot, and routing instructions accompany new messages and replies to older ones. This matters when a person needs to understand which role produced an answer. The release page gives a calendar date without a clock or timezone, so this is a seven-day supplement. The earlier Product Hunt appearance is discovery evidence, not the release timestamp.

Recovery includes ownership and state

The recovery changes are more specific than a general stability claim. Reassigning a recovered answer keeps its original conversational position and removes obsolete thinking status. Streamed text and attachments survive recovery, manual assignments are respected, and the request queue is released when the response finishes. Older recovered replies should also stop appearing pending. Editorially, the interesting control is the user’s assignment surviving an automatic repair. A completed answer should not leave a misleading busy indicator or prevent later work from advancing. These are documented fixes, not measured reliability results; no installation or interruption experiment was performed for this report.

[S24]

Several roles share the same underlying memory

The homepage says personas have their own roles, personalities and conversation views while sharing one underlying Dot and its memory. Requests are processed sequentially. In the Product Hunt discussion, the maker describes a primary Dot coordinating others and assigning individual tasks. That is useful context, but it does not establish parallel execution by independent models. A writer could organize editing and planning in separate views without assuming their conclusions are independent. Persona names are an organizational interface, not an information barrier. The app requires an Apple Silicon Mac running macOS 14 or later, an existing Dot and internet access. Some inputs open in the connected service’s window. The named roles help navigation, but the underlying service remains the same dependency for every persona.

[S25] [S27]

Local deletion does not erase service memory

The privacy notice says local conversations, drafts, personalities and pending requests are encrypted, with a key in macOS Keychain. Sign-in state uses WebKit’s per-account data store. Messages and persona context still travel to the connected Dot service, whose own data practices apply. Clearing the local transcript therefore does not delete that service’s conversations or memory. Signed updates use Sparkle, and automatic checks can be disabled. Camera or microphone access depends on the selected feature and system permissions. Dotverse explicitly describes itself as independent and neither an official ChatGPT application nor endorsed by OpenAI. Its usefulness depends on whether shared context fits the person’s tasks.

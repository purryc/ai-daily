A request can change during a reach

When a robot reaches for an object, a person may realize the requested colour, item or recipient was wrong. Ludi investigates interaction in which that new information changes the ongoing task. The earlier paper shows a real-robot sequence in which a drink request is corrected and the robot changes its selection before completing delivery. This illustrates the interaction problem; it is not a new-version reliability measurement. The October 30 demonstration of 0.2 names feedback, gestures, context and coordination as improvement areas. A correction merely queued behind the original instruction would still allow the wrong action to finish. Interpreting the new request therefore has to include the physical progress of the existing task, not just the latest spoken sentence.

A reasoning model chooses the next tool

The published 0.1 architecture separates a vision-language reasoning model from the program that runs its tools. The model chooses whether to speak, wait, navigate or manipulate. The surrounding harness gathers context, executes the selected tool, records its result and presents the updated situation again. Speech output runs asynchronously, and new speech can initiate another turn. Physical skills are supplied by specialized navigation and manipulation policies, not by treating generated prose as a motor command. These are documented foundations of the earlier system, not a complete disclosure of 0.2. The handoff between components matters to correction: a spoken acknowledgment does not, by itself, establish that the previous grasp instruction has actually stopped or been replaced.

What is remembered and where inference runs

The earlier system keeps a shared record of dialogue, visual observations, decisions, tool calls and outcomes, with a helper model compressing older material. That gives a short correction access to task history, but it also makes the retained context consequential: losing a preference or constraint can change the next action. In the paper, a Unitree G1 connects to an onsite GPU workstation. Local inference means external model services are unnecessary; it does not mean all compute fits inside the body. Camera imagery and transcribed speech are disclosed inputs, while the new announcement provides no fresh sensor specification. Viewing conditions, sound and the connection to the workstation remain practical deployment concerns that a fluent demonstration cannot settle.

The new version has no comparable published score

KRAFTON presents 0.2 as research progress and lists improvements in social cues, reasoning, memory, gestures and motion. It does not publish matching sample sizes, response times or success rates. Earlier simulation results cannot be relabeled as 0.2 results or converted into household task reliability. The intended use is nevertheless concrete: someone guiding a robot through delivery or tidying should be able to add a condition without restating the whole job. What needs to remain shared is the current goal, the step being executed and whether a correction has taken effect. Ludi 1.0 remains a research direction. The demonstration provides no public download, commercial interface or robot delivery arrangement on which a household could base an installation.

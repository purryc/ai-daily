01 · Task and evidence

When a user says not to remember a passage, they may mean not to use it for personalization, or they may mean not to retain the conversation at all. Combining those requests into one switch creates room for mistaken expectations. FENCE distinguishes three states. Vanishing excludes material from memory and removes local application chat history after a configurable period, defaulting to 24 hours. Protected retains the chat while excluding it from memory. Normal permits memory admission. The design makes eligibility for later reuse a visible object: people can establish policies before talking, inspect the resulting classification and override it, rather than waiting for the assistant to recall something sensitive before searching for a deletion control.

02 · Evidence and scope

The research collected 179 forgetting incidents from 146 people and organized them into 15 categories, followed by a within-subject prototype study with 15 participants. That evidence can illuminate control needs, usability and preferences; it cannot show that every sensitive passage will be recognized correctly. The architecture uses local Qwen through Ollama for classification, Mem 0 and Qdrant for memory, PostgreSQL for chat history, and a Gemini API for response generation. Consequently, local classification does not mean the complete conversation remains local, and deletion from local chat history does not establish deletion of every copy held by an external provider. Whether users understand those different storage and processing boundaries should itself be tested. A reassuring label is insufficient if people infer a broader guarantee than the implementation provides.

03 · Control and recovery

Compared with temporary chats, globally disabling memory or deleting individual memories afterward, FENCE offers finer admission control at the cost of understanding and correcting categories. Evaluation should include easily confused cases: a preference valid only for this task, information about another person, and a conversation the user wants to preserve for context without long-term personalization. Recovery should explain whether an action changes chat history, a stored memory or the admission policy, and allow the user to adjust future treatment of similar content.

04 · Acceptance and alternatives

The falsifiable judgment is that mistaken admissions decrease and people can accurately explain where information remains and whether it may be reused. If later responses still draw on explicitly excluded material, or if the interface leads people to assume an external provider has also deleted data, the prototype has improved perceived control rather than completing the requested memory-control job. Its value depends on both enforcement and a correct mental model of the boundary, not only on preference for the interface.

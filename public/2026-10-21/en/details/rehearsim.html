Evidence and scope

An interview guide can read smoothly without helping a researcher obtain clarity in an actual conversation. An ambiguous question may produce a short answer, while a hidden assumption may remain unnoticed until the session. Rehearsim lets researchers define a target population and source material, retain, remove or edit simulated profiles, and then rehearse interviews, flag questions and develop follow-ups. Themes link back to particular responses and questions, making it possible to inspect how an interpretation arose. Keeping profiles fixed within an iteration can also help compare the effects of changing the guide. The researcher retains responsibility for the final protocol. That makes the system a preparation aid rather than a substitute source of participants for recruitment or field research.

Evidence and scope

The study used a before-and-after design with 21 qualitative researchers and no control group. The authors observed opportunities to identify omissions, assumptions and follow-up questions, but also important failure modes. Overly cooperative simulated participants can conceal questions that would confuse a real person. Researchers may discount unexpected material because it is synthetic, or become anchored by generated themes before hearing real participants. The implementation uses React, TypeScript, FastAPI, Neo4j, Asta literature capabilities and multiple models, with checks for unsupported or inconsistent content. A traceable generation process does not establish that a profile is representative of the population. In particular, a simulated person’s statement of need cannot be converted into evidence that real target users expressed that need. The evaluation design also cannot isolate a causal improvement in subsequent real interviews.

Evidence and scope

Adoption should keep three categories separate: simulated responses, hypotheses generated during rehearsal and testimony from actual participants. A revised guide needs a human pilot to test whether it reduces leading or ambiguous questions, supports follow-up on contradictions and still leaves room for unexpected answers. Alternatives include role-playing with a colleague, conducting a manual pilot and asking a general model to portray a participant. Rehearsim’s structured source material and linked review may make preparation easier to inspect, but they do not eliminate recruitment costs.

Acceptance and alternatives

The falsifiable judgment concerns later real interviews: question quality and follow-up completeness should improve without unjustified confidence in synthetic conclusions. If the tool merely produces a longer guide and a more confident researcher while preserving the same leading questions and omissions, the additional rehearsal output has not become better research. A practical evaluation should therefore examine the real conversation and the researcher’s interpretation of it, not just satisfaction immediately after using the simulator. Commercial access and pricing were not established by the research materials.

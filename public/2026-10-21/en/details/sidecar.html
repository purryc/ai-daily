01 · Control and recovery

Waiting for a long agent task is not always empty time. A person may remember another requirement, want to inspect an implicit choice or prepare a revision for when the result arrives. Moving to an unrelated chat can lose context, while adding an instruction to the main conversation may alter work that is still running. Sidecar places that thinking in a parallel, read-only conversation that can use visible chat, execution traces, an editor, a canvas or a preview. It offers paths for examining implicit decisions and exploring alternatives while preserving a distinction between considering an idea and instructing the main agent to act. That separation is a control boundary, not merely another chat window.

02 · Evidence and scope

People can stage useful ideas and deliberately promote them into the main composer. Promotion is not automatic submission and should not be interpreted as changing the running task. The side panel can be docked, moved or minimized so users can decide when they need it. The research includes formative work with 12 people and a within-subject study with 16 participants using video and prototype conditions. Participants reported less perceived fragmentation and context switching, but also higher mental workload. That mixed outcome matters: filling waiting time does not necessarily reduce effort. A panel that keeps proposing questions or alternative directions can leave someone busy throughout the wait and facing more unresolved choices when the main task finishes. Perceived continuity and reduced cognitive demand are separate outcomes.

03 · Acceptance and alternatives

Compared with private notes, a second chat, a progress display or direct mid-run steering, Sidecar’s distinction is shared visible context combined with an explicit submission boundary. Adoption requires making clear that a side conversation has not yet become an execution request, and checking whether staged ideas are stale after the main task changes. Estimated completion times should not be treated as reliable promises. A falsifiable evaluation examines whether people remember their original intention, resume the task accurately and make fewer contradictory revisions after waiting, while also measuring mental workload.

04 · Mechanism and limits

The number of messages exchanged with the panel is not an adequate substitute. If the panel generates more ideas but slows the final decision, the adoption benefit has not been established. The short study also cannot determine sustained use after novelty fades. The authors’ institutional affiliations should not be used to describe this research prototype as an already released Google product. Its strongest supported contribution is an interaction pattern for separating preparation from execution, with a workload tradeoff that still needs to be managed.

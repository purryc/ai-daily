01 · Task and evidence

An instructor’s correction of AI feedback can contain both a local factual repair and a more enduring teaching preference. A suggestion that is inappropriate for the current code does not necessarily mean that similar suggestions should never appear again. If every edit becomes a global preference, automation can copy a small misunderstanding across more students. ValiAssist first organizes comments, chat and direct edits into smaller revision intentions, then develops conditional criteria grounded in code and feedback for instructor review and approval. The workflow moves from repairing each feedback item manually to reviewing a reusable rule and its intended scope. Reduced repetition therefore comes with a more explicit responsibility to govern how that rule generalizes.

02 · Control and recovery

Positive and negative examples, linked text spans, conflict checks and highlights of where a criterion applies make the consequences available for inspection. Their value is not proof that clicking approval means the instructor understood every implication; they provide concrete material to examine. In the study with 16 instructors, the authors report criterion-alignment figures of 91 versus 57 percent and correction of problematic criteria of 77 versus 32 percent. Those measures must not be rewritten as overall grading accuracy or improvements in student achievement. A rule can faithfully express an instructor’s intention yet still produce incorrect feedback on unfamiliar code. Assignment differences, ambiguous conditions and mistaken underlying facts can all break that second link. Evaluation needs to preserve the distinction between understanding the intended rule and applying it correctly.

03 · Acceptance and alternatives

For adoption, an instructor can prepare held-out cases that should change, should not change and sit near an ambiguous boundary, then check that the rule affects only the intended feedback. The instructor also needs to locate, disable and revise the criterion responsible for an error. Manual alternatives include rubrics, reusable comments, individual edits and a maintained global prompt. Those approaches do not automatically infer preferences, but their scope can be more straightforward to inspect.

04 · Acceptance and alternatives

The prototype uses React, FastAPI, GPT-4 o and embedding components; implementing that stack does not validate the entire teaching workflow. A short study cannot show that conflicts will not accumulate across unfamiliar assignments or a full semester. The falsifiable judgment is that repeated editing decreases without new errors and rule-review or withdrawal costs consuming the savings. If an instructor still needs to inspect every downstream application individually, generating criteria has merely moved the editing burden. The adoption case depends on controlled reuse rather than the volume of rules the system can infer.

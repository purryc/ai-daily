01 · Control and recovery

Enterprise agent control extends beyond deciding who can open a chat window. An agent may execute code in a workspace, call business systems through tools, send context to inference services and obtain credentials that remain usable elsewhere. Different teams own those paths. Controls on individual components do not automatically establish that the combined deployment has no bypass. NVIDIA’s new deliverable is the published reference architecture and its integration boundaries, rather than a first release of OpenShell or Sentry. That distinction matters because both platforms already appeared in earlier coverage.

02 · Mechanism and limits

The design separates several logical planes. A user endpoint attaches to a workspace, while execution happens within the controlled environment. Policy, credentials, inference and observation should not all be governed by the same agent. Policy authority remains outside the agent, which cannot grant itself additional permission through its own reasoning. For product teams, these boundaries explain where administrators make decisions, where users need explanations and which system owns a failure. Adopting a runtime does not automatically complete enterprise identity integration or establish access to an approved inference service.

03 · Control and recovery

The previous workflow required platform teams to connect virtual machines, network allowlists, credential brokers and logging independently, then discover integration gaps through testing. The reference design can reduce ambiguity over ownership, but every deployed path still needs validation. One explicit limitation is especially useful: revoking execution does not automatically revoke credentials issued to downstream services. Those credentials may require separate action by an identity or business system. Describing a stop button as a complete reversal of external effects would confuse execution control with transaction recovery.

04 · Control and recovery

Interaction and recovery should be evaluated through specific events. What happens to an active task when an administrator changes policy? Can a user identify the boundary responsible for a denied call? After termination, how are issued credentials, queued actions and external system state reconciled? The architecture provides separation and governance patterns rather than a universal recovery contract. Its perimeter-oriented first phase and runtime or signed-policy capabilities in the second phase should remain distinguishable. Implementers must still prevent alternate execution routes and confirm that inference dependencies enforce the intended access controls.

05 · Adoption and costs

CrowdStrike’s related announcement helps explain the ecosystem without expanding the delivered scope. It describes Falcon Guardian AI Detection and Response work with OpenShell and plans for Sentry and BlueField integration. Connecting prompt, model and tool activity with security context is a concrete direction. Hardware-backed effects, commercial availability and pricing are not sufficiently established to present the entire partnership as a shipped system. This belongs as a clearly qualified deep read within the architecture story, rather than a separate claim of a fully available product.

06 · Acceptance and alternatives

The alternatives include an enterprise’s existing combination of virtual-machine sandboxes, IAM, network proxies and security monitoring, alongside dedicated agent-governance products. Adoption is justified if the design reduces integration ambiguity and continuing maintenance effort. A falsifiable pilot should exercise unauthorized requests, policy changes, termination, downstream credential revocation and audit reconciliation, then check for usable bypass paths. If those outcomes cannot be reproduced, higher throughput figures do not demonstrate completed governance. The architecture is valuable as a testable deployment contract between teams, not as a substitute for those tests.

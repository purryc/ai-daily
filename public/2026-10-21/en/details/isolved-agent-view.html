Agent View assigns accountable owners, but third-party registration is not live monitoring

2026-10-21T12:00:00Z · main

01 · Task and evidence

The issuer’s original release has a verified publication time of 08:00 ET, or 12:00 UTC, on October 21, 2026. That places it inside this issue’s fixed main window. It is a publication timestamp, not a verified time for a physical event. The product’s novelty is placing agents inside the existing People Cloud organization chart with roles, managers, permissions, and review responsibilities. Its immediate job is to make organizational accountability discoverable. This should not be presented as proof that every agent is automatically monitored in real time.

02 · Workflow and handoffs

The earlier workflow spreads information across spreadsheets, tool settings, and informal team agreements. After staff changes, it may be unclear who maintains an agent, approves changes, or can stop it. Agent View proposes putting those relationships into a familiar organizational structure. HR can inspect business ownership, IT can record platforms and access scope, and managers can review responsibility. For third-party agents, the supplied documentation establishes an editable manual register: department, manager or supervisor, model, platform, deployment date, access scope, review frequency, and an audit-log link. Recording a link does not establish ingestion of its logs or enforcement over its actions.

03 · Adoption and costs

Native Guardian and third-party registration require separate treatment. The product page describes Guardian reviewing payroll and preparing corrections that an administrator approves. The vendor also describes accuracy, approvals, saved time, monthly cost, and alerts, but the definitions and performance of those metrics were not independently verified. Guardian is available; five other named agents are planned and should not be presented as delivered capabilities. Third-party records still require human maintenance, while inspection, audit, or suspension may need to happen in an external platform. Undisclosed pricing also prevents a complete purchasing comparison.

04 · Control and recovery

The interaction question is whether the record stays aligned with reality. When an agent changes models, gains broader access, or acquires a new supervisor, someone must update its information and perform the next review. An authoritative-looking organization chart containing stale records could create misplaced confidence. The evidence does not establish automatic discovery of all third-party changes or third-party permission enforcement. A manual register can still be useful, but only with an accepted maintenance process and a clear route from an exception to the external audit destination, accountable person, and actual mechanism for pausing or repairing the agent.

[S38] [S39] [S40]

05 · Acceptance and alternatives

The direct alternative is a maintained shared spreadsheet or asset directory with explicit review responsibilities, while existing operational and audit platforms continue to perform real checks. A useful acceptance exercise would introduce a model change, a permission change, and a failure, then ask a manager who did not configure the agent to locate its actual owner. Measure stale records, orphaned agents, overdue reviews, and time to find the responsible person. Native Guardian time savings should not be attributed to the whole registry. Faster accountability with consistently accurate records would support adoption. Continued guesswork, or treating manual entries as a live control console, would undermine the central promise.

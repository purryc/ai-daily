01 · Task and evidence

Physical interaction cannot be evaluated solely by whether a robot adopts the right pose. A high-five requires two hands to meet at an appropriate place and time; a hug additionally depends on relative body placement and sustained contact. Copying human joint trajectories onto a robot with different geometry can preserve the appearance of a motion while losing its interactive purpose. The REACT paper uses a vision-language model to annotate contact relationships in demonstrations, retargets those relationships to robot geometry, and trains a privileged teacher before distilling a student using depth and proprioception. Partner prediction helps address occlusion. The significant methodological shift is that success depends on a relationship between people and robot, not only the robot’s own motion trajectory.

02 · Mechanism and limits

The project page presents author demonstrations on a Unitree G1, including high-fives, hugs, linked arms, pats on the back and handshakes, as well as three consecutive hugs. These show target behaviors under the demonstrated conditions; they do not establish a success probability across arbitrary users, rooms or disturbances. Comparisons with GMR, OmniRetarget and external-pose conditions need their original experimental qualifications. In particular, the high-five ablation is not uniformly better on every measure. A product team selecting attractive clips could miss the more consequential question: when a partner changes timing or moves toward the edge of the field of view, does the robot recognize that its current contact target has become invalid, and can it stop, reposition or restart the interaction appropriately?

03 · Control and recovery

Adoption is limited by sensing boundaries and reproducibility. The system uses a single forward-facing depth view without active gaze recovery for a partner who moves out of sight. Its behavior repertoire is limited, and rigid hands do not provide dexterous fingers or fine contact-force control. The public repository should not be treated as a complete training release: the authors plan to release training code in early November.

04 · Acceptance and alternatives

Compared with external tracking, teleoperation, manually scripted motions and other retargeting approaches, REACT offers a research route toward less dependence on complete external pose information, not evidence that those alternatives can already be discarded. A falsifiable judgment should test recovery with partners of different heights and timing, temporary occlusion and position changes, separately measuring contact establishment, failure recognition and controlled interruption. If successful behavior requires a partner to follow a prescribed rhythm, the demonstrations cannot be generalized into dependable everyday social interaction. Physical safety would require evidence beyond these task demonstrations.

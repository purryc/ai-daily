The shop changes between attempts

A convenience-store task repeats, but its inputs change: customers move, packaging faces different directions, and regular and sugar-free drinks can look similar. AGIBOT assigned six wheeled G2 robots to customer assistance, shelf work, coffee, checkout and food service, and cleaning. It reports 1,730 completed tasks, with four robots passing final assessment and two failing some criteria. The Shanghai trial ran from 10 a.m. November 2 to 10 a.m. November 3; the recap appeared November 6. This is a recent trial account, not a new robot launch. Those organizer-reported aggregates cannot be converted into an order success rate or evidence that six employees were replaced.

Coordination needs results from the physical action

Genie Master connects planning, execution and feedback through EEP, assigning work according to robot capabilities and store conditions. Recognizing an item is different from grasping it, and holding it is different from delivering it to the right customer. Feedback must cover the whole chain. The separateG2 product page emphasizes force control, interaction and multi-user conversation, but the trial account does not expose task-level sensor logs. A specific failure therefore cannot be assigned to an invented camera or model configuration. An implementation should preserve the relationship among an order, its target item, the placement result and any human takeover so investigators can locate the failure.

A completion count leaves staff work unresolved

A store manager needs dependable work during peaks, rather than only a daily total. Moving one item and handling a conversation are different units; adding them does not establish labor savings. The public account does not provide complete attempted-task, intervention, waiting-time, consumables-preparation or downtime data, so cost per completed order remains unknown. A procurement pilot should begin with one stable task and explicit start and finish conditions, while staff record what they do on the robot’s behalf. Faster picking could still create frequent shelf preparation, resets or explanations to customers. Those activities belong in the same operational calculation as the visible automated step.

Preserve exceptions for review

A more informative comparison uses staff or existing fixed equipment for the same task in the same store period, recording wrong picks, spills, congestion and recovery time. Similar-looking drinks, blocked paths and repositioned goods can be tested consistently, including whether the robot stops, retries or hands over appropriately. Claims that experience informs later behavior also need version records to show that an adjustment reached a subsequent policy. Livestream attention should not be treated as purchase demand: novelty can attract viewers while a store still rejects the space, maintenance or training burden. The account establishes exposure to changing shop conditions; it does not establish sustained commercial economics.

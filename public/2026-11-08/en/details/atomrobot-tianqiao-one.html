Tianqiao One combines seven degrees of freedom with six touch sensors for robot integrators

Move the drive behind a tendon-driven hand

Tianqiao One places drive components behind the hand and transmits movement through flexible tendons. The announced dimensions are 221 millimeters overall and 126 millimeters for a finger. Seven degrees of freedom combine with joint coupling to support bending, fist poses and thumb opposition. Independently commanded fingers should not be confused with independent actuation of every joint. For an integrator, the first practical question is whether the mounting arrangement, cables and reachable finger poses match the arm and workpiece. A demonstrated hand gesture does not establish access around a crowded fixture. This is a component for a robot, rather than a complete household machine that can operate independently.

Use contact signals in the control loop

Five fingertip sensors and one palm sensor report contact and pressure. The vendor says the signals support slip recognition and force feedback and can be combined with vision. The operational input therefore includes both a commanded pose and evidence of what actually happened at contact. A thin or fragile object may require a different response from a rigid part slipping between fingers. No sampling rate, measurement range or standardized object evaluation was supplied in the announcement. The presence of tactile sensors consequently does not establish successful handling of arbitrary unfamiliar objects. Calibration, surface properties and the chosen control policy remain relevant parts of the integration.

Deliver the controller with the hand

The control framework accompanies the hardware, with position and compliant-force modes for demonstration replay, teleoperation and autonomous-grasp development. Tendon transmission introduces friction and creep as well as compliance; the vendor describes nonlinear compensation for those effects. Its repeatability claim lacks complete measurement conditions, so it should not be expanded into manipulation accuracy under arbitrary loads. Bundled control can spare a small team some work connecting drives, state and feedback after buying a bare hand. The amount of development effort saved has not been independently measured. A fixed operation already served reliably by a simpler gripper may not justify the additional complexity.

[S62]

Sample access leaves production suitability to test

The November 5 announcement opens sample evaluation, business discussions and customized adaptation. A universal price, full load envelope and cycle lifetime were not disclosed, and the listed target industries are not evidence of widespread production deployment. A useful evaluation would keep the actual workpieces fixed and examine grasp selection, small placement errors, excessive contact force and recalibration after maintenance. Research users also need to know how tactile and visual observations are synchronized. These are inspection questions rather than claims that the hand has already passed them. The announcement establishes an available development route; sustained operation across production shifts requires separate evidence.

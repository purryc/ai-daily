ReviewMap · Paper / research prototype

ReviewMap separates questions about evidence from judgments of contribution before people respond

Two negative comments can need different responses

A missing control experiment and a contribution judged too small can both appear in a negative review, but they ask the author to address different concerns. ReviewMap distinguishes assessment of claims or standards from calibration of contribution value and venue fit. This is not sentiment classification: praise can belong to either category too. For someone preparing a response, the distinction could help avoid answering a value judgment with unrelated experimental detail or answering an evidence gap with promotional language. The classification organizes reading. It does not decide whether a criticism is justified, and an author cannot use the category alone as proof that a paper deserves acceptance.

Retaining disagreement preserves useful information

The pipeline segments comments and combines repeated annotations from several models. Finer attributes ask about the assessment target or the stated grounds for a value judgment; unresolved fields stay unresolved. The useful design implication is to show the source sentence alongside the votes behind a suggestion. A clean label without that context would conceal the uncertainty the framework is meant to expose. Segmentation can itself remove context, so the complete review should remain reachable. Several models can also share a misinterpretation. Their agreement is evidence about consistency under a coding procedure, not a substitute for determining whether the underlying assessment or the paper’s contribution is sound.

The sample supports structural analysis, not ranking

The study examines reviews for five hundred ICLR 2026 papers and uses five human annotators on 470 development comments. Agreement is stronger for the broad act categories than for finer attributes, and the human reference is not unanimous either. Some sampled reviews contain no explicit contribution-calibration statement. That observation concerns what was written in the sampled sections, not what the reviewer privately considered. Turning it into a reviewer-quality ranking would overreach. Venues may also express novelty and significance differently. The corpus helps investigate a particular review structure; it does not establish the prevalence of these patterns throughout scholarship or explain the quality of any individual acceptance decision.

[S78]

People still choose the response

The proposed applications include reviewer self-checks, response strategy menus and a discussion view for area chairs. Their effects on people’s decisions still require study. A menu could prompt clarification, additional evidence or an acknowledgment of limits without automatically producing a defensive rebuttal. For a chair, contested statements and their grounds may be more informative than an automated overall score. A usable implementation would allow labels to be corrected and disagreements to remain visible while returning readers to the source. It should not silently turn descriptive annotations into paper rankings or sanctions against reviewers. The present contribution is an inspectable analytical framework, not demonstrated improvement in acceptance decisions.

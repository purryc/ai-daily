The review identifies remaining household work

Abby Ferguson’s October 22 SFGATE review finds stronger hard-floor mopping than carpet vacuuming, with cables and room boundaries still requiring intervention. It is a household observation with a dog and cat, not a standardized comparison across machines; the page discloses affiliate links. The practical inference is that floor mix matters more than treating cleaning as a single task. A household predominantly seeking wet cleaning may gain something different from one seeking deep carpet extraction. Keeping a handheld vacuum is also a workflow choice, not necessarily proof that every robot task has failed. No second test was performed here, and this account does not establish a population-wide failure rate.

Targeting a stain differs from scheduling a clean

ECOVACS describes FocusJet as spraying to loosen difficult stains before mopping, while AIVI changes clearance according to boundaries and objects. These address different steps: treating dirt and deciding how closely to approach it. The household benefit is a more specific task than simply sending the robot through an entire home. A small dirty area can become the unit of work, which makes sense for an entryway or kitchen spill where the surface tolerates water. This is a workflow interpretation of the vendor mechanism. A successful plan does not establish physical removal of dirt, and an obstacle-avoidance promise does not establish that every loose object has been recognized. A completion message and a clean floor remain different outcomes.

Planning depends on the environment it represents

The US specification page lists dToF navigation, RGBD obstacle avoidance and an RV1126B processor. The global page separately promotes AGENT YIKO planning around rooms and habits. Those specifications explain parts of the sensing and planning stack, but they do not remove the dependence on an adequate representation of a home. A map is both the machine’s representation and the interface through which a person specifies intent. If that representation is wrong, an apparently orderly schedule can repeatedly target the wrong area. Manually selecting rooms and times is a meaningful alternative to broader autonomous planning. The tradeoff is less delegated scheduling in exchange for a more explicit scope, without requiring the owner to understand the processor inside.

A dock changes maintenance frequency

The dock proposition includes bagless dust collection, roller washing and wastewater handling, but the official accessory kit still lists filters, brushes, a roller mop and cleaning solutions. Ownership therefore includes replenishment, space for the station and disposal of material removed from the home. The observed US promotion page lists an X12 plus Buddy Kit bundle at $899 against $1,550; that is a dated bundle observation, not a verified enduring standalone price. Regions, variants and promotions should remain separate. A traditional vacuum may still perform the principal job in a heavily carpeted home. In a home dominated by hard surfaces, automating the repeated sweep-and-mop cycle is a more coherent reason to consider this system.

Turn daily outfit decisions into reusable plans

MUSE starts with clothes already owned: add photos individually or in batches, review the identified garments and attributes, and browse clothes, shoes and accessories in a wardrobe. Its canvas lets users rearrange pieces, substitute an item and save the combination. The useful object is an outfit that can be found and worn again, not just an attractive generated picture. For repeated occasions, that saved object may reduce starting from an empty decision each morning. The initial work still includes photographing, organizing and checking the collection. No independent timing test establishes how quickly a large wardrobe can be entered or how much time a user ultimately saves.

Calendar dates and trips give outfits somewhere to go

Saved looks can be assigned to dates. For a trip, the user enters the destination, dates and activities, reviews each day’s choices and collects the selected garments into a packing list. This connects everyday outfit work to a later journey instead of starting another isolated checklist. The iPhone app requires iOS 17 or later and supports English and Simplified Chinese. App Store version history dates version 1.0 to October 20 and version 1.0.1 to October 21. The latter describes improvements to loading, calendar previews, dictation, city search and synchronization. These changes touch the planning workflow directly, but developer release notes and an insufficient ratings overview do not establish widespread user satisfaction.

Recommendations use fewer credits than generated try-ons

The US listing shows $5.99 monthly or $49.99 annually, with packs of100 credits for$0.99,400 for$2.99 and 1200 for$7.99. The published terms specify five credits for a garment cutout or trip cover,20 for an avatar or try-on, and two per recommended outfit. Trip generation charges for its outfits and cover, with the amount displayed beforehand. New accounts receive300 credits; membership resets its balance to1000 each month without rollover. Separately bought credits persist, although packs are sold only to active members. Failed or cancelled generations return credits. Repeatedly generating previews therefore has a different cost from requesting outfit ideas; a free download does not imply unlimited generation.

Cloud photos require separate request and deletion controls

This is a cloud-backed wardrobe. The privacy policy says photos and wardrobe content are stored on servers, while selected images and related text are passed to service providers for recognition and generation with permission. Chosen cities can inform weather suggestions; the policy says precise device GPS is not requested. Users can limit iOS photo access and withdraw AI permission under Photos & Privacy. That stops new requests without recalling sent requests or automatically deleting saved material. Account deletion is separate, as is cancelling an Apple subscription. For personal clothing records, those distinctions determine what is uploaded, what remains available and how a user leaves the service.

[S12]

Preview a combination without mistaking it for a fit test

MUSE can create or select a personal avatar and generate a preview of a saved look. Its terms expressly exclude guarantees about real size, fabric and fit. A sensible interpretation is to compare combinations and occasion suitability before choosing physical garments, rather than treating the image as proof of how something will fit. The distinctive feature is the chain joining inventory, editable outfits, dates and packing. Continued usefulness depends on whether corrections and newly acquired clothes remain easy enough to maintain. Public material establishes the available controls, not recommendation accuracy or retention. It is an emerging wardrobe tool to explore, not a demonstrated replacement for personal judgment about clothing.

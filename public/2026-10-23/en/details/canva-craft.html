Plan the composition before rendering it

Canva Craft is an image model developed by Canva Research and released into Dream Lab on October 22. Its two-stage architecture first plans elements, spatial relationships, scale, hierarchy and text, then renders the image. For someone making a party poster, community announcement or personal portfolio graphic, that targets a practical concern beyond photographic realism: the headline, date and subject need to belong in the composition. Editorially, planning before rendering may help treat layout as part of generation instead of adding words after a picture is finished. However, the announcement does not show a user-editable interface for the internal plan, so that architecture should not be mistaken for a new manual planning tool.

Keep designing after the first image

Craft is designed to work with Magic Layers, which turns an image into editable design elements. Canva Research describes the latter as learned decomposition combined with a font library to produce editable text. The intended benefit is more specific than putting a flat picture on a single layer. A creator might keep a background and illustration they like while changing an event date or moving the headline. That makes the first generation material for continued design rather than an accept-or-retry endpoint. The practical payoff depends on whether the separated text, boundaries and objects preserve the relationships the person needs; the architecture alone does not establish perfect decomposition of complex pictures.

A concrete route from image to selectable objects

The Magic Layers help page gives two entry points: upload an image from the home-page tool, or select an image in the editor, choose Edit and then Magic Layers. After processing, individual elements can be moved, removed, resized, given effects or replaced. Canva’s teaching guide positions this for a design that is already close to the desired result. Editorially, it suits a casual creator who wants more control than a finished image offers without learning an entirely different professional application. Selecting and moving an object provides a direct spatial action for the last round of changes, rather than requiring a fresh verbal description of the whole picture.

Output formats and access do not imply unlimited use

Craft supports up to 2K output, more than thirty preset aspect ratios plus custom ratios, and image text in fifty-five languages. Dream Lab access is global and the public API is available; the specific MCP section says coming soon, as do the faster model variants. Consumer access therefore does not depend on waiting for an agent connection. Editing has its own constraints: Magic Layers accepts single-page PNG or JPG images, may separate photorealistic material poorly, and does not support images whose longest edge exceeds five thousand pixels. The announcement does not specify Craft’s per-plan allowances or per-generation cost, so broad availability should not be described as unlimited free use.

The shutdown also breaks apart mobile search

Arc turns Spaces, pinned tabs and folders into a persistent browsing workspace; Arc Search adds mobile browsing and AI entry points. The October 22 announcement closes new account creation while existing users continue temporarily. The first mobile break arrives November 23: AI features, sign-in and sync stop on iOS and Android. A lightweight iOS browser remains. Desktop access ends December22, when Android is also withdrawn. The support page separately sets December15 as the deadline for account-data requests. For someone moving between phone and computer, the practical change is a sequence of losses across search, tabs and account access, rather than a single renamed application.

The importer preserves links but changes their organization

The documented route is to choose Arc during Dia setup and select which extensions to install. Keep Arc installed until the import finishes: Dia uses the app to locate its data. On Windows, close Arc first to avoid the documented locked-cookies-file interruption. Spaces become Profiles, pinned tabs retain names, and folders become pinned Tab Groups. Nested folders become separate groups because Dia does not nest them. Passwords, history, cookies and extensions transfer; saved payment methods and autofill do not. The editorial implication is concrete: a user who navigates by folder depth may recover every address yet still need to rebuild the way those addresses are organized.

[S61]

Other exits are narrower, especially for created objects

For a different destination, Arc’s Advanced settings export Arc Data Export.md. That is a link list organized by Space, not a bookmarks file another browser can directly import. Users must open or bookmark the links themselves. Easels require individual PNG exports through their sharing control and do not transfer into Dia. Boosts cannot be exported or migrated. This creates very different moving costs: someone keeping a few addresses can reconstruct them, while someone relying on customized pages or visual boards loses editable objects. The important distinction is between recovering destinations and preserving the work built around those destinations; the announced export covers those needs unevenly.

The discussion centers on habits and replacement tradeoffs

In the HN branches read, one participant described separating planning from implementation in different Spaces while retaining a shared Jira bookmark. Another asked about moving Arc bookmarks into Zen. A third reported returning to Chrome after an alternative disrupted Google Meet audio sharing. These are useful task-level objections: similar appearance does not guarantee equivalent organization, meeting behavior or migration. One participant expressed willingness to pay to preserve the existing experience, but that is stated intent, not a purchase. The article draws on the first discussion branches, not the entire thread. These individual reports explain replacement tradeoffs without establishing general reliability or a majority preference about AI.

[S63]

Dia keeps basic browsing free while AI remains a separate choice

Dia’s plan page separates a free browser from AI subscriptions. The free tier lists Profiles, tab groups, split view, blocking tools and sync, and explicitly excludes AI. Better Days is listed at $100 monthly. Better Answers has conflicting prices on the same page: $10 in its card and $20 in the FAQ, so an exact price should not be asserted here. Meanwhile, the October 22 Mac 1.54.0 notes add a Favorites shortcut, external-app dialog shortcuts and preloaded chat tools. For an Arc migrant, the relevant question is whether the free organization features preserve the daily workflow. Briefings and cross-tool answers remain a separate choice, not a compulsory consequence of moving.

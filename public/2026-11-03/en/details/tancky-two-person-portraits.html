Separate the people before describing the scene

Two friends living apart may want a birthday card but only have solo portraits with different lighting and backgrounds. Tancky’s new entry requires one image in Person A and another in Person B. Presets offer a starting relationship and setting, while image references attach individual instructions to the intended person. The service then creates a new shared scene. The reduced manual work concerns cutting out subjects, matching lighting and arranging a composition. It does not remove the decision about whether each person still looks right. Compare both faces with the references before choosing a result, rather than evaluating only whether the whole scene appears convincing.

Preserve the setup through sign-in

The November 2 log includes smaller interaction changes alongside the new tool: example buttons explain their purpose, and Google sign-in opens separately so prepared images and text remain in place. That addresses a familiar interruption for someone who starts composing before registering. Both portraits are required and each generated result uses three credits. A later November 3 colorization entry was also visible during collection, but its presence before the fixed cutoff was not established and it is excluded here. The inspected material describes the intended flow. No independent upload, authentication or generation session was completed, so preservation through sign-in remains a documented behavior rather than an observed test result.

[S01]

A convincing scene is still invented

The product page acknowledges that teeth, glasses and small facial marks can change, particularly with blurry or tiny faces. Contact between arms, body proportions and relative height deserve a separate inspection. A revised instruction can clarify the intended height difference without guaranteeing that only that detail changes. A mutually understood creative card fits this capability better than an image presented as evidence that a meeting occurred. A conventional collage remains a practical alternative when preserving the original faces matters more than creating a seamless scene. It also avoids inventing a new pose and environment. The choice depends on the intended artifact, not simply on which result looks more photographic.

[S02]

Include retries and retention in the choice

The pricing page offers credit packs and subscriptions, including a sixty-credit pack displayed at $4.99; the inspected section did not explicitly resolve the currency. Cost should be measured per accepted picture, including retries, rather than only the first three-credit request. The privacy policy says uploads are processed in the cloud, source images are cleared within twenty-four hours, and generated composites remain in account history. Those are vendor commitments, not an independent audit. For a family project, establish which people agree to this use before choosing the inputs. A small permitted sample is enough to evaluate likeness and correction effort without sending an entire album for comparison.

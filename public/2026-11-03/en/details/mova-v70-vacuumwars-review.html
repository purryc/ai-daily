Reach beyond the robot body

A round robot can pass beside a chair while leaving dirt on the inside of its legs. The V70 extends cleaning parts beyond its body rather than relying only on stronger suction. People select rooms and cleaning settings; navigation and obstacle recognition guide movement while the attachments approach edges. MOVA specifies a seventeen-centimeter mop extension and retractable navigation hardware for low furniture. The useful question is whether those mechanisms reduce moving chairs and wiping missed strips afterward. Reach alone cannot establish cleaning quality: contact, moisture and removal of the dirt all matter. A household trial should therefore inspect the concealed side of familiar furniture, not simply watch an arm extend.

New testing found both avoidance strength and a water-setting problem

Vacuum Wars published its purchased-unit review on November 2. The robot passed twenty-three of twenty-four obstacle tests, while water settings produced an unexpected ordering and navigation was slower than the publication's average. These are new observations, not another product launch. Avoidance can reduce interruptions without guaranteeing that the whole house finishes promptly. Likewise, an app slider is useful only when changing it produces a sufficiently predictable floor condition. Owners should check for dry sections and wet residue on their own surfaces. The review's anomaly does not identify the cause of every owner's complaint or prove that all units behave identically. Its test conditions also differ from an occupied home.

A second review makes the carpet question less settled

An October 16 TechRadar review was less impressed by carpet pickup and the extending side brush, while appreciating furniture-leg mopping and the bagless dock. The disagreement should become a practical trial question rather than an invented average score. Debris, carpet pile, settings, routes and test duration can change the outcome. Try the same amount of debris on a familiar rug and count repeat passes; then consider whether stronger settings still fit the household's noise and charging constraints. The earlier review remains dated background. A favorable later verdict does not establish that an earlier issue was fixed, especially without a matched firmware comparison or the same cleaning protocol.

[S20]

Count the household work that remains

A bagless dock avoids replacement bags but still leaves a container to empty and clean. Automatic mop washing similarly does not remove every maintenance task. The US manufacturer page listed $1,299 and displayed sold out when read; checkout and regional stock were not tested. Threshold advertising needs its footnote: nine centimeters describes a spaced two-step arrangement, not an arbitrary single obstacle or a staircase. Compare the machine with the work a household currently performs. Can it finish before the room is needed? How much spot cleaning, rescuing, emptying and maintenance remains each week? Those observations connect the mechanisms to an actual buying decision more directly than counting automated features.

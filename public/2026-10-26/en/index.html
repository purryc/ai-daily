GELLI builds Japanese practice scenes around the learner’s goals

Present Drawing lets an avatar leave ink on real paper

Present Drawing lets headset-wearing visitors watch an avatar sign a real sheet of paper and keep the physical result afterward.

The creator records body motion and tablet strokes; the performance system overlays the avatar while a concealed plotter reproduces those paths.

Magnetic coupling moves and lifts the marker. During drawing, its reported position drives the avatar timeline to reduce visible hand-marker mismatch.

Eight participants watched the same prerecorded autograph. They attributed drawing to the avatar, but perceived synchronization did not significantly exceed the neutral midpoint.

jk-Plots shows how little a small sample can pin down

jk-Plots shows small-sample analysts the observations alongside plausible distributions, so a crisp chart does not imply a stable estimate.

The prototype fits a Bayesian model to univariate data, then draws candidate distributions as ensembles, interval bands or gradients beside the actual sample.

Representations tighten as evidence increases. A wrong model still misleads: the paper’s skewed-data example is better represented by an ordinary empirical boxplot.

Evaluation uses synthetic samples of ten, one hundred and one thousand observations, plus four external experts and a coauthor’s feedback; lay comprehension remains untested.

Product perspective

An early R proof of concept exists. Analysts still choose and inspect models and priors; visual uncertainty cannot repair a misspecified model.

Confidence labels shape first impressions, until the answer is checked

This interface study compares confidence labels, percentages, bubbles and source icons beneath chatbot answers, examining how readers judge reliability when the underlying answer stays fixed.

Each of 5,151 US participants saw one mobile conversation screenshot, rated it, learned whether the answer was correct, then reassessed trust.

High confidence raised initial perceived accuracy, but indicator effects were small. After correctness was revealed, display-type differences in trust and sentiment disappeared.

Gardening, mathematics and medical examples produced different reactions. The experiment measured perceptions, not source clicks, real advice-taking or repeated use; medical examples drew greater skepticism.

Product perspective

Designers need understandable confidence meanings and inspectable evidence: a percentage alone does not establish that an answer has been verified.

Turning requirements into build files

Anthropic’s November 9 release presents Fable 5.2 as an option for open-ended engineering work. Its hardware example, developed with Diode, starts with a short description and hardware requirements. The deliverables span specifications, component choices, circuit layouts, an enclosure, firmware and a parts list. The three examples are a USB speakerphone, a home voice-assistant hub and a handheld virtual pet. Diode engineers manufactured and assembled them. This is evidence of a coordinated design workflow rather than unattended physical production. The publication exposes a date without a timezone or exact clock time, so this issue treats the item as a recent supplement with a main-window boundary uncertainty.

A local edit can change another constraint

The electronics report describes rerouting and pin reassignment when a layout becomes impractical, consultation of component datasheets, and checks that a board fits its enclosure. An installation constraint was carried into assembly instructions. Those are concrete handoffs between electrical, mechanical and firmware work. For a design team, the useful retained output would be versioned files, component references and assembly constraints rather than only a polished rendering. The devices remain early prototypes: the report does not establish long-term durability or commercial certification. A working specimen therefore cannot stand in for a production qualification process or a measured success rate across unrelated board designs.

[S02]

Model selection and speed have separate costs

The model page lists standard Fable 5.2 pricing at USD 8 per million input tokens and USD 40 per million output tokens; fast mode is listed at USD 16 and USD 80. These token prices do not determine the cost of a complete engineering job. Fable and Mythos share an underlying model, but access and safeguards differ, so a standard account should not be assumed to include Mythos. The model overview recommends Opus 5.5 for ordinary work before escalating demanding tasks. A practical migration would identify which decisions need the larger model and preserve budgets and interruption points rather than switching every request indiscriminately.

Inspect the handoff, not only a benchmark

The overview documents text and image inputs, text output and tool use. Those interfaces determine what engineering material can be supplied; they do not imply a direct connection to arbitrary instruments. Vendor benchmarks and customer quotations describe particular tasks and configurations. For an adopting team, a useful comparison would take an existing low-risk design and check whether a component substitution leaves its schematic, routing, enclosure and parts list consistent. Engineers should be able to trace each edit. We have not performed that comparison. If a digitally accepted design still demands extensive physical rework, the proposed reduction in design handoffs would need to be narrowed, and unresolved constraints should travel with the files.

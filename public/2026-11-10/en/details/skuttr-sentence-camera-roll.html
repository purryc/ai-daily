Describe the collection you want to share

skuttr begins with a sentence about a trip, place or recurring date. It finds candidate photographs and videos in an iPhone library and assembles a vertical Reel. Product Hunt’s November 9 newsletter documents that recent appearance; it does not establish the first App Store release date. The official site describes a silent, 15–30-second output, with music added later in Instagram or TikTok. The removed task is browsing and arranging individual clips. A person still decides whether the result represents the experience they intended to share. Attractive framing, a coherent story and permission to show another person are separate judgments that a generated sequence cannot settle by itself.

No manual selection also limits correction

One visible Product Hunt question asks whether a selected shot can be replaced before export. The maker answers that version one keeps the generated sequence fixed and suggests a more specific sentence or trimming the exported video elsewhere. That is a clear interaction tradeoff: fewer controls shorten the path to a first result, but one unsuitable shot can require another generation. The maker also says the app reports when it finds no good match rather than assembling a weak result. We did not test that behavior. The practical question is whether a revised sentence produces an understandable change in selected footage, rather than leaving the user to guess what the system considered relevant.

Originals stay local while planning previews leave

The readable indexed body of the official privacy page distinguishes local candidate selection and rendering from remote planning. Place names can be looked up through Apple’s map service. With permission, the sentence and low-detail candidate previews go to OpenAI, potentially with capture dates and video durations. Originals remain on the device, but previews can still contain people. Account-linked usage and request-status records are another data category. “Originals stay local” therefore cannot be shortened to “fully offline.” The direct privacy-page open was unavailable, so this account is limited to the official text actually retrieved through search, and the data-flow statements remain vendor claims rather than traffic we independently inspected.

Judge a finished result before choosing a plan

The official site offers five completed free Reels per iPhone, with errors, cancellations and no-match results excluded from that allowance. Its stated US Pro prices are $4.99 monthly or $29.99 annually, with up to 20 Reels per day; iOS 17 or later is required, and Android and iPad are not yet offered. This workflow fits someone who otherwise leaves footage unshared more naturally than someone demanding detailed shot control and finished sound design. A small set of nonsensitive footage could reveal whether theme matching and opening-shot choices are useful before subscribing. Product Hunt points would not answer that question or establish sustained use, and the final store price still needs checking.

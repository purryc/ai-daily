Stopping does not rely on the job checking itself

Control Room gives a research workload a job_id, which travels with its requests to an inference engine. The engine asks the central service whether that job may continue receiving model output. A stop instruction makes subsequent requests for the same identifier fail, even if the research process remains alive. OpenAI states a maximum 35-second delay caused by authorization caching; that is not the total time from detecting suspicious behavior to response. Enforcement sits in the relatively centralized inference path rather than depending on every research script to implement its own stop check. The disclosure concerns internal infrastructure, not a new customer API or purchasable service.

Detection and enforcement are separate stages

Control Room receives a stopping decision; it does not replace the system that decides something is wrong. The October 29 background describes screening, model review, investigative agents and further investigation using system logs. Serious cases can reach people, while some critical alerts can pause activity automatically. That account also acknowledges that false alarms constrain around-the-clock paging coverage. The existence of enforcement therefore does not show that every incident will be detected. A user-facing operations view should preserve the evidence, affected job and response state. Reading an alert, deciding to intervene and actually preventing new output are different observable outcomes, and should not be collapsed into one reassuring status badge.

A stop control needs accountable operating procedures

The September 28 safety-case document is supporting history, not a list of newly shipped features. It discusses structured risk arguments, independent dissent, accountable approvals, escalation and pausing procedures. It also recommends immutable traces and the ability to undo downstream consequences. Control Room supplies an enforcement mechanism, but responsibilities still have to be defined: who can pause work, how the result is explained and what evidence permits resumption. A generated summary cannot answer those organizational questions by itself. These documents are not a general compliance certification. Their focus on frontier training should also not be casually transferred to every ordinary consumer conversation without identifying the task boundary and actual authority.

[S82]

Failing closed has an availability cost

The disclosure says inference fails closed if the control service becomes unavailable. That behavior is already enabled for frontier research workloads, while rollout to remaining research workloads is still continuing. We have neither accessed the internal operations interface nor measured the delay. Our design interpretation is that an operations view should distinguish stopped inference, a live process, pending investigation and authorized resumption, while preserving prior outputs for review. Pausing also cannot be assumed to recall external actions that already occurred. Availability and control must be evaluated for a specific workload. For an outside team, this is an architectural example to examine, not a directly installable product or a transferable performance guarantee.

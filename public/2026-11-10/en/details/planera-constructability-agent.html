A computable schedule can still be unbuildable

A schedule may calculate successfully while placing finishes before rough-ins or a concrete pour before its inspection. Planera’s November update introduces a Constructability Agent for this kind of sequencing problem. A planner starts it from the Schedule Quality Report side panel and can supply organizational standards. Manny returns an overview and separates hard violations from softer concerns associated with activities. The input is a schedule and supplied guidance, rather than a sensor-verified account of site conditions. That distinction matters: a flagged dependency gives a planner something specific to investigate, but an unflagged schedule is not evidence that a site is ready or that construction has been approved.

How a risk reaches a planner’s working schedule

The monthly article also revisits a Procore RFI scanner, which had its own October 21 announcement and should not be described as a November 9 debut. That workflow connects outstanding RFIs with critical-path and near-term work, then adds references, ownership and dates to activity notes. Uncertain matches remain visible for review. Annotation does not itself revise scheduling logic or dates. This older integration is useful context for the new review agent: the product attempts to put an issue beside the work it affects, rather than leave it in a separate narrative. A planner should still inspect the actual correspondence between an RFI and an activity before accepting the suggested consequence.

External reading and internal editing are different paths

Planera’s documented MCP connector offers a separate route for asking about critical paths, comparing versions and exploring scenarios from supported AI clients. Its scheduling engine accounts for project calendars. The connector is explicitly read-only: it cannot save a new version, change activities or update progress, and a scenario is not automatically committed. In-product Manny has a different editing and approval path. Access follows the signed-in user’s project scope, with organizational setup potentially requiring an administrator. Budgets, contracts and rates supplied by other connected systems should not be mistaken for information stored and validated by Planera itself. These boundaries identify what a conversational answer can actually change.

Validate a dependency before widening use

Availability is a practical constraint. The Constructability Agent is being rolled out gradually, while Summary Schedule remains a forthcoming feature. The pricing page uses custom quotes rather than a public standardized amount. A modest adoption check could use a schedule with dependencies already reviewed by an engineer, then examine whether the agent identifies the correct activities and distinguishes a serious conflict from an acceptable construction variant. After a proposed correction, the planner should check for a new conflict elsewhere. This is an evaluation suggestion, not a test performed here. No independent measurement establishes time saved, false-positive rates or coverage across trades; an authentic interface image cannot answer those performance questions.

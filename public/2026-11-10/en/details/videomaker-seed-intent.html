VideoMaker · Paper / interaction research prototype

VideoMaker carries confirmed creative intent across stages in an editable Seed Panel

Keep confirmed decisions available

VideoMaker organizes video creation into six stages and keeps creator-confirmed intent in an editable Seed Panel. Its shared agent carries that state forward as the idea becomes a script, shots and a finished sequence. The useful distinction is between a commitment and an exploratory suggestion. A character’s motivation might be fixed while the setting remains open. Our interpretation is that this boundary should be visible at the moment an idea is accepted, not reconstructed from a long conversation later. A creator should also be able to see whether a seed is global or relevant to one shot. Otherwise persistent memory can preserve the wrong assumption just as efficiently as the intended one.

Translate a mood without silently changing it

A script library and cinematic knowledge base support the agent’s translation from an informal idea into narrative and audiovisual choices. The creator can confirm, add, remove or revise intent rather than repeatedly rewriting one increasingly long prompt. Our product concern is semantic intensity: making a description more specific can also make it more extreme. A slightly uneasy scene should not silently become a scene of terror. A practical interface should retain the original phrase beside the elaboration and make the changed emphasis easy to reject. Cinematic terminology is useful as an editable proposal; its professional appearance is not evidence that the creator’s intended mood has survived.

The comparison retains an unresolved boundary

The authors interviewed twelve creators and then ran a within-subject study with thirty participants. The comparison used a purpose-built baseline combining chat, image and video generation. Reported intent preservation improved, but negotiating the agent’s initiative remained difficult: excluded ideas could return in later shots. This should not be presented as a commercial solution to cross-shot consistency. The baseline also does not represent every professional editing toolchain. For evaluation, the relevant question is narrower: when both interfaces use similar generation capabilities, does explicit intent state help a creator preserve a particular requirement? A short study can inform that question without establishing durable advantage across different kinds of production.

Make the scope of revision legible

Our proposed test would remove one character attribute and trace the effect through the script, storyboard and generated clips. A second edit would apply to only one shot, checking whether unrelated scenes remain intact. The evaluation should include returning to an earlier choice and generating again, because a correct-looking final clip can hide an inconsistent project state. These are proposed adoption checks, not tests performed by this publication. Consumer pricing and regional availability were not established. The contribution is a concrete workflow to investigate. It still requires evidence that ordinary creators can manage the persistent state without spending more time repairing propagation than they save by avoiding repeated prompts.

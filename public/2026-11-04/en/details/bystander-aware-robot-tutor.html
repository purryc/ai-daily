Bystander-aware robot tutor · Paper · research prototype

A robot checks who is nearby before moving feedback from speech to a turned display

Change delivery when the audience changes

The tutor combines a rotating body with a learner-facing display. After detecting a bystander, it turns away and presents feedback silently, using speech only to direct attention. The interaction contribution is a change of channel when the audience changes. An observable adjustment can help the learner understand what the device is doing. However, the learner should still be able to choose: some feedback is suitable for shared discussion, while other feedback belongs in a private exchange. The presence of another person is useful context, but it should not automatically settle every disclosure decision or override a person’s expressed preference.

Feeling considered is not measured confidentiality

Each condition had twenty participants, and the adaptive group rated the robot more favorably on privacy awareness and social competence. Actual bystander access was not measured, and detection logs were not retained. We therefore separate perceived consideration from measured exposure. Rotation might communicate care while failing because of reflections, changing positions or multiple observers. A product trial should vary distance and sightlines, measure what remains readable and ask the learner what protection they believe is active. The mismatch matters: a considerate-looking gesture could encourage someone to share more than the physical arrangement protects. That possibility requires testing rather than an assumption in either direction.

Evaluate content separately from delivery

Participants received the same scripted criticism regardless of their actual pronunciation or handwriting. That holds content constant but does not evaluate teaching effectiveness. Evidence of reduced embarrassment was inconclusive. Courteous delivery cannot repair an inappropriate message. A tutoring product must also establish whether feedback is accurate, specific and actionable. A follow-up could present the same constructive advice through different channels and examine comprehension, uptake and continuation of the learning task. Those outcomes should remain separate from ratings of the robot’s personality. A learner may appreciate discretion while still finding the content unhelpful, or learn something useful while disliking how it was delivered.

[S121]

Let the learner take over when detection is uncertain

The study used a fixed bystander position, a university sample and nonrandom assignment. Moving groups and prolonged teaching remain outside the evidence. We would make uncertain detection an explicit state and offer a choice to pause speech, move to a personal device or view the message later. Testing should also examine whether scanning attracts attention and whether the learner must turn awkwardly to read the display. These are proposed checks for a future implementation. The current work is a research prototype, not a validated commercial tutoring feature. Its real setup photograph documents the apparatus and arrangement; it cannot establish production reliability or a general privacy guarantee.

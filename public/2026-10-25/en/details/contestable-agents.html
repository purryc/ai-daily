The same abbreviation can name different positions

Building equipment often uses vendor-specific abbreviations. A field named DISCH does not by itself establish whether the sensor measures air leaving a mixing box or air after a coil. An incorrect class can make a downstream fault rule interpret normal mixing as equipment failure. The Contestable Agents reference case supplies a point list and manuals to separate agents: one examines names, another reads documentation, and each gives supporting or challenging arguments for Brick classes. The steward can inspect the inference behind a label rather than simply approve the label. Inputs remain existing records and documents; the system introduces no new sensor or physical measurement.

Editing the argument behind a label

The reviewer can reject an unsupported challenge, revise its text or add a manual passage, after which the scoring engine recomputes candidate strengths. A finalizer explains the outcome but does not independently choose another class. Stored arguments permit later recomputation without calling the model again. In the synthetic example, the steward rejects an abbreviation-only inference and adds evidence locating the sensor in the mixed-air section. That makes the correction easier to explain than a ticket containing only the final label. Multiple agents do not create independent facts, however. Two arguments drawn from one claim record can still reinforce the same underlying evidence twice.

[S99]

A display score can exaggerate certainty

The paper notes that nearly tied raw margins can appear as 1.00 and 0.99 after normalization, inviting a misleading impression of high confidence. The model writing an argument also assigns its base score; illustrative parameters and warranty-example scores are not calibrated against correctness. There is an additional ontology issue: the implementation scores supply-air and discharge-air labels separately even though they are equivalent in the cited Brick version. Deployment would need canonical classes and source-applicability checks before deciding how to display scores. Otherwise a detailed argument graph may organize duplicate labels and irrelevant evidence persuasively without making the classification more dependable.

[S99]

What is implemented and what is proposed

The reference implementation uses CrewAI, GPT-4o, Voyage embeddings and MongoDB, executing the workflow on one point in a synthetic site. The site contains 247 points, eighteen flagged as ambiguous; that is not a completed accuracy evaluation over all points. The paper proposes short evidence review for clear cases, full argumentation for ambiguous ones and promotion of verified decisions into precedent. The implementation lacks triage and verified-result writeback, and a second edit overwrites earlier argument content. Append-only history and the cross-system warranty record are also proposals. Equipment teams can borrow argument-level review while still implementing storage, deduplication and version handling. The example does not establish fewer real-world diagnostic errors. Reviewer effort and subsequent reversals have not been measured.

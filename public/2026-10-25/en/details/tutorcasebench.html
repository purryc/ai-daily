A correct answer can still be the wrong response

When a learner says their work is poor and refuses to present it, encouragement may miss the problem. In one paper example, the documented response is first to establish that the work is correct. Models instead reassure the learner that mistakes are acceptable, leaving the mistaken belief intact. TutorCaseBench adds such interactions to tutoring evaluation. A model receives minimal context and six tutor–student turns, then responds after the final difficult behavior. Other items ask for a support plan over several weeks. The intervention guidance is hidden from the responding model, and evaluation checks case-specific evidence rather than merely a friendly tone or a correct academic answer.

How documented cases become synthetic dialogue

The construction pipeline first screens ERIC papers for sufficiently concrete accounts of behavior, classroom context and teacher actions. It extracts learner evidence separately from instructional evidence, then builds scenarios, profiles, effective or ineffective strategies, dialogues and checklists. Three hundred papers yield 518 student categories and 923 English dialogues spanning resistance, attention, academic, emotional and interpersonal difficulties. This gives developers test material without inventing every difficult situation from scratch. Synthesis can nevertheless combine events from different moments or add unsupported detail. Keeping the source case connected to the generated test matters when somebody disputes what an appropriate response should have been.

Recognizing a strategy is easier than applying it

There are 1,560 open-ended items and 2,537 four-option questions. The main paper counts a response as successful when it meets at least one case criterion, because several valid approaches need not be combined into one reply. The highest open-ended result is 55.8%, while the strongest multiple-choice result is 63%; these use different tasks and denominators. Immediate responses generally outperform longer-term plans. Reported failures include misplaced reassurance, generic scaffolding, excessive intervention and returning the student to a task that should itself change. For a tutoring product, a menu that recommends a strategy and a feature that writes the entire response require separate checks. Recognition does not establish context-sensitive expression.

Who still needs to check the benchmark

Twelve university students experienced in reading research checked a sample of thirty papers, with two reviewers per paper. The validation interface requires a source summary before ratings and written rationales for pipeline outputs; its screenshot shows this research tool, not a student-facing tutor. Exact agreement across three-point judgments was 58.2%, with many outputs judged only partly supported. Construction and grading primarily use Gemini models, although the authors also test alternative models and judges. Cases concentrate in North America and Europe, and text cannot capture all nonverbal behavior. Teaching teams can use the collection to locate inappropriate response patterns, while checking whether cases and interventions apply to their own learners, language and curriculum.

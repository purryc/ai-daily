Scheduling work and executing motion

A factory cannot make every reach wait for a remote server after assigning a box-moving job. In its October 23 engineering Q&A, Agility distinguishes Arc workflow orchestration from onboard execution and safety. Losing a connection should therefore not remove the local limits governing the current motion. That does not establish every detail of receiving new jobs, synchronizing state or recovering after an outage. For a supervisor, the separation is useful because a delayed assignment and a robot unable to move safely are different operational conditions. A single online indicator would hide that distinction and make diagnosis harder than it needs to be.

A separate check on the moving body

The Q&A describes an isolated safety path using constraints that can be checked, such as a permitted arm-height limit that triggers a stop when crossed. The plan integrates computing previously housed in an external box into an IGX Thor and Halos arrangement while retaining separation. The October 22 release further specifies Thor modules for the general-availability machine and an expanded proprietary human-detection system. Buyers need to understand which checks run in that isolated part, what inputs cause a stop and who restores operation afterward. Consolidating processors is a packaging decision; the operational meaning of each limit still needs to be specified.

Handling work includes service and integration

The product page describes interchangeable grippers and fleet software intended to connect with compatible warehouse, manufacturing and mobile-robot systems. Those interfaces can let several workflows share a machine, although a different gripper changes the grasp, load and collision envelope. The engineering discussion also describes separately replaceable compute near the upper backpack and a battery that requires maintenance. These details connect directly to daily ownership: which failure can be repaired by changing a module, which change needs recalibration, and what spares a site should hold. A selected box-moving video cannot answer those questions. A complete service quotation and deliverable configuration list remain unavailable in the material reviewed.

The planned machine has its own delivery boundary

Early access is expected in the first half of 2027, with availability increasing over the next six to nine months. Participation in NVIDIA’s inspection lab precedes final third-party certification. Experience from the existing Digit fleet helps explain the design, but it is not operating history for this unreleased generation. This update also differs from the AGCO pilot covered in the October 21 issue: one describes compute and control architecture, while the other identifies a planned industrial task. A prospective buyer can use those separate facts to price site changes, networking, service and acceptance. Combining a future pilot with a chip partnership would not establish a mature factory deployment. These remain separate decisions.

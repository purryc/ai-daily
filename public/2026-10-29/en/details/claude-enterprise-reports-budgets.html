Finding friction behind the usage total

An overall token or spending chart says little about why people repeatedly try the same task. Smart reports lets an administrator choose a team, period and product, then organize sampled sessions by work, outputs, cost and recurring obstacles. Missing connectors, failed tools and unsuitable outputs can become concrete improvements rather than a blanket request to use fewer tokens. Reports also suggest recurring patterns worth packaging as skills or repository rules. These classifications are model interpretations of a sample, not a complete account of all work. An expensive session does not, by itself, show that its owner worked inefficiently.

Ask users about results the transcript cannot show

In-app surveys appear after a reply or between tasks. Administrators choose the audience, schedule and questions; a member can dismiss the card to decline. This adds information a transcript may not contain, such as whether a draft was eventually used by a customer or which step would slow down without the tool. Responses can be exported, but the documented CSV contains email, group and submission time, so this is not an anonymous survey. Estimates of time saved are self-reports rather than causal measurements. Decisions about wider adoption should connect the reported experience with the actual work produced.

A common pool with individual limits

The pooled budget addresses a mismatch in which one person exhausts an allowance while another hardly uses it. Each request counts against the individual monthly limit and an available pool. Usage is constrained when the individual limit or all accessible pools are exhausted. Members in several groups also have a pool priority, so budgets cannot simply be added together. On Standard and Premium seat plans, the pool applies to usage credits rather than seat-included usage. A member can request more when blocked, leaving an administrator to review the allocation instead of making account-switching the informal recovery process.

Configuration changes what administrators can see

All three features were announced generally available on October 28, but eligibility differs. Smart reports is off by default and excludes CMEK, HIPAA and hybrid inference configurations; surveys support CMEK but not HIPAA configurations. Attributing reports to named individuals requires another setting, with identity reveals logged. Survey data has its own handling rules. These distinctions should be explained to the team instead of presented as one enterprise-analytics switch. Anthropic explicitly excludes individual performance evaluation and employment decisions as intended uses. The more appropriate operational question is which connector, permission or budget is preventing a group from completing its work. Average cost per session is also not the price of a completed job. One job may span several sessions or finish in another tool, so the report’s unit of measurement needs to remain visible when budgets are discussed.

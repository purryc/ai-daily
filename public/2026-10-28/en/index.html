EMG gestures reduce personal calibration, but still depend on segmentation

The research reads forearm activity through an eight-channel EMG armband, classifying hand and wrist gestures as discrete inputs without collecting training examples from each new user.

Energy thresholds first isolate a gesture from the continuous stream; a temporal network then classifies it. This supports event-like commands rather than unrestricted finger tracking.

The best configuration reached 96.1% accuracy on fourteen-person continuous data; the reported 700 ms prompt-to-prediction delay includes performing the gesture.

Both datasets used the same armband and similar placement; this does not establish arbitrary-sensor or all-day reliability.

Product perspective

It informs calibration-free input research, not a newly shipped wristband. The paper’s training-split description remains internally inconsistent. No commercial deployment was independently established.

HILMARTS aligns a model by moving the tablet, then shows missing scan coverage

HILMARTS is a surgical-navigation research prototype. Holding a grab control couples the virtual model to iPad motion, followed by scanning the body surface.

LiDAR and RGB inputs are filtered with body segmentation; a local server computes geometric registration and returns the alignment to the tablet’s AR view.

A surface-coverage score indicates incomplete capture and gates registration, helping operators judge whether more scanning is required before proceeding.

Sixteen people tested a physical phantom, with mean error around 3.3 mm. Only one participant was a clinician; these are not patient outcomes.

Product perspective

The score’s independent accuracy benefit was not significant. Clinical trials and tissue deformation remain unresolved; this is not an approved medical product.

CogArena separates finishing, accuracy and humanlike behavior

CogArena puts multimodal agents through ten browser experiments involving memory, risk and other behavior. Inputs are rendered stimuli; outputs are clicks, keypresses and trial records.

Scoring separates completion, accuracy and human behavioral signatures instead of trusting an agent’s success claim or calling every measure a completion rate.

Four economy-tier models ran ten sessions per task: only 167 of 400 sessions passed every completion check in the reported experiment.

Per-trial deadlines were disabled. Human baselines come from earlier studies; no people completed this version for a direct comparison.

Product perspective

It helps examine agents together with their interfaces, without proving human equivalence or supplying a general bot detector for online research. The study does not establish long-term effects in maintained production repositories.

Repeated refactoring requests can keep an agent editing after completion

This study repeatedly asks coding agents to refactor, observing when file changes stop and whether a claim of completion actually matches the diff.

Version snapshots, line diffs and code-structure trees expose rewriting, additions later removed and repeated edits, producing six behavioral signals across successive turns.

Across sixty Java codebases, five models and 3,000 turns, only 454 turns made no edits. Roughly 31 times describes cumulative churn, not final size.

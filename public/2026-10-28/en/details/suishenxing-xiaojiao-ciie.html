Sending the question to the right service

A first-time visitor may know the destination without knowing which menu contains parking, a shuttle or the entrance on the other side of the exhibition grounds. Xiaojiao puts a question box inside the existing Suishenxing app and WeChat mini program. The useful output is a service card with somewhere concrete to go next. This interaction can help someone express a journey before learning the service’s categories. It should not be read as evidence that an assistant completes every form behind those cards. The visible handoff matters: a person can see which service was selected and decide whether it actually matches the request before continuing.

Parking still has a form and a commitment

The parking example keeps the important choices in the reservation interface: area, specific car park, date, time and vehicle plate. A later query retrieves the record. That division gives the conversational layer a narrow job, finding the relevant operation, while the form remains responsible for an unambiguous booking. For a visitor, the distinction is practical. A helpful answer is not the same thing as a reserved space. The record should be checked for the actual plate and arrival period, especially when several people are coordinating a car. Good wording around the card needs to distinguish a suggested action from a confirmed reservation without forcing users to guess which state they have reached.

[S26]

The digital journey still includes paper

The official guide states that a reservation cannot be cancelled after its permit is obtained and that the permit must be printed on both sides and displayed at the front of the vehicle. This is a consequential step for someone who expected an entirely digital trip. It deserves to be visible before commitment, particularly for visitors changing cars or coordinating a late arrival. The subsequent journey has another useful split: driving to the reserved car park, then finding a pedestrian entrance. A walking video can make the second part more tangible than a pin on a map. It cannot, by itself, establish that every entrance remains available when a visitor arrives.

[S26]

The service connection matters more than a long answer

The wider zone also brings together rail, hotel buses, transfers and surrounding transport points, with Chinese and English interfaces. For an unfamiliar visitor, consistent names across a card, the booking page and the actual stop are more valuable than a longer conversational answer. A useful card should make clear whether it opens a reservation, a lookup or navigation. Service notices and on-site directions still matter when traffic arrangements change. Public material does not identify the underlying model, positioning technology, latency or completion rate, and does not establish automatic payment.

Souffleur · Paper / research prototype

Souffleur turns voice chats into topic cards, without a demonstrated short-chat advantage

2026-11-05T18:36:18Z · First arXiv submission; public announcement time unconfirmed. · Read 11-06 09:08Z

Keep the current question visible

A voice assistant can finish speaking while leaving the person with a long transcript and little indication of what remains unresolved. Souffleur keeps a topic and its current decision visible, folding older subjects into compact forms. The design gives a returning user a place to look before searching several turns. Its practical test is whether a label identifies the unfinished question. “Travel” may provide less help than a specific unresolved booking, but adding specificity can also introduce a false commitment. An evaluation should therefore compare the displayed state with what the speakers actually agreed, including uncertainty, rejected options and unfinished tasks.

Make compressed conversation inspectable

The interface retains current recognition text and distinguishes human and assistant contributions. When an earlier subject returns, its former slot remains apparent rather than making every item move. This may reduce visual search, but does not eliminate the need to inspect wording. A pilot should include a misrecognized name and an assistant suggestion that the person never accepted. Review whether users can discover each error from the compact view and recover the relevant exchange. Stable positions and colored provenance are useful cues only if the underlying attribution is correct. They should not make an uncertain interpretation appear more authoritative than the original conversation.

[S26] [S25]

A short study did not establish superiority

The authors recruited 48 participants; analyzed groups contained 22 and 24 people for the reported measures. The comparison did not detect a reliable benefit, which is not evidence of equivalence. Conversations and interruptions were short, and participants had no concurrent activity. A product team should consequently avoid translating these results into safer driving, better long-term memory or easier work while walking. Those are different use conditions. A useful follow-up would vary interruption length while keeping the conversational task comparable, and independently check whether the person resumes the correct unfinished decision rather than merely resumes speaking quickly.

Evaluate what happens after returning

Pinned notes, a manually maintained task list and an ordinary transcript are reasonable alternatives. For a longer trial, prepare conversations with several open decisions and revisit them hours later. Count incorrect resumptions, omitted qualifications, correction time and requests to reopen the full exchange alongside speed. This distinguishes a genuinely useful reminder from a reassuring but incomplete summary. We did not run the prototype, and commercial access and pricing were not established. If the compact display accelerates navigation while causing more mistaken commitments, the appropriate response is to expose the missing context rather than further compress the text.

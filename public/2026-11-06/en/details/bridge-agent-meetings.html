The input is more than a transcript

Bridge previously offered preparation and notes as Simplora. This launch emphasizes its own video conferencing and customizable agents that participate in meetings. Users give an agent a name, task, permissions and triggers. It can see participants and screen shares, hear discussion, respond in the meeting or work privately. Inputs therefore include live material and instructions, while outputs may be answers, research or actions in connected tools. A practical use is checking a customer’s question during the call instead of copying it into another assistant afterward. These are documented capabilities; no independent trial here establishes that agents consistently choose the right moment to speak or act.

Keep the same material across the meeting

The official site combines scheduling, preparation, meetings, notes and shared memory, alongside transcript imports, capture from other meeting platforms and phone recording for in-person discussions. Reusing material can reduce repeated explanation of a customer’s background. It can also carry an error or outdated conclusion into later work. A trial should preserve a correction made during a discussion and examine whether the final notes and subsequent agents still use the old statement, with references leading back to the conversation. Existing recording tools and manually maintained task lists remain sensible comparisons. Putting features in one workspace does not by itself establish a time saving.

Triggers need an understandable meaning

Agents can start on a schedule, keyword or meeting type, or receive a direct handoff. Existing connected-tool permissions apply, but permission to use a calendar does not mean a conversational suggestion authorizes an invitation. Saying a demo might happen next week differs from explicitly asking to book it. An acceptance exercise should establish whether the host can identify participating and background agents and understand intended actions. These are proposed checks, not a claim that every control has been verified. Begin with read-only questions, then test how mistaken triggers are paused, explained and corrected. Undo should not be assumed without testing the relevant action.

Separate free conferencing from agent access

The displayed Starter plan allows five participants and sixty-minute meetings with notes. Professional is listed at $20 per user monthly and includes custom agents, workflows and longer meetings; Business is $30 with larger meetings and additional administration. The page also offers a yearly switch, so these are observed monthly figures, not inferred discounts or taxes. Teams must clarify expectations around recording, sharing and external tools; bot-free capture does not remove that need. Compare the manual organization, checking and unfinished work remaining after similar meetings, while also counting interruptions and mistaken actions. That examines the promised workflow rather than just the quality of generated notes.

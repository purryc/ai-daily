Tasteply · Paper / research prototype

Tasteply makes feed preferences blendable without treating every browsing mood as permanent taste

2026-11-05T11:30:27Z · First arXiv submission; public announcement time unconfirmed. · Read 11-06 09:08Z

One person can have several browsing contexts

Looking for design references during work and browsing jokes on a weekend need not imply the same recommendation goal. Tasteply starts with liked material and lets people reorganize proposed preference groups around their motivations. A useful evaluation would ask users what belongs in a group and what should stay outside it. Topic similarity alone may be insufficient: two posts about the same subject can serve different purposes. The product’s promise depends on whether the resulting controls correspond to distinctions people actually want to make, not merely whether a clustering model can produce plausible names for their reading history.

Movement changes the current mixture

Moving among attractors changes the mixture used for recommendations, with a separate weight for persistent overall taste. This offers a more direct instruction than repeatedly rejecting individual posts. The interaction nevertheless requires a readable relationship between position and output. A comparison task could ask someone to move from professional examples toward casual inspiration and then return to the previous mix. Large unpredictable changes would encourage random searching; negligible changes would make the map feel decorative. Useful feedback should expose enough of the resulting difference for people to make a deliberate choice without requiring them to understand the embedding calculation.

Exploration preference is not recommendation quality

The preliminary evaluation involved five HCI researchers in a short exploration and interview. Casual blending was attractive, while precise goals sometimes favored a single attractor and nearby positions could be hard to distinguish. This is formative interaction evidence rather than a population estimate or retention result. The paper also proposes context-limited learning from later likes; that should not be represented as a completed feature. To assess temporary-interest contamination, inspect subsequent recommendations after an unusual browsing session. A participant’s confidence that the system kept contexts separate cannot substitute for observing whether the preference state actually remained separated.

Compare with lists and saved collections

Saved collections, separate follow lists and temporary searches provide practical alternatives. They offer less continuous blending but can make the active scope easier to explain. A small pilot could hold the candidate content constant across exploratory browsing, precise retrieval and return-to-normal tasks. Measure relevant discoveries, time spent discarding irrelevant material and understanding of the persistent preference weight. We did not independently test recommendations or establish commercial pricing. If the map produces more movement without reducing filtering work, the team should reconsider the control granularity rather than add further attractors and assume that more selectable interests necessarily improve the experience.

A photograph becomes something editable

Copying a shelf from a photograph requires more than its silhouette: each panel needs dimensions, a position and a relationship to its neighbors. Fabivo’s assistant accepts an image or text and produces a parametric furniture model. A builder can supply a known width, ask for doors or move a shelf with the mouse, then regenerate the current cutting list. That keeps an edit connected to the parts instead of asking somebody to transfer dimensions into another spreadsheet. The supported territory is sheet-goods furniture. Sloping boards are possible; upholstery, curved backs, metal frames and turned parts are outside its stated scope. Hidden depth and joints still need human input.

Check the boards before planning the cuts

The planner connects the drawing to sheet layout and assembly. Users enter their actual stock, saw kerf, edge trimming and grain direction. Its optimizer arranges edge-to-edge cuts and produces a numbered sequence alongside the layout. Exports carry the parts or diagrams to the workshop or supplier. Assembly then follows the model’s panel contacts and supports: structure, internal parts, fronts and adjustment. Each step identifies the pieces, hardware, tools and a check such as squareness or clearance. Choosing face screws or pocket holes changes markings and parts of the order. The useful handoff is a set of explicit steps that the builder can inspect before fastening.

Seventeen photos test this particular assistant

The October 26 internal comparison used seventeen furniture photos, the same prompt and tools, and medium reasoning for three models. Each photo received one session, with tool-based corrections allowed inside it. The maker publishes side-by-side outputs and acknowledges that some examples informed development, so this is not a held-out evaluation. Every output passed the app’s validity checks, but a pictured tapered leg became square. Separately, the October 27 recording reaches nine boards, a single-sheet layout and nine assembly steps. Its AI waiting period is accelerated sixfold and other segments are shortened. It demonstrates the connected workflow rather than elapsed task time or a completed wooden object.

Local projects and an online assistant

The core modeler, optimizer, assembly flow and exports need no account and can work offline. The public repository describes browser autosave and versioned JSON projects. AI is optional: invoking it sends the message, open model and attached images to the service, which uses Azure OpenAI. The privacy policy retains complete conversations, tool results and photos for thirty days; the browser also retains its own conversation. Clearing site data deletes local projects, making exports important. For someone starting from a reference photograph, this can reduce the initial redrawing. Before cutting, the builder still has to confirm dimensions, materials, joinery and fixing details against the actual project.

Start with a defined workstation

A factory robot is usually being asked to perform a repeatable piece of work: moving material to a defined place or transferring it between machines. AGIBOT’s October 29 formal G2 Max release targets manufacturing and logistics, with 21 degrees of freedom, up to 40 kg dual-arm payload and a stated working-height range of 0.1 to 2.5 meters. These specifications help an integrator decide whether further evaluation is worthwhile. They do not replace acceptance at the workstation. Objects, contact conditions and the response to failure remain site-specific. Existing deployments of the earlier G2 must also remain separate from evidence that the newly released G2 Max has completed a customer acceptance process.

A software layer opens to selected partners

Link-U OS 1.0 organizes hardware abstraction, an embodied kernel and intelligent services into a common stack. The stated aim is to reduce repeated adaptation between hardware and skills. AGIBOT says it has been validated on production robots, is entering trials with selected partners and is scheduled for full open source by year-end. Joining a partner trial now and downloading source code later are distinct access paths. The material reviewed does not establish an immediately available complete repository. For integration, the practical work remains checking drivers, real-time control requirements and responsibility for recovery. A shared interface can organize that work without automatically supplying the correct behavior for every connected machine.

A model preview is not field acceptance

GO-3 Preview combines reasoning, prediction and action in demonstrations of zero-shot or few-shot task handling. The Preview label matters. A demonstration can show the intended sequence of behavior without proving repeatability across factories, objects and lighting conditions. WITA Omni similarly brings together vision, speech, dialogue, expressions and action in the vendor’s account of interaction capabilities. Neither model name establishes the complete sensors or computing configuration of a particular robot. Chinese launch reporting puts GO-3 open source at year-end, which is different from having downloadable weights or a deployment package today. These announcements give developers directions to examine; they do not substitute for testing an integrated physical system.

Ask what the robot produces while working

AGIBOT also proposes Worken, combining active robot count, autonomous working hours and an output-efficiency ratio. The useful question behind it is whether attention can move from shipped units to productive work. It remains a vendor proposal rather than an externally accepted standard. Comparing sites would still require definitions for human interventions, task difficulty and repeated work after failure. Powered-on time, online time and autonomous operation describe different states. For prospective users, the next meaningful evidence would be clear integration documentation and task records from the partner trials. Those would make it possible to judge whether the new software and hardware combination helps a real production step consistently.

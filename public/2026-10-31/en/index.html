MLNS turns sparse steering into target guidance while preserving user priority

MLNS studies wheelchair navigation when users can provide only sparse left or right commands, sometimes incorrectly.

Direction buttons and visual targets feed a planner; a separate avoidance layer checks the wheelchair footprint. User commands take priority over automatic targets.

Ten nondisabled participants attempted 360 laboratory runs: 359 completed, with no collisions. Some conditions randomly reversed twenty percent of commands.

Visual guidance reduced average corrective inputs on two routes but increased them on the route with several tables. Direct reversing was disabled because rear coverage was insufficient.

Product perspective

StepControl edits motion through foot-contact timing, with sliding still unresolved

StepControl lets animation researchers direct full-body motion by specifying when each foot touches the ground.

A short motion prefix and two binary contact tracks produce continuing skeletal motion. Schedule edits change cadence, insert pauses or synchronize both feet for jumping.

On held-out motion data, twenty-second rollouts improved contact-transition F1 over two adapted baselines. This was not an animator usability study.

Raw output has more stance-foot sliding; correction reduces it, while floating and single-foot hopping remain problems. Runtime figures use one RTX 4090.

Product perspective

Timing becomes an explicit control, but physical contact guarantees and a validated consumer editor remain absent. Contact timing does not specify every movement or establish finished animation quality.

Preflight checks a target before clicking and replans when the screen changes

Preflight Bench studies clicks planned from stale screenshots and provides checks, measurement code and data for computer-use agents.

Wait for network quiet before capturing the screen; inspect target pixels before consequential clicks, and replan from a fresh screenshot when the check fails.

Across thirty-one public pages, stale targets affected 0.4 percent of planned clicks after loading and seventeen percent within the first second after opening.

On synthetic tests, the pixel check detected eighty-two percent of stale targets with four percent false alarms. Outcome-detection results have a limited test scope.

Product perspective

Commercial evidence covers archived desktop home pages, without checkout tasks or end-to-end execution of the ten reviewed agents. in this study.

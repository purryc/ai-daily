One request across an existing deck

Revising a finished deck can involve repeating the same small adjustment across many pages. Brush appears under October 29 in Canva’s newsroom index and October 30 on its research landing page; this entry keeps that ambiguity and treats it as a recent supplement. Users open an existing design or provide its link in Canva AI, select Brush, and describe the change. The result remains a design whose objects can be selected and adjusted. One instruction can therefore express an intention for several pages. The practical review still happens page by page, including elements the user expected to remain unchanged. Adapting a slide for another channel produces a new layout to inspect, not evidence that every spacing decision is finished.

The editing loop checks the page

Canva describes a loop that runs code to adjust a design and inspects the page before deciding what to do next. It reports post-training an open-weights base model and applying reinforcement learning to design tasks. That explains the iteration mechanism without guaranteeing preservation of every relationship. A practical approach is to start with named objects and broaden the scope once the result is understandable. Editing text objects and layout also differs from the existing Magic Edit photo workflow, where a user selects a photograph and marks an area to replace. These operate at different levels. A brush-shaped control elsewhere in Canva is not, by itself, this newly announced model.

Stopping and correcting a broad edit

The conversational-editing help page gives a concrete recovery path: submit a request, review the changes, and continue the conversation. During processing, Stop lets the user interrupt and either provide another instruction or edit manually. Unexpected changes can be undone before retrying with clearer references to position, size or color. The help page also cautions that complicated layered designs and long conversations can slow work or reduce quality. These are controls of the surrounding editing experience, not all new Brush features. For a bulk change, body copy, page numbers, charts and brand elements deserve separate checks. A visually consistent deck may still contain an incorrect statement, and an undo command does not detect that mistake.

[S32]

Brush access is narrower than Canva AI as a whole

The launch announcement limits Brush to supported edits for Canva Pro and identifies Brand Kit support, reference attachments and image-heavy layouts as unfinished work. Canva AI’s broader help page lists other eligible plans and possible language, region or administrator restrictions; that does not establish Brush rollout to every one of those plans. The announced speed and operating-cost gains are internal task comparisons, not a promised subscription discount or fixed completion time. Adoption begins with checking whether the model choice appears in the account and trying a familiar recurring edit. Teams with strict brand requirements should retain their templates and review process rather than infer Brush support from a general Canva AI feature description.

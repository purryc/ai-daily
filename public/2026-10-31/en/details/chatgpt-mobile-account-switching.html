Changing identity becomes a mobile setting

Keeping personal and work ChatGPT accounts can make repeated mobile sign-outs interrupt a conversation. The October 30 release extends account switching to iOS and Android, with the help page explicitly describing a gradual rollout. A missing menu therefore does not necessarily mean an account is misconfigured. In Settings, users select Switch account, add a second login and then choose the identity they want. Two accounts can remain signed in, with only one active. Web switching already existed; the new workflow is mobile. It helps alternate between contexts without turning two accounts into one shared conversation space or adding another paid entitlement.

Notifications can change the active account

Switching displays the selected account’s conversations, memory, files, settings and workspaces without copying them between accounts. Notifications can arrive for both signed-in accounts according to their settings and device permissions. Opening a notification from the other account switches into that account and opens its content. Remembering which account was active earlier is therefore not always enough to identify where the next message will go. The current-account indicator is the direct check before sending. On mobile, logging out of the active account opens the remaining one; logging out of the last returns to sign-in. When two are already present, Add account is hidden until one is removed.

The subscription follows the purchasing account

Subscription guidance distinguishes the ChatGPT account from the Apple or Google Play billing account: a mobile purchase is linked to both the store account and the ChatGPT identity used for purchase. Switching to a Free account does not bring the other subscription with it, and restoring a purchase does not transfer it to whichever identity happens to be open. If a paid user sees Free, identifying the original sign-in and purchasing account helps avoid duplicate charges. Switching and moving records are also separate tasks; OpenAI provides separate export-transfer guidance. The switcher should therefore be understood as a faster route into another context, with historical-data migration still requiring its own process.

Organizational controls remain attached to the account

Managed-account guidance says an organization may, subject to its configuration and applicable rules, access, export, retain or delete data and control available services. A personal and a managed account remain distinct even when both appear in the same switcher. Content created under the work identity continues to follow organizational controls. Web switching can also be restricted by an administrator; broad web plan availability does not prove that every mobile account has received the rollout. The improvement is fewer steps between identities. The remaining decision is which identity is active and where the content belongs. Clear account feedback matters especially when a device is shared or the user frequently moves between personal and work tasks.

Allowance changes belong to the relevant account

A separate October 29 announcement changes Work and Codex allowances. Pro 100 and Pro 500 receive twice their previous allowance. The planned Pro 200 reduction is canceled: subscribers already on the higher allowance retain it, while those on the lower allowance receive a doubling from that date. The heading also names Business Premium, but the body does not provide the same precise increase for that plan. This is separate from mobile account switching. Moving between accounts does not combine those allowances or subscriptions; available usage still belongs to the corresponding account.

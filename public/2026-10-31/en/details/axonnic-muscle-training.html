When the hand leaves the camera view

Recording a skilled worker gives a robotics team visible motion, but similar movements can involve very different effort. Axonnic puts its sensing sleeve on the forearm so the fingers remain free to handle the actual workpiece. In the maker’s Product Hunt demonstration, a hand enters a box while the system continues decoding its movement; another demonstration drives a robotic hand from muscle signals. These are useful demonstrations of the proposed input channel. They do not establish that a sewing or assembly policy will succeed in production. For a buyer, the first distinction is between recovering a movement signal and teaching a robot a complete task with that signal.

The sleeve belongs in data collection

The company describes a collection pipeline that aligns EMG with video and hand pose, then retargets demonstrations into a robot’s action space. The deployed robot runs on its own sensors rather than wearing the sleeve. That separation matters: a manufacturer is buying a way to produce training data, not necessarily adding a new sensor to every deployed machine. It also leaves a substantial integration task. A human hand and a robot gripper may have different joints, reachable motions and contact behavior. Inferred effort should therefore be treated as an additional training signal, with a clear distinction between electrical measurements and model estimates of physical force. The resulting policy still needs task-level evaluation.

Re-donning is part of the workflow

Axonnic’s own site describes a comparison with Apple Vision Pro hand tracking and discusses recalibration after putting the sleeve back on. A separate recording uses a decoder trained four days earlier. Those conditions should remain separate; a favorable result in one setup does not establish performance across workers. An October 24 trigger demonstration also compares a force estimate with an instrumented glove before removing the glove. These examples suggest practical questions about collection sessions: how much placement variation is tolerable, when recalibration is required, and whether the target work changes the signal. The appropriate evaluation is repeated recording during the intended job, including ordinary interruptions and changes of tools.

[S21]

A pilot is the beginning of integration

The Product Hunt maker says sleeves are shipping and paid pilots have started. The company site separately describes a manufacturing letter of intent and recording work on construction sites. Those are different commercial stages, and none supplies a published production-throughput comparison. Data ownership is agreed per engagement. A customer therefore needs a concrete agreement covering recordings, permitted training uses and contributions to broader models, alongside the task evaluation. Axonnic offers an interesting collection channel for difficult manipulation, but the purchasing case depends on what that channel changes in the trained policy. A successful recording session, a paid pilot and a reliable deployed robot are three separate outcomes.

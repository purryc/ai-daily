Matic Serene changes the vacuum stack to reduce its high-pitched tone

The target is the conspicuous tone

Matic’s October 27 Serene explanation focuses on a robot cleaning while people share the room: during calls, films or rest. The revision targets a conspicuous high-frequency tone in the vacuum assembly while retaining the motor and suction. The company describes changes to the paths by which sound travels through airflow and the motor structure. The user’s cleaning request and intended floor result remain familiar; what changes is the sound entering the room while the task runs. That is a meaningful hardware distinction. It does not need to be presented as a new AI model, and it is separate from the software maintenance guidance covered in the previous issue.

Overall level and tonal change are different measures

The engineering article reports two different vendor measurements: an overall change from 55 to 52 dB and approximately 10–15 dB less of the prominent high-frequency tone. The latter should not be represented as the reduction in total machine noise. An accompanying spectrum is explicitly simplified for illustration rather than supplied as a complete raw measurement trace. Translating either measurement into a household experience introduces room reflections, floor material, distance and operating mode. The numbers therefore provide a specific design target, while a useful comparison would hold the cleaning task constant and assess both sound and cleaning results. A marketing percentage cannot establish identical perceived comfort in every home.

The existing vision system still handles the floor

Serene builds on Matic’s existing cleaning platform. The technical page describes RGB and infrared cameras feeding environmental perception, a three-dimensional map and recognition of floors and obstacles for vacuuming and mopping. These explain how the robot carries out its task; they are not newly introduced sensors in the October 27 revision. Users still choose cleaning areas, schedules and exclusion zones in the app, and still maintain the water tank, bag and rollers. A less intrusive sound could make occupied-room operation more acceptable. It does not establish simultaneous improvements in avoidance, mapping or cleaning speed. The adoption judgment should stay attached to the mechanical change actually disclosed.

Read the selected variant’s delivery terms

The current configurator lists the original Matic at $1,495 and Serene at $1,695, with Serene shipping scheduled to start November 15. Generic two-week wording elsewhere on the page should not override the selected variant’s date. Official sales are currently limited to the United States, with conditions attached to returns and warranty. Those details affect the cost of trying a quieter machine. The advertised perceived-loudness improvement is a vendor characterization, not a statistic from an independent household trial. Existing owners also need the actual hardware-upgrade terms; a software update cannot be assumed to install a new vacuum assembly. Long-term experience remains a question for observation after delivery.

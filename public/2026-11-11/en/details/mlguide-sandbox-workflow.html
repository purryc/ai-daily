AI Sandbox connects data preparation to experiments while methodological judgment remains separate

Make the experiment’s stages visible

AI Sandbox connects tabular data preparation, experiment configuration and recorded execution. Its maratus component prepares data and metadata; mlkitchen organizes the experiment; mlflow supports runs and comparison. A beginner can construct the workflow without first writing a complete program. Our interpretation is that the strongest interface benefit is making order and dependencies visible. A diagram should help someone understand why a transformation must not learn from a held-out test set. Replacing code with blocks is insufficient if the blocks conceal which data each step has seen. The visible chain is useful when it makes a methodological question easier to ask and inspect.

Separate guidance from implementation

mlguide supplies structured methodological guidance, while the implementation tools turn selected choices into an executable configuration. The paper evaluates those two forms of support separately. Our product recommendation is to retain the reason, conditions and alternatives behind a recommendation, particularly for imbalanced labels, missing values or potentially leaking fields. A default can reduce the number of clicks while leaving the important decision unresolved. A beginner should be able to explain why a metric fits the task rather than merely selecting the highlighted option. For a team, that explanation also matters later, when another person needs to reconstruct why the original experiment was considered appropriate.

A more pleasant interface did not establish lower workload

Eighteen predominantly technically trained participants completed two within-participant studies. Guidance improved high-level workflow structure but not the detailed methodological quiz significantly. Compared with Google Colab, the visual environment received better hedonic-experience ratings; perceived workload and acceptance did not differ significantly. Fixed ordering in the first study limits causal interpretation. These outcomes argue against merging ease of use, understanding and time saving into one success metric. Our proposed evaluation would separately inspect errors, explanations and transfer to a different dataset without guidance. Preference ratings can inform interface refinement, but they cannot establish that the resulting experiment is statistically or methodologically sound.

The paper does not establish a ready deployment

The paper uses a modified task dataset to examine workflow design; it does not establish clinical deployment. Although the authors describe the tools as open source, a footnote says the repository link will follow publication. We did not verify an installable release, pricing, regions or maintenance arrangements. The early November 10 submission precedes this issue’s main window and is labeled as a recent supplement. A practical adoption test could begin with a low-risk teaching task: can configurations be exported, can runs be traced, and are old results clearly distinguished after data changes? Those are proposed checks, not tests completed by this publication.

Start with an animation that remains editable

Lottie AI focuses on short product motion such as loaders, icons, empty states and onboarding scenes. A user describes movement, optionally supplies an SVG or image, and sets duration, palette and dimensions. Generation produces a central preview with an Objects & Controls panel. The standalone generator should be distinguished from Lottie Creator, the existing editor that can receive results for more detailed work. Turbo is positioned for exploration and Quality for more planning and refinement; these labels are not a benchmark. An animation containing imported artwork still needs inspection, and its result should not be assumed to have the same properties as a wholly vector scene.

Changing a color need not restart generation

The official introduction describes a prompt-to-Frolic-script path compiled into standard Lottie output. Controls allow changes such as color, size or speed without generating the entire sequence again. Layer-directed requests and version history support further revision. This makes the first result a starting point that can survive a brand-color change, rather than a flattened clip requiring another attempt. Readable code also gives developers an object to inspect, but does not prove visual correctness or compatibility with every player. A useful acceptance task is to recolor a success animation, reduce its display size and check whether its meaning survives a reduced-motion treatment.

Credits follow generation rather than every adjustment

The allowance documentation lists ten lifetime Starter generations, not a monthly reset. Pro includes 300 monthly AI credits and Team 600. A successful generation or refinement costs five credits; adjusting existing controls without regeneration is free, and Turbo and Quality have the same credit cost. Paid credits are shared with other LottieFiles AI tools. Monthly leftovers expire, while purchased credits persist. Failed or canceled generations are described as restored allowances, not cash refunds. The current full subscription price was not independently verified here. For a creator’s budget, repeated new prompts and ordinary control adjustments are materially different operations, even when both appear to be small visual edits.

Sharing the animation can also share the conversation

The November 10 terms permit commercial output use even on free plans, while preserving responsibility for imported material and possible similarity to existing work. A share link reveals more than a preview: its version includes prompts, replies, code and incorporated artwork. Recipients can copy and continue working; deleting the conversation does not remove the link or recall existing copies. The vendor names external model providers and says inputs and outputs are not used for training. That does not make generation offline. Availability also depends on supported provider regions. Unreleased client material deserves particular care before sharing. No generation, commercial clearance or cross-platform playback test was performed here.

Start with the screenshot backlog

Snipkit combines capture, recording and a searchable reference library in a Mac menu-bar app. Its November 8 version 0.4.16 tackles a practical adoption problem before adding more AI: existing screenshots are already scattered in a folder. Setup now locates the system screenshot folder and offers to import real screenshots while skipping items already in the library. Choosing a folder for saved copies also offers an import. The same setup lets people choose capture shortcuts, while a failed shortcut registration opens the relevant settings with an explanation. This is a change to an existing app, not the first release of voice capture. The date has no published timezone; it is conservatively treated as a weekly lookback.

Point, speak, and keep the edit movable

For an edit, the person holds Fn, points to a location and describes the annotation, or first draws a rough selection. Shapes and text remain movable objects when the saved capture is reopened. The library retains screenshots, recordings, text clippings, colors and bookmarks with their source and spoken context, so retrieval can begin with remembered content rather than a carefully chosen filename. The current update makes source-app chips clickable and supports tag or app filters in search. This can reduce the separate naming, moving and annotation steps after capture. It does not establish that the model understands every visual reference or that automatic personal-information hiding will catch every sensitive detail.

The keyboard path must work when speaking does not

The actually visible Reddit discussion exposes a different constraint from recognition accuracy. One commenter says they work almost entirely by keyboard and cannot comfortably speak commands during a video meeting; they also could not find scrolling capture. The maker acknowledges discoverability and explains the shortcut and menu paths. Another asks about nested-folder import, with the maker then describing a two-level limit. A separate installer reports a missing Screen Recording permission entry. These are individual reports from the visible discussion, not incidence measurements or proof of current defects. Showing shortcut choices during onboarding addresses a plausible usability gap, but does not prove every reported problem has been fixed. Relative post ages conflict with cache dates, so they are not used as release timestamps.

Local models and shared links have different boundaries

The download page requires Apple silicon and macOS 26 or later and offers a fourteen-day trial without an account. The homepage lists US$7.99 for one year of updates or US$24.99 for lifetime updates, each for two Macs. Local AI edits use Gemma or Apple Intelligence; manual annotation does not require a thinking model. The privacy policy separates local processing from optional sharing: creating a link uploads the finished image or chosen text. Anyone holding that link can view it without authentication, and expiry cannot recall downloaded copies. A useful adoption check is therefore a harmless import-and-edit task followed by inspection of the exported redaction, rather than assuming local AI makes every later sharing action private. Shared links expire after 24 hours.

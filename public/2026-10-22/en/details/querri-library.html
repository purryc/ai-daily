Questions become persistent objects

Querri’s original October 19 article announces Library and Librarian; a later Product Hunt appearance does not establish another release. Without a precise launch clock, this issue conservatively places it in the seven-day lookback. A recurring analytics workflow often begins by uploading another spreadsheet, restating a metric and asking an assistant to write another query. Previously agreed calculations can disappear inside earlier conversations. The new library organizes questions alongside metrics, facts, reusable views and sources, grouped into collections. Its proposed advantage is making a previously answered question discoverable and reusable, rather than simply generating another answer that sounds similar. That distinction matters when several colleagues need the same definition.

Reuse calculations and inspect their basis

Librarian first considers whether existing views and metrics answer a question before accessing underlying data. Once a metric is defined, refreshes reuse its calculation code rather than requiring a language model to invent the formula again. Results expose rows, calculations and source relationships. Yet unchanged code does not guarantee unchanged business meaning: an upstream status, currency or duplicate record can invalidate an earlier definition. Our acceptance recommendation is to review both the calculation and its interpretation, including whether the assistant reused a similarly named metric with a different scope. Traceability provides a place to investigate. It cannot replace checking representative records or establishing who owns a definition when business processes change.

Cleaning and recommendations have decision boundaries

Janitor checks data quality, raises ambiguities involving dates, currencies and duplicates, and presents a cleaning plan. Approval produces a cleaned view while retaining original data. Nightly recommendations enter an inbox where a person can accept, edit or dismiss them. These are meaningful boundaries between proposing an improvement and changing an analytical artifact. Query-time permission claims still need validation with the organization’s actual roles. Planned self-healing after upstream changes and additional notification destinations should remain separate from delivered behavior. Being able to trace a failure back to a source does not establish that every dependent result can automatically recover or that an inappropriate proposed rule will be caught.

Measure the total cost of reuse

Fixed SQL, business-intelligence semantic layers, spreadsheets and a maintained data dictionary are practical alternatives. Querri’s hypothesis is easier reuse of business questions, calculations and cleaning work, against the cost of organizing sources and maintaining definitions. Pricing and real permission enforcement were not established here. A pilot should repeat the same metric over several weeks, introduce an upstream change, and compare consistency, investigation time and maintenance effort with the existing process. If colleagues still have to reconstruct the definition on every occasion, or faster answers hide stale assumptions, centralization has not solved the handoff. Reliable reuse and easier diagnosis would provide stronger evidence than an isolated fast query.

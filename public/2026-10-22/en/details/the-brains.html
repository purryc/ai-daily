Turn imitation into retrieval

The maker describes a recognizable problem: ask how a particular founder would price a product, and an ordinary assistant may answer confidently without separating documented statements from invention. The Brains packages public interviews, essays and posts into searchable MCP collections, advertising thirty-seven profiles at launch. The result is a route to material someone previously expressed, not a consultation with that person or an endorsement of the user’s project. The October 21 listing is a discovery and presentation event. Earlier privacy material and repository records mean this is a lookback rather than a claim that the code first appeared that day.

A connector still needs source reading

A user can connect one person’s endpoint or use a combined connector to list and search collections. The hosted service uses an email code at first connection. Search returns passages with source links and dates, and a separate tool lists the material available. This moves a familiar sequence—find an interview, copy a passage, ask an assistant to synthesize it—inside an existing client. The improvement should be faster access to relevant evidence, not permission to skip reading it. A cited statement about a different company stage or market can still be applied incorrectly to the current decision, even when the link itself is valid.

[S66]

Curation shapes the answer space

The construction process is curated rather than exhaustive: selected material becomes cards, third-person notes and a search index. Its runbook asks builders to distinguish the person’s own statements from a host’s views, exclude derivative or duplicated material, and check source references and sampled notes. Those rules make the maintenance process inspectable without establishing that every summary is correct. Selection still determines what the system can answer. Product researchers should look for counterexamples, changes over time and uncovered topics. Convenient retrieval can reinforce an existing assumption if a client surfaces only the passage that best supports it and ignores the conditions around that passage.

[S67]

Separate code, hosting and content

The MIT license explicitly covers code, while underlying works remain with their authors and publishers. The hosted privacy notice says it receives tool calls including search text, although not the client’s entire conversation. Self-hosting still requires maintenance and resources, and the README notes that local endpoints are open without database configuration. That differs from the authenticated hosted service. A bookmark collection or a separately maintained retrieval library remains an alternative. A small evaluation should sample citation support, speaker attribution, omitted counterexamples and total checking time. More named profiles are not a sufficient benefit if locating a source becomes faster while correcting misleading synthesis takes longer. The test should retain unanswered questions as well, so missing evidence cannot disappear behind a fluent response.

Move small decisions out of long answers

A request such as feeling cold does not need a paragraph before the application chooses whether to increase the temperature. Decisions isolates bounded judgments: the developer defines allowed answers, supplies context, and uses the result to choose the next step. The public beta appeared in our October 7 issue. October 22 adds general availability, native GPT-Live integration, caching and regional processing. For language practice, support routing or animated characters, the interesting change is the accumulation of tiny pauses within an ongoing interaction. Speak reports reducing next-action latency from 1.4 seconds to 0.13 seconds in its testing. That customer example is not a guaranteed latency for another application.

Choosing and acting remain separate

The documented car example asks separately about temperature, defogging and whether a broader response is needed. The application performs the change, reads the actual state and returns a confirmed result to the conversation. A selected action is not evidence that the device executed it. For an avatar, transcript updates can drive expressions while either participant speaks. Results may arrive out of order, so the application matches the originating event and applies the newest relevant result. This is an interaction detail, not just backend bookkeeping: a delayed happy expression could otherwise overwrite a reaction to bad news. Event identity gives the developer a concrete way to manage that timing.

Answer shape determines the job

The three answer shapes are a probability, a supplied choice and a score over ordered levels. They suit tagging, routing and bounded action selection rather than arbitrary content generation. Up to 400 independent questions can share an input; a decision that depends on an earlier answer still needs another request. A weighted score may fall between levels, so it cannot simply be treated as a category. My product interpretation is to start with the most repetitive, well-defined judgment in an existing workflow. Compared with a rule engine, language variation is easier to accommodate. Compared with full generation, the exchange gives up open expression for a clearer output contract.

Make the gain visible in conversation

Published base pricing is $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.01 for cached reads, without output-token charges; cache writes and regional processing have additional conditions. The product comparison should include the time a person waits after speaking, mistaken interruptions and the clarity of recovery when an action fails. An implementation can separate decorative expressions from actions that change external state: the former permits faster experimentation, while the latter benefits from an explicit no-action choice and state readback. This is a design inference from the interface. Faster classification accompanied by more mistaken triggers or awkward responses would not make the conversation more natural.

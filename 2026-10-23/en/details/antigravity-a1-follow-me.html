From piloting experience to self-filming

The October 20 Follow Me Bundle shifts the A1 from an immersive goggles-based piloting experience toward filming a person who is running, cycling or spending time with family. A watch handles follow modes and basic controls, a ring adjusts positioning, and the aircraft records the surrounding scene for reframing later. This changes the allocation of work rather than merely the controller casing. Capturing an activity and choosing its final framing become separate steps. The resulting benefit is less composition effort during movement, while the remaining cost is editing afterward. If the desired result is simply one fixed-angle clip, the flexibility of 360-degree footage may also mean more material to organize.

Gestures move the aircraft; the watch conveys status

Antigravity combines visual tracking with GPS in the watch to maintain a subject through movement and brief occlusion. The Drone Girl’s vendor-supplied review describes battery and tracking status on the watch, plus holding the ring button while moving an arm to change relative position. The division of work is distinctive: a person expresses a desired viewpoint through a small movement, and the system translates it into motion through space. The wearable input is therefore more than a shortcut. It changes the distance between a real machine and surrounding objects. Visible tracking and control state matter to the claimed ease of use. These observations describe a particular test, not a reliability statistic across environments.

Following a person is not avoiding everything

The independent accounts are not uniform. TechRadar praises activity tracking; The Verge reports three crashes involving difficult obstacles, while The Drone Girl notes awkward low-battery landing intervention. The broader implication is that locating a subject, maintaining composition, selecting a traversable path and ending the task all need to work together. A guard can change the consequences of contact without solving path selection. This makes the kit better understood as assisted filming that removes some piloting actions, rather than a photographer that can be ignored once activity begins. The disagreement also leaves environmental and operator differences unresolved. Combining these accounts into a single claimed success rate would erase the very distinctions that make the reviews useful.

Existing owners can upgrade; kits remain distinct

Reviews list $799 for a package including the aircraft and approximately $340 for accessories for existing owners. The announcement mentions an owner promotion, with regional availability distinguished. Separate dual-stick and cellular accessories were announced alongside it, but they are not required Follow Me components. The official page explicitly says the dual-stick controller still requires Vision goggles. Combining the simultaneous announcements would wrongly imply that every new control mode works without a headset. Guards also increase takeoff weight, with reviews listing roughly 351 grams when fitted. The purchase decision should center on subject-following and later reframing, rather than accessory count or an assumption that every newly announced capability belongs to one default package.

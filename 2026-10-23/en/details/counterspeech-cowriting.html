Personal intent comes before generated wording

This research prototype places counterspeech assistance in a compact browser extension. Users paste a hateful comment and must contribute their own idea, wording, or perspective before generation. They identify their relationship to the targeted group, choose familiar or formal style and approximate length, and receive three editable alternatives. Selecting, revising, and copying the final message remain human actions. This sequence creates two opportunities for authorship: before a draft exists and after alternatives become visible. It does not prove that a required text field always produces independent thought. The interesting design choice is that personal intent becomes an input to generation rather than merely an approval gesture applied to a completed machine response.

Retrieved examples shape the alternatives

The implementation combines a Manifest V3 extension, a FastAPI backend, FAISS retrieval, and a Llama model hosted through Groq. It retrieves five semantically similar hate-speech and counterspeech pairs from Multitarget CONAN and adds them to the prompt alongside the user's contribution and controls. The study did not isolate whether dynamic retrieval improves output relative to static examples. Its prompt also restricts introducing new factual claims, aiming to limit unsupported material. That choice may frustrate someone who wants an evidence-based rebuttal. My editorial interpretation is that expressing a position and researching a factual response are separate tasks. A product should make that boundary legible instead of quietly substituting a familiar rhetorical template for evidentiary support.

Longer composition and uncertain quality ratings

Ten participants first wrote unassisted responses to three self-selected comments and then repeated the task with the tool; five also contributed think-aloud sessions and interviews. Mean composition time increased from 2.52 to 3.88 minutes. Peer-rated averages rose across five textual qualities, most strongly for empathy, but agreement among the four raters was low. These are descriptive text judgments, not evidence of persuasion in live online exchanges. The fixed condition order also leaves familiarity entangled with assistance. Willingness to post increased for four participants, decreased for two, and stayed unchanged for four. For an everyday user, this may support deliberate formulation without establishing faster posting or a greater willingness to enter a hostile conversation.

Voice requires more than a politeness setting

All five interviewees wanted a wider range of rhetorical styles, including sarcasm, direct disagreement, anger, and factual argument. Empathy-oriented outputs sometimes felt generic or unnatural. The extension acts on explicit submissions without scanning the page, and users are told that data goes to an external model; previously transmitted data cannot be retrieved through a local deletion action. The paper supplies no verified store release, consumer price, or long-term usage result. The product lesson is not simply to remove restraint. It is to distinguish attacking someone from firmly defending one's own position, offer meaningfully different arguments and tones, and preserve the option not to respond. Three polite paraphrases are not necessarily three meaningful choices for the person carrying the social consequences.

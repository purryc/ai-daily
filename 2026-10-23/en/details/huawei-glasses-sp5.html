From spoken payment to a reversible head gesture

This update changes the final action in a familiar transaction. Huawei documents a payment mode in which a nod accepts a spoken confirmation prompt and a head shake cancels it. The everyday appeal is easy to picture: someone carrying shopping bags can finish a transaction without reaching for a temple button or speaking again. Our interpretation is that the meaningful design choice is the combination of a prompt and a deliberate response. The glasses are not choosing a purchase on the wearer’s behalf. Nor does adding a gesture establish that it is faster or more comfortable than pressing a button. That comparison still depends on recognition, posture and the circumstances in which the wearer actually pays.

Parking starts with vehicle setup

Parking is a separate workflow with a preparatory step. CNMO describes registering the vehicle plate inside the parking section of the glasses app before looking at a car park’s payment QR code. This matters because paying for parking has an extra piece of identity: the vehicle whose stay must be settled. For someone walking back from a shopping centre, the potential saving is avoiding a sequence of phone retrieval, app navigation and plate entry. That is an editorial reading of the workflow, not a measured time saving. It also does not mean every gate will open automatically. The available reporting gives no coverage count or evidence about the delay between a successful payment and recognition at the exit.

Availability depends on a complete device combination

The official requirements are more specific than a simple instruction to upgrade the phone to HarmonyOS 7. The support page specifies minimum glasses and app versions, includes certain Pura phones on a qualifying HarmonyOS 6.1 release, and otherwise points to compatible phones running HarmonyOS 7 or later. Alipay setup and companion authentication are required. A reconnect or an authentication session older than four hours requires locking and unlocking the phone again. Those dependencies explain why two owners of the same glasses may have different experiences. In our assessment, the feature makes most sense as an incremental convenience for someone already using the supported ecosystem. Buying an entire device combination solely to remove one phone interaction is a different proposition.

Longer capture is useful for a continuous moment

IT Home also reports that continuous recording extends to five minutes. This belongs to the same software update on an existing pair of glasses, not to a newly introduced hardware model called SP5. A longer clip could help someone capture a complete demonstration or a short uninterrupted travel moment without restarting the camera midway. That is an inference about the interaction; it is not a claim about image quality or battery endurance. Payment and recording illustrate two different kinds of convenience in this release: shortening a transaction and extending a capture session. The reporting does not establish a universal rollout date for every account or provide new subscription terms. Owners should distinguish an available hardware product from the arrival of its latest firmware.

Two different interaction paths

Eiliko combines two interaction paths in a small clip-on toy. Expressive faces and touch reactions provide one kind of companionship, while internet-connected conversation provides another. Its official FAQ describes Bluetooth and app setup, Wi-Fi for AI chat, and more than ten preset offline voice commands. Buying the physical object therefore also means depending on a changing service. The present change concerns service features and data handling; it does not establish that every offline animation has stopped. This distinction matters to the intended use. Someone seeking a decorative companion with simple reactions may value a different part of the product from someone expecting a conversational relationship that remembers previous exchanges.

The new event is removal and a deletion pledge

The October 22 Verge follow-up reports that Energize Lab removed long-term memory and custom avatars and promised deletion, while the reporter still found media exposure and device access. The new evidence is the response and its contradiction, not another product launch. Feature removal changes what owners can do now; it does not itself demonstrate that previously collected information has disappeared. The distinction is especially consequential when the interface encourages personal conversation. Friendly wording from a toy cannot serve as a storage-status indicator. If memory returns later, whether it starts fresh or uses retained material would change the relationship users believe they are resuming.

A portable object with service dependence

The store still exposes a purchase interface and advertises subscription-free chat, a 70-gram body and up to 150 minutes of operation. The extracted page did not provide a reliable current standalone price, so a neighboring product price is not substituted. Battery duration remains a vendor specification. Availability for purchase does not contradict the reported service problems. The app offers volume and brightness controls, but those controls do not communicate data-retention status. Another easily misunderstood constraint is permanent pairing: the official FAQ says it concerns the couple ID between two toys, rather than binding an owner to one phone. That distinction affects gifting and reassignment of the social interaction.

Continuity includes the way memory ends

The earlier October 15 investigation grounds this follow-up in a concrete owned-device conversation that a researcher could retrieve. No intrusion or access to exposed personal material was performed for this report. The product implication is about continuity: if remembering an owner makes a companion feel personal, the end of that memory needs an intelligible explanation too. A conventional offline interactive toy offers less potential personalization but does not depend on the same server-side memory relationship. For Eiliko, the supported conclusion is narrower than either a permanent product shutdown or a completed repair. Its feature scope has changed, and the reported condition of the service remains contested. A future review must not be presented as completed independent certification.

OKA Mori · Preorder / companion robot

Mori offers companionship through touch and ambient reactions, while preorder terms conflict

One touch as the entry point

Mori is a home companion robot presented as a lamp-like object for people who value a responsive atmosphere. Its October 22 announcement describes quiet company delivered through movements, expressions, light and sound. The practical change is the entry point: touch the object to begin an ambient experience, instead of finding a phone, choosing media and putting the screen away. That is an editorial interpretation of the workflow, not a measured reduction in stress. A buyer should understand the object as a particular way to spend time at home. A reaction that feels appropriate can be enjoyable without establishing that the device understands the person's emotional state.

Variable reactions rather than a command list

OKA calls its internal state VIBE, covering vitality, interaction, bonding and emotion. It says this state changes with experience and affects how expressions are combined. The same interaction can therefore produce different reactions. The design tradeoff is specific: variability can make a companion feel alive while weakening the predictability expected from a tool. Reading quietly and hosting friends also demand different kinds of presence. For this category, successful interaction is partly the ability to leave the object alone without an unfinished conversation. These are implications of the announced mechanism. The release does not supply longitudinal household results or an accuracy measure showing how reliably Mori chooses an appropriate response.

[S01]

The camera has a physical visibility state

The release says retracting the head physically conceals the camera; the homepage separately claims that perception is processed locally without sending sensed data to the cloud. One is a visible physical state, while the other is a vendor statement about data processing. They answer different household questions. A guest can notice a concealed camera without first learning the companion app, whereas consent to account terms normally belongs to the purchaser. No network test was performed for this report, and the inspected pages do not fully enumerate diagnostic and account data flows. Local perception should consequently remain a bounded claim. The useful design feature is that a sensing boundary has a physical expression in the object itself.

A clear entry price with conflicting benefits

The $649 entry price is consistent, but surrounding terms are not. The launch release describes later standard preorders at $799 with a $79 deposit. The observed shop instead compares $649 with $849 and estimates delivery in late December 2026. It offers two years of Nature Time, while a separate First Access support article says one year. The subscription support page directs customers to the Mori app and says regional plans and renewal terms can differ. These are preorder representations, not evidence of units delivered. An ordinary lamp and audio playback remain a simpler substitute; Mori’s added proposition is the responsive object. The unresolved discrepancy matters because those accompanying benefits form part of the purchase, and the most generous statements cannot safely be combined into a single guaranteed package.

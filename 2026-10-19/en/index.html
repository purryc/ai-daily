Hardware / API / system stack

The documented stack includes knowledge, tools and CRM write-back; Enterprise lists API/MCP. Integration and permission details still require verification. [S02] [S05]

Concrete use cases

Hypothesis: faster follow-through yields more accepted qualified leads per dollar. First test ten scripted scenarios, including refusal, interruption, wrong contact and unavailable representative. If safety and routing pass, compare a small consented pilot with the existing process. Measure accepted leads, corrections, complaints and fully loaded cost; worse quality or economics falsifies the initial case. This is our proposed evaluation, not a completed test.

For a multimodal Input Agent, treat the recording, its interpretation and the proposed business action as separate objects. A client saying they would like to see something next week should not silently become a delivery commitment. Preserve the statement, identify its uncertain meaning and ask the operator to resolve it. If a caller refers to an image that arrives later, the resulting brief should retain the relationship between that image and the relevant conversation segment. The reviewed materials do not establish that Harmony supplies this cross-modal correspondence; it is a design hypothesis, not an additional feature claim.

Pain points solved

Manual SDR calls are the baseline. Vapi documents the same qualification, booking and CRM-update job using configurable tools. Harmony therefore needs to prove easier operation or better economics, not merely that an AI can call. The reviewed sources did not include a head-to-head benchmark. [S04]

For a small studio, the first decision is whether telephone conversations are a valuable input channel at all. If clients mainly communicate through email and comments on designs, adding a calling agent may add another channel to maintain. A more useful hypothetical job is a client describing a project by phone, followed by a reviewable brief that helps the studio decide whether to accept the work. The valuable output is not an impressive recording. It is a handoff that distinguishes agreed requirements, open questions and the person responsible for resolving them. This is our proposed scenario, not a verified Harmony customer deployment.

Limits and unknowns

The interaction model makes individual operations inspectable. Its permissions guide describes per-tool allow, approval and block settings, with approval cards exposing parameters and retaining the decision. However, that older guide’s broad prohibition on deletion conflicts with the latest Outlook-calendar description. Do not promise a universal no-delete guarantee or assume every connector shares identical safeguards. [S08] Separately, live web-research indicators show searches and domains, then collapse into expandable step history. Their role is progress visibility; they are not a substitute for citations in the finished answer. [S09]

Recovery is a particularly valuable test. Imagine that a draft is saved, but the network fails before the agent receives confirmation. On retry, the system should look for the existing artifact before creating another nearly identical draft. In a second case, the meeting exists but the email has not been sent. The interface needs to explain those states separately rather than display a single completion badge. For a small team without a dedicated administrator, understanding partial success may matter more than having additional model choices.

Product verdict

The practical alternative is retaining the existing office-tool workflow and its human handoffs; teams already satisfied with another assistant may have little reason to switch. UI evaluation should ask whether users can distinguish proposed action, approved action, saved draft, sent message and completed event without reading a long transcript. Recovery deserves special attention: resuming must not silently duplicate work. A useful pilot would demonstrate lower total handling time at unchanged factual accuracy. No measured productivity improvement or revenue uplift is established by this release coverage.

Use five synthetic project exchanges: a routine revision, conflicting attachments, unavailable colleagues, a withdrawn request and an execution interrupted by an exhausted budget. Ask team members to identify the current state and appropriate next action without reading the whole conversation transcript. Measure mistaken judgments, duplicate artifacts and the actions required to recover. These observations directly test whether the workflow reduces handling effort. If a person must reread every source before trusting every approval, the system may have exchanged data-entry labor for review labor without improving the result.

Limits and unknowns

The critical interaction is failure handling. An empty result, blocked page, exhausted balance and rate limit should not become an identical “try again” message. The documentation distinguishes those cases. A PM can use that distinction to make recovery intelligible: show what failed and preserve useful partial evidence. This is a proposed product design, not a tested DeepSeek interface. For self-hosting, the official model card describes prompt encoding and protocol conversion, while leaving inference, tool execution and transport with the integrator. Open weights do not erase those operational responsibilities. [S14] [S15]

A concrete prototype is source checking before an input becomes a permanent record. The user supplies three links and a summary. The agent produces an evidence inventory showing which page supports each claim, when it was observed, whether the body was readable and whether sources conflict. If a page cannot be retrieved, keep the link and failure reason and let the user supply a file or skip it. Do not quietly replace an unread page with a search summary and mark it reviewed. A ranking score should not remove the need for judgment. These are proposed behaviors, not results from testing DeepSeek.

Product verdict

The value hypothesis is lower integration and maintenance effort for teams already using DeepSeek. A small evaluation should use public, preselected questions and measure source coverage, citation validity, empty-result causes, tail latency and total cost per acceptable answer. Include cases where the target page denies access; success should mean an honest incomplete answer, not invented evidence. Reject the hypothesis if fewer suppliers fail to reduce maintenance or missing content causes more manual work. The useful design lesson is to show search, reading and synthesis as distinct states, with source inspection and stop controls. This is an editorial recommendation, not a claim that those controls ship today.

Make the task budget explicit. Define a maximum number of retrieval rounds and page reads, plus the condition for returning the unresolved question to the user. The actual limits should follow the team's measurements; an arbitrary universal budget would be misleading. A cost report should distinguish discovery, reading and generation so that a cheap call does not disguise an expensive completed workflow. For a small team, the strongest comparison may be which approach makes missing evidence visible and reduces rework, rather than which engine returns the longest list.

Limits and unknowns

The useful studio workflow is feedback to reproducible case, then domain review, then an approved regression example. Make it clear whether the reviewer is confirming a label, correcting an output or authorizing inclusion in an evaluation set. Those actions have different consequences. A sample with unclear origin, unhandled personal information or uncertain reuse rights should remain pending rather than enter a permanent dataset automatically because it exposed an error. This is a proposed governance requirement, not a claim about a specific product setting.

Product verdict

Editorial analysis: evaluate the product against a complete job, such as producing an approved, reproducible dataset from a known family of failures. The alternative is the existing collection of retrieval, annotation and experiment tools, connected by scripts and human handoffs. Consolidation is valuable only if it removes enough coordination work to justify migration, configuration and specialist-review costs.

A practical comparison would reuse historical failure cases and hold the quality threshold constant. Measure retrieval time, label-error rate, reviewer minutes, cost per accepted example and downstream model performance. Audit whether investigators can recover the exact data revision behind a result. In UI review, prioritize who owns the next step, why a sample was rejected, whether a background operation failed, and what retry will change. Do not let a completed task badge imply a better model. The value hypothesis is fewer lost handoffs and faster reliable iteration; it remains a hypothesis until the team’s own end-to-end measurements confirm it.

A minimal experiment does not require training a model. Take a known set of failures and compare the full cost of managing them in the existing spreadsheet-and-folder setup against a dedicated data platform. Ask another team member to reproduce the issue, identify the current judgment, explain why a sample was returned and determine whether the fix was verified. Count handoffs and maintenance work as well as time spent locating an example. If configuration and administration outweigh coordination savings, lightweight tools may remain the better early-stage choice.

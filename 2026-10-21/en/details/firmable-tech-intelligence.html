Task and evidence

The Firmable help documentation identifies October 15 as the actual replacement-availability date. This belongs in a seven-day lookback; the October 20 public news item cannot reset its launch date. Tech Intelligence connects signals from websites, job advertisements, DNS, and public-service observations to company research, contact discovery, and CRM work. Natural language can produce visible filters, but the result remains an observation to interpret, not a procurement fact. Absence of a detected signal does not prove nonuse. A detected technology also does not establish seats, spending, contracts, or renewal dates.

Workflow and handoffs

The earlier sequence requires a salesperson or researcher to gather technology clues, match them to a company, find relevant contacts, and move the result into CRM. The proposed workflow connects those steps and reduces movement across pages and tools. It supports named technologies, while broad categories or every product from a vendor are not yet supported. Users must inspect the filters generated from a natural-language request instead of assuming they perfectly express the original intent. For sales, the job is narrowing a set of accounts worth verifying. For account research, it is preserving the source and confidence behind a judgment, rather than merely producing a longer list.

Mechanism and limits

Confidence and last-observed dates are important interaction details. An older job advertisement may describe a previous technology choice, and a public website observation may cover only part of an organization. The available source and date therefore need interpretation rather than turning any individual signal into a company-wide fact. Detection accuracy was not independently tested, so this issue cannot rank competitors by accuracy. Reviewers should particularly inspect what happens after export, contact enrichment, and CRM delivery. If only a definitive technology label survives downstream, caution in the original interface cannot prevent subsequent outreach from overstating the evidence.

Adoption and costs

Access and billing conditions also shape adoption. This is an account-wide paid add-on for Teams Pro and Enterprise accounts with at least five users, and it is excluded from the standard trial. The exact add-on price remains unverified. Searching within an account that has access does not consume credits, but MCP contact reveals and the first CRM push involve credits. Free searching therefore does not make the whole prospecting workflow free. Purchasing should account for research, verification, contact selection, delivery, and maintenance together. For a small team, the seat requirement and account-wide charge may determine feasibility before conversational convenience does.

Acceptance and alternatives

Alternatives include BuiltWith-style technology lookup, manual public-source research, and existing sales databases, without sufficient evidence here to declare an accuracy winner. A falsifiable pilot should sample across confidence levels and source types, then manually check false positives, staleness, contact matching, and whether CRM records retain dates and uncertainty. Measure valid research effort removed, not only the number of prospects generated. Incorrect technology claims in outreach can damage credibility and increase downstream cost. Faster creation of verifiable lists with evidence preserved through the workflow would support adoption. More stale or incorrect signals producing more expensive outreach would fail the product case, even if the list becomes larger.

Task and evidence

When developers use several models, much of the hidden cost occurs during handoff: copying a diff, explaining rejected approaches, supplying terminal output and waiting for another assistant to reconstruct the project. Antigravity’s addition of Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 matters if the shared workspace reduces that reconstruction. A longer model menu is not itself the product outcome. Earlier Antigravity and Argon coverage should not be counted again; the new delta is provider choice within the environment and the access rules accompanying it.

Mechanism and limits

The documentation describes switching between turns while preserving workspace state, artifacts and terminal context. That could make an implementation-then-review workflow easier to continue without a blank conversation. However, switching during an active run does not instantly transfer partially completed execution to the new model. The change applies after the current turn finishes or after cancellation. This detail affects how the interface should explain waiting and cancellation, and whether developers correctly understand which model is responsible for a step still in progress.

Workflow and handoffs

The old workflow is concrete: one model produces a change, the developer exports the diff and reconstructs context, and another model reviews it. The new workflow retains those materials inside the task environment and changes the model for subsequent work. What it saves is preparation and context-rebuilding effort. It does not automatically provide two independent expert judgments. Models can share failure patterns or be misled by the same project instructions. A longer review, or a larger number of disagreements, is not evidence of better defect detection.

Adoption and costs

Access and billing are meaningful adoption constraints. Free and Plus do not include these models; eligible paid Pro, Ultra and Enterprise access has separate conditions. Enterprise Claude use requires administrative permission, applicable pay-as-you-go settings and provider terms. It is not simply another name drawing from an existing pooled Gemini allowance. Background coordination may still use Gemini and generate additional consumption. The public documentation establishes these boundaries, but this research did not verify a complete per-model tariff, so it cannot support a fabricated estimate of cost per completed task.

[S90] [S91]

Mechanism and limits

Administrators must also distinguish an individual’s model selection from organization-wide budget, permission and model-availability policies. Seeing a model in one context does not establish that every team has equivalent authorization. The scheduled removal of older 4.6 models on November 2 introduces migration and regression work. Teams depending on stable behavior should retain reproducible tasks and acceptance criteria rather than treating a model’s brand name as a lasting quality guarantee. Preserved workspace context can ease a transition without eliminating the need to validate outcomes.

[S90] [S91]

Acceptance and alternatives

The alternatives are staying with one model, handing work between separate tools, or adding another assistant to an existing code-review process. A falsifiable comparison should record escaped defects, review time consumed by false positives, retries and total usage cost. If switching saves context preparation but produces more unproductive disagreement or repeated edits, the team may not gain overall. The useful claim is that a shared-workspace workflow reduces completion time or defects under the same acceptance standard. More selectable models, by themselves, do not establish either result.

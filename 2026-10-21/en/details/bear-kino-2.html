Task and evidence

Bear Robotics announced KINO-2 at 11:30:31 UTC on October 21, followed by a detailed engineering article twelve seconds later; its recorded update at 13:22:30 UTC also precedes this issue’s cutoff. The substantive change is a workflow joining learned manipulation to mobile robot operations. In an older arrangement, people pick and pack while a mobile robot transports goods, and engineers separately program or tune each manipulation task. KINO-2 proposes demonstration capture, model learning, robot execution and outcome logging as a connected development loop around KR1. Warehouse and industrial teams still need the resulting handling step to fit their order systems, aisle conditions and exception procedures.

Adoption and costs

The demonstrated warehouse sequence starts with an order from a warehouse-management system, picks bottles into a crate, delivers it and returns with an empty crate. Customer proofs of concept include warehouse crates, supermarket returns and industrial work. These are pilot applications, not evidence of broad commercial deployment. Bear’s installed base of roughly 16, 000 robots largely concerns its service-robot business and must not be assigned to KR1 or KINO-2. Pricing, a public software download and unrestricted commercial availability are not disclosed in the inspected originals. The accompanying robot image is vendor-supplied and visually stylized; it is not independent photographic proof that a particular customer workflow is operating.

Control and recovery

The control loop is more informative than the label “physical AI.” Teleoperation and supervised demonstrations capture synchronized cameras, robot state and human actions. Task-conditioning inputs specify the intended object or action. The depicted in-house policy predicts forty steps at ten hertz, a four-second horizon, but executes only the first 0.1-second step before replanning from fresh observations. That limits how far execution proceeds on an aging prediction; it does not eliminate perception errors or establish safe contact. Segmentation-mask conditioning is planned rather than already implemented. Cosmos can restyle demonstration video while retaining timing and action labels, but Bear does not quantify a performance gain from that augmentation.

Mechanism and limits

Several reported numbers concern different experimental layers and must remain separate. A simulation example uses 1, 024KR1 instances and improves pick time from 7.4 to 3.7 seconds during one training run, using simulator-provided states. It is not measured camera-based throughput on a physical warehouse robot. On the runtime side, Isaac OS, Holoscan 5.0, TensorRT and Jetson AGX Thor reduce the in-house model’s reported execution time from 72.3 to 17.2 milliseconds against PyTorch eager execution. The detailed GR00T 1.7 result is 56.6 milliseconds median GPU execution per action chunk, compared with 92.2 milliseconds in an earlier four-engine build. Neither number is an end-to-end camera-to-completed-order measurement.

Control and recovery

The launch article’s approximately 500-to-60-millisecond comparison starts from an initial unoptimized deployment, a different baseline from the engineering comparison. Reported commanded jerk falls from 237 to 33 metres per second cubed after execution changes; that measures command smoothness, not injury risk or object damage. Failure recovery remains an engineering process: inspect interventions and outcomes, collect targeted demonstrations, retrain or change execution, then evaluate again. The originals do not specify a complete operator emergency-stop interface, safe retry policy, maximum retries or customer task-success rate. Those omissions matter because a stuck or misidentified object can interrupt the surrounding warehouse workflow.

Control and recovery

The relevant alternatives are manual picking plus existing mobile transport, fixed-arm cells with fixtures, and other mobile manipulators. KINO-2 is attractive if demonstrations reduce changeover work while the integrated runtime preserves reliable handling under real customer variation. Evaluate correctly completed orders, wrong or dropped objects, interventions per hundred orders, recovery time and engineering effort for unfamiliar stock-keeping units. Also separate improvements caused by a faster model from improvements caused by a more constrained task or better demonstration data. If inference latency falls while exceptions still require frequent expert intervention, the technology has improved without yet establishing a compelling operational return.

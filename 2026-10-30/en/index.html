Themes summarize classmates' comments; opening one reveals the original passage and reply links so students can enter the relevant thread. Original comments remain available behind each theme, letting students check the summary against the exchange before composing a reply.

Opening the editor produces questions about understanding, challenging and connecting ideas, grounded in the passage and nearby comments, without supplying answers.

The Hypothesis-based prototype was tried by five people. One missed the overview entirely, and prior knowledge shaped the value of questions.

Product perspective

Course logs motivate the design, but do not establish improved comprehension from the new interface. A classroom deployment remains planned.

HAIDL-X records the code, errors and feedback around notebook assistance

HAIDL-X brings assistance into JupyterLab, keeping questions in the notebook while recording the code and execution state behind them.

The frontend captures cells, outputs and errors. The backend constructs context, calls a model and stores the response, feedback and provenance together.

Records connect by student, assignment and time, showing what happened around help requests rather than merely counting messages. Notebook execution evidence also covers work without assistant requests, preventing analysis from observing only people who actively asked questions.

Authors captured about 800 interactions across 35 students and four assignments, retaining cases where observed use differed from assigned access.

Product perspective

As-If Machine lets people co-write hypothetical experiences one suggestion at a time

As-If Machine is a research writing interface where participants imagine an event in their own lives and decide whether to accept each suggestion.

An intake form informs a story opening. Pausing produces a short continuation; Tab accepts it, while users can keep writing or revise. Researchers configure the intake questions and scenarios used to create the displayed opening.

Four agents handle onset, consequences, plot and completion; instructions constrain the completer from assigning participants thoughts or feelings.

Two preregistered studies involved 1,007 and 894 people. Perceived closeness increased, while attitude effects differed by topic.

Product perspective

Measures were immediate and do not establish lasting behavior change. Rejecting a sentence does not give participants control over the predefined scenario.

A landing tutor adjusts shared control and feedback using recent practice

This research prototype teaches two-dimensional landing with a throttle and joystick, choosing shared control and the tone of feedback after each attempt.

Users report confidence and workload. The system combines these with trajectory and landing scores, then revises teaching priorities at phase boundaries. Reports concern the same attempt that produced the measured score and landing outcome.

Priorities cover confidence calibration, workload and skill progression, allowing different learners to receive different policies instead of one fixed objective.

Forty participants trained, then completed five manual trials without feedback. There was no randomized static-policy control group.

Product perspective

Associations between priorities and scores do not prove personalization caused improvement. Desktop results cannot establish effectiveness for real flight training.

Zwift Plus reshuffles training around availability, with gaps in activity selection

Plan around the time actually available

A training calendar becomes less useful when one missed ride leaves the following days out of step. Zwift Coach asks for experience, goals and availability in the Companion app, then presents the day’s session in the app and game home screen. The official page says indoor rides, outdoor rides and wearable information contribute context, with the plan changing after rest, rescheduling or missed sessions. The input is therefore a real calendar as well as an ambition. The output is an activity the rider can start and feedback afterward. This shortens the handoff from a separate plan to finding the corresponding workout, while the rider still assesses how the session feels and performs the effort.

Training load and its explanation are separate

Eric Schlange at Zwift Insider describes four components after several weeks of beta use: coaching knowledge, planning, activity matching and language. In that account, the plan establishes load and intensity targets, content selection finds a suitable session, and the language component explains the guidance. This is his reported product description, not an independent code audit. It matters because fluent feedback may explain a decision made elsewhere in the system. A rider can use that explanation to understand the day’s session without treating polished wording as proof that the load is personally appropriate. The reviewed material does not establish a controlled long-term improvement in outcomes for every type of rider.

Filmed scenery still follows the ride

Zwift Video is the other part of the tier: 21 initial routes filmed in real locations, while familiar progression, PowerUps and leaderboards remain. It gives an existing indoor setup another visual environment rather than introducing a new required sensor. Linking recorded scenery to riding speed also creates a different kind of experience. A real road’s gradient may look less dramatic than a stylized game environment, and recorded vehicles are scenery rather than opponents that can be caught. Those are useful distinctions when interpreting feedback during a ride. More destinations are planned, but the initial selection should be judged as the currently available collection rather than a completed future library.

The reported weakness is activity matching

Schlange identifies activity matching as the current weakness: Coach does not select specific Zwift events, which riders must insert themselves, and some free-ride routes do not fit the target or his preferences. He separates that limitation from his positive experience with planning and feedback. Alternative sessions, deeper preferences and conversational adjustments are listed as future improvements rather than current controls. Subscription access is another practical boundary: upgrade trials can differ by account and payment route, so one US account’s offer is not a universal entitlement. A rider considering continued use should check the actual upgrade screen and whether the plan fits the coming week, rather than relying on the launch headline alone.

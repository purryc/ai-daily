StableGen edits selected 3D regions, rigs the mesh and exports baked assets

Camera projection becomes a local editing tool

StableGen sends several camera views of a mesh through image generation, then projects the resulting images back onto the geometry. Projection weights depend on surface orientation; hidden and out-of-frame areas do not simply receive that view’s pixels, and overlapping contributions are blended. The user can adjust cameras and reproject existing images without requesting another generation. Version 0.4.0 also gathers operations into a redesigned panel with separate texture, camera, blending, mesh and tool sections. This produces an editable material setup, with visible seams and hidden surfaces still requiring inspection rather than treating one attractive viewpoint as a finished asset.

Paint the region, then decide whether to keep it

Masked Edit starts from selected meshes or all visible meshes. The user paints white on the surface to change, leaves other areas black and describes the edit. A temporary joined working copy can be used while the original objects remain separate. Existing cameras are ranked by how much of the mask they see; an area unseen by those cameras stays unchanged. The result is previewed before Apply or Discard, and discarding restores the original data. Different views can still interpret the requested change differently, so that review has a practical purpose. The documented workflow also requires one material per mesh and an available UV slot.

A generated rig still needs animation work

A character can begin as an existing mesh or move from a prompt through candidate images and TRELLIS.2 generation. The new SkinTokens route sends the mesh to a ComfyUI server, receives an armature and weights, and imports them into Blender. Keeping the original object is the default, making comparison possible. Users can then import FBX or BVH motion and retarget it using a bone map and reference pose. Automatic rigging reduces the initial setup, but does not establish that fingers, joints or clothing deform correctly. The guide explicitly leaves mappings open to inspection and correction before the character is used in another application.

[S45]

Bake the materials before handing off the asset

Projection materials are not the same as ordinary texture maps that an engine can consume. The export guide requires baking onto UV textures before GLB, glTF or FBX export; the export action does not do that baking itself. In the community demonstration, the creator uses ten cameras and an RTX 4080 with 16 GB of memory, exports props into Unreal and labels time-lapse portions. This is a creator demonstration, not an independent speed comparison. The add-on is GPL-licensed, while model terms differ: Qwen Image 2.1 and FIBO 2.0 Edit are listed for noncommercial use. Blender compatibility also needs attention, with version 5.0 specifically excluded.

Composition has its own processing stage

Preparing landscape and portrait versions of an event poster requires more than two attractive images. A size change can disturb the relationship between headline, subject and empty space, while the wording must remain complete. The processing stages described on the main page suggest a concrete comparison: which constraints survive a changed format, and which must be explained again? For brand material, compare several versions of the same brief rather than selecting attractive examples from unrelated prompts. Repeated revisions expose composition control more clearly than a single successful draft.

Generation and decomposition are separate operations

Magic Layers is an existing editing operation, already described in the October 8 tutorial. The help page gives a concrete path: select an image, open editing, run Magic Layers, then change individual elements. Single-page PNG and JPG are the principal inputs; PDF requires conversion, and complex photographs may separate poorly. These distinctions matter when choosing a workflow. A design with its original editable source is usually better kept that way than flattened and reconstructed. Decomposition is more relevant when only a flat image remains and a local revision is needed.

Planning information does not guarantee reconstruction

Canva’s research material connects image interpretation with editable structure. In the product, the resulting objects still need inspection. A selectable text element can contain an error, and repairing a background can affect nearby material. Selection is the beginning of revision, not evidence that the reconstruction is faithful. A practical comparison would keep one event graphic and change its date, move a logo and replace the background separately, checking collateral changes after each step. If these corrections require substantial re-layout, generation followed by decomposition may take more work than starting with an editable template.

Available entry points and future promises

Individual creators and teams embedding generation in their own tools face different adoption costs. One needs a revision that can be exported; the other also needs failures returned to the right task and a clear owner for final proofreading. Exact quotas and generation prices remain unconfirmed here. Follow a revision-heavy job through first draft, local correction and delivery, counting the manual work at each stage before replacing a template workflow. If changing a date repeatedly requires rebuilding the layout, speed at the first draft cannot remove the later work.

Search starts with remembered content

People often remember what a document discussed before recalling its filename or folder. The retrieval sequence on the main page fits that starting point, but semantic relevance and the correct version remain separate decisions. A draft, a signed replacement and an expired attachment may contain nearly identical passages. Dates, surrounding context and relationships between files can matter more than a plausible summary. If someone still compares every attachment after opening the results, the saving concerns keyword guessing rather than the entire retrieval task.

Separate the model claim from the result

The maker describes bidirectional late-interaction embeddings as a way to retain detailed matching and surrounding context. A promotional hit-rate claim appears alongside that explanation, but the inspected material does not provide a reproducible dataset and protocol sufficient for adopting it as a measured accuracy result. More revealing checks are whether a paraphrase retrieves the same material and whether irrelevant wording promotes the wrong file. Neither a model label nor a carefully chosen demonstration answers those questions. The approach is a product mechanism to investigate, not a probability of success across every file format or language.

Offline search still has setup work

Adoption starts with choosing a useful collection rather than assuming the whole disk belongs in it. Personal files and organizational material carry different obligations: shared-directory permissions, departing staff and device management need separate answers. The enterprise page discusses deployment and evaluation, which should not be projected onto the consumer app. Someone with a reliable folder and keyword system may gain little from maintaining another index. For older material with inconsistent names, the useful question is which part of recovery becomes easier and which checking remains.

[S07]

Test against files whose answers are known

Start with a small collection of documents, photos and scans whose locations are already known, then query them using descriptions a person might remember later. Record misses, confused versions and the checking still required after opening a result. Move or replace a file and check whether retrieval reflects that change. This is a modest test of someone’s own information collection, rather than a market experiment or a speed contest. The October 23 Product Hunt listing provides a dated discovery point. It does not establish retention, audience size or willingness to buy an enterprise deployment.

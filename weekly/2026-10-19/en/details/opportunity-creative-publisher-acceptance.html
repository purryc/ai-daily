Migration ends when the next issue can be edited

An April Microsoft Q&A contains a church-bulletin workflow and a separate concern about newsletter text boxes and columns changing in Word. These are historical users, not newly identified buyers this week. On October 20, Saint Systems reported recent inquiries about files that could no longer be opened or edited. That is a vendor account without independently verified volume. The recognizable job is preserving the next editing cycle, not simply retaining old files.

Existing tools may already complete the job

Microsoft distinguishes loss of subscription access from the end of perpetual-version support; perpetual installations can remain usable. It warns that PDF-to-Word conversion can alter layout. Markzware's October 23 guide covers editable Canva, IDML and local batch routes, while PubKeep advertises direct PUB opening and a trial. These are strong counterarguments to a new service. If representative files open and edit correctly, extra migration work is unnecessary. Their conversion quality was not tested here.

Serve only the inspection work that remains

The proposed narrow service would accept authorized originals, a visual reference and fields needed for the next edition, then return an editable template and exception list. OCR could help locate missing text; visual comparison could flag displacement. People would still judge dates, reading order, substituted fonts and print bleed. This does not promise reconstruction of every original structure. Measure the existing page-by-page review time first: a convincing comparison image is not proof of a usable editing workflow.

Visible discussion is not a paying customer list

One multiformat conversion listing and five visible reviews were read. An explicitly described job involved Canva-to-Illustrator work; none established a Publisher-specific transaction. This supports adjacent service activity, not a price for this niche. Potential channels would be consenting print-service partners or newsletter maintainers, not an assumed contact list extracted from a forum. Distinct qualified buyers, budgets, repeat purchases and reachable proportions are unknown. Multiplying post counts by a standard conversion rate would manufacture a market estimate.

[S67]

Use twelve pages to find reasons not to sell it

A smallest test could use three owned or synthetic templates totaling twelve pages, capped at six hours with no service purchases. Seed missing text, changed dates, overflow and font substitution. Compare manual inspection with an assisted exception list. A proposed continuation rule is finding every critical error while reducing repair plus review time by at least a quarter. That threshold is a test choice, not an observed result. Stop if native tools already work, false alarms consume the saving, authorized samples are unavailable or editability cannot be preserved.

Repair time can erase the margin

For an illustrative unit calculation only, assume CAD150 for a small batch, CAD10 in direct tooling and model cost, and labor valued at CAD40 per hour. Smooth, ordinary and difficult cases requiring one, two and four hours leave CAD100, CAD60 and negative CAD20 respectively. These are assumptions, not quoted prices or revenue forecasts. Acquisition, refunds, platform fees, tax and later support remain uncounted. Repeated font repairs or monthly layout changes would require new timing rather than treating the first delivery as recurring profit.

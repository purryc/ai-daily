Place cameras to fit the machine

Camera placement often follows the machine’s mechanics before it follows a convenient off-the-shelf enclosure. OAK 4 FFC combines five camera connections, compute and vision software while allowing supported modules to be positioned around the machine. It can run a simpler application or supply depth, detections and tracks to a central computer responsible for planning and action. The proposed saving concerns integration work across drivers, synchronization and data movement. It is not a measured reduction in engineering hours. A team still needs to compare the platform with its own existing capture and processing stack rather than translate the vendor’s description of complexity into a project return.

Lens parameters are not rig geometry

Factory lens calibration travels with each module, but mounting cameras on a robot creates another geometry problem. Luxonis describes entering positions and angles in OAK Viewer, then moving the rig slowly so common scene features refine overlapping views. A camera with no overlap retains the entered position; a moved mount requires recalibration. Five synchronized images consequently do not establish all-around depth or one fused point cloud. A proposed acceptance check moves a known target before and after mounting and compares spatial consistency. Non-overlapping views need their own error accounting. A convincing preview from one stereo pair is insufficient evidence for the geometry of the complete machine.

Five ports share one processing platform

The datasheet lists a six-core CPU, Linux, five MIPI interfaces, inertial sensing and a magnetometer. Images feed the visual pipeline; applications may use motion and orientation signals. A parts list does not establish a complete, validated navigation system. Developers must budget resolution, frame rate and model load across simultaneously operating cameras. The platform requires OAK-FFC V2 modules rather than assuming compatibility with older RVC2 modules. Suggested tests include low texture, changing light, repeated calibration and sustained operation. These reveal whether the complete pipeline supplies useful perception under the intended conditions, something a peak compute figure cannot determine by itself.

Separate prototype pricing from delivery status

The store currently lists the developer kit at US$849, with camera modules separate and a temporary memory/storage surcharge included. The announcement and current page differ slightly on barebone pricing, so the older figure should not anchor a procurement total. The October 27 announcement planned November kit shipping and Q1 2027 barebones; the November 2 product page says kits ship now without dating that change. The datasheet still identifies prototypes whose final hardware may differ. Lab evaluation can begin from those configurations, while production requires confirmation of revisions, modules, cooling and power. The previously restricted reference-design target was not retried, and no license suitability is inferred.

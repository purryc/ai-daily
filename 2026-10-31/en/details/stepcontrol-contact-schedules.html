Specify touchdown and lift-off

Making a character slow down, pause and resume requires precise timing that can be awkward to express through repeated text prompts or frame-by-frame pose editing. StepControl uses two temporal tracks describing whether the left and right feet touch the ground, together with a short observed motion prefix. The model generates subsequent full-body poses, movement and stride patterns without requiring future joint positions, target keyframes or semantic prompts. The paper demonstrates cadence changes, a two-second pause, asymmetric support and synchronized two-foot flight and landing. These are programmable control signals, rather than a demonstrated consumer editing application. Pressure, inertial and acoustic sensors are discussed as possible future sources of contact information; they are not integrated hardware features established by this study.

Preparing for the next contact

A binary state alone makes brief touchdown changes easy for a generative model to overlook. The authors expand contact states into event features that distinguish touchdown, lift-off and transition direction, then feed them through dedicated cross-attention. A moving window contains ten clean history frames and twenty progressively refined future frames. More distant frames remain noisier until they approach the next commitment point. This gives the model advance notice of a coming contact, allowing preceding body movement to adjust. Training begins with HumanML3D and then uses LAFAN1 to sharpen timing. The reported long-horizon evaluation reaches twenty seconds. The software can keep rolling forward, but these results do not establish performance over several minutes or across arbitrary terrain.

Better timing is not perfect contact

Evaluation uses held-out LAFAN1 subject-five sequences, with earlier subjects used for training and validation. The comparisons adapt DART and ARDY to contact constraints. At twenty seconds, StepControl reports contact-transition F1 of 42.27 percent, versus 14.60 and 15.98 percent. This score requires exact agreement at transition frames; it is not a percentage of usable videos. Raw stance-foot sliding is 14.62 millimetres per frame, compared with 2.41 for DART and 7.83 for ARDY. A planar correction reduces StepControl’s value to 7.75, but that is additional post-processing rather than a structural physical guarantee. Residual sliding, occasional floating and unseen single-foot hopping remain limitations. A timing advantage therefore should not be described as an across-the-board improvement in animation quality.

Throughput and editing readiness differ

The appendix reports roughly 7.5 milliseconds per frame and 2.9 seconds for a twenty-second rollout on a single RTX 4090. This is model throughput under the stated inference setup, not total authoring time including preparation, editing, rendering or export. No animator study establishes learning effort, revision counts or finished-film quality. Contact timing also does not establish control over direction, facial expression or scene interaction. For prototype builders, the contribution is an explicit rhythm input that could complement spatial-path controls. An editor would still need to decide how to display contact tracks, reveal sliding, accept corrections and preserve completed motion. The paper identifies WACV 2027 and should be read as research material, without a commercial delivery claim.

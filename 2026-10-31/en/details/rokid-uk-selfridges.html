A place to assess the wearable interface

Rokid’s October 30 announcement places a permanent demonstration display inside the Selfridges London Tech Bar, in distributor-operated retail space. For eyewear, that changes the evaluation process: a shopper can assess the frame, visible text and optical fit before choosing a device. This is a channel expansion, separate from the Glasses2 hardware story. It is not evidence of availability at every UK retailer. The official product page also describes an in-store counter and advises confirming demonstration models and stock before visiting. A reachable trial location is useful, while a brief store session still cannot establish comfort over a full day or performance in the conditions of a real trip.

Captions and visual questions share the frame

The UK product page describes the original Rokid Glasses combining voice input, capture and a binocular monochrome display. A wearer wakes the assistant with “Hi Rokid,” asks a question or captures an object, and can follow up on the response. Translation and prompting also appear in view. The documented microphone array, camera and Micro-LED waveguide explain the input and output path; this is not a camera-free caption device. Reducing repeated phone retrieval is a plausible travel use, but the intended language, surrounding noise and task still matter. The retail announcement adds no new sensor and provides no independent measurement that turns these existing capabilities into guaranteed outcomes.

The bundle groups travel accessories

The Travel Set groups clip-on sunglass lenses, an audio concentrator, a charging cable and a pouch; the announcement also lists a charging-case gift. Its contribution is mainly packaging the accessories needed away from home, rather than adding another model or sensing capability. For a wearer, those choices affect what must be carried and purchased separately. Their inclusion does not by itself quantify longer runtime or improved voice capture. The UK store also sells a non-display Style line with different accessories. That makes exact product identification important: similarly named packages should not be compared as though they contain the same hardware. The practical purchasing question is which glasses and compatible items the selected bundle actually includes.

Announcement terms and live orders differ

The release lists a £699 bundle and a planned one-day £569 promotion for October 31. At observation, the Travel Set page instead shows £579 preorder pricing and November 5 as first shipping. Neither figure should silently replace the other, and today’s page should not be projected backward into a universal announcement-time status. The product page also notes that bundle items may arrive separately. The clear increment is the physical trial opportunity and UK sales route. Whether a customer can take a unit home, when an online package arrives and which promotion applies remain channel-specific questions. This improves access to evaluation without resolving every purchasing or delivery uncertainty.

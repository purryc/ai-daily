Scene building becomes an agent task

Building a pouring, welding or cloth-folding environment takes more than attractive assets: it needs contact behavior, material dynamics and a definition of completion. Gym-Anything assigns that construction work to coding agents. Occupational tasks, economic data and everyday activities guide the selection of physical capabilities. The authors select 120 action groups and implement twenty-five simulations in this paper. RoboWorld turns them into twenty-five test tasks and 175 training environments. Robots, object poses, appearance and physical parameters can vary, but those variations are not counted as additional tasks. The October 30 arXiv listing dates this supplement; it does not mark new abilities deployed on physical robots.

An observation-and-command interface

Restricted evaluation provides an instruction, camera images, robot feedback such as joint positions, and documentation for permitted commands. The agent issues joint targets, end-effector movements, gripper actions or other supported controls. Physics advances during commands; deliberation consumes wall-clock time without moving the scene. A verifier subsequently scores the physical state rather than accepting the agent’s own completion claim. A whiteboard task checks ink left on the board, while a filleting task checks the actual placement of meat and frame. Privileged access additionally exposes object poses, contact forces, material states and evaluation information. That richer channel supports teaching and training, and must remain distinct from the restricted benchmark interface.

What the zero-completion result measures

GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Terra and Claude Opus 5.5 run through their respective coding-agent harnesses with extra-high reasoning effort and three-hour attempts. They receive no reward during an attempt. None completes the twenty-five test tasks. On a separate easier subset of twenty-five training tasks, completion is 24%, 12% and 32%, respectively. This characterizes a difficult interface and task collection, not an inability to perform every physical task. Separate learning experiments let a privileged teacher inspect a student’s scene and provide qualitative hints. Code policies also produce demonstrations for π0.5: on two simulated gift-box tasks, success rises from zero to 53%, reaching 55% under unseen scene variations.

Rendered realism still needs physical validation

Post-generation audits check dynamics, task solvability and scoring loopholes. The authors report that adversarial legal robot actions broke roughly three quarters of initial simulations, including a ball launched by an abnormal turf interaction; repairs were checked by replaying the counterexamples. The paper’s visual and physics comparisons use a model judge, not external laboratory certification. Some solvability evidence permits phantom assisting forces: it establishes that an interaction can occur, not that a restricted robot policy can complete the whole task independently. Physical-robot transfer is unmeasured, and economic weighting is not a revenue forecast. The research environment remains dependent on whether its dynamics and verifiers represent the intended skill.

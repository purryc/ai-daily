An action needs a location

Recognizing a cup does not establish where a hand should touch it to pick it up. EgoContact narrows that problem into an input and output that different methods can share: a first-person image before contact and an intended action go in; candidate contact coordinates come out. Those predictions are compared with the human contact observed later in the video. The authors describe roughly 22,500 manually verified events, with examples such as picking up a knife or closing a faucet on their project page. This isolates spatial choice before manipulation without asking the model to produce an entire hand pose, obstacle-avoidance trajectory and controller at the same time.

Finding the object is a separate hurdle

The evaluation distinguishes whether a prediction falls inside the target object, how far it is from the reference contact, and whether it meets tolerances of one or five percent of the image diagonal. Top-1 and Top-5 measure the best match among one or five ranked alternatives for the same contact; they are not five fingers to place simultaneously. A generous tolerance can make a central point look adequate while hiding a precision problem. Conversely, some methods miss the target object before contact reasoning can help. For someone choosing a perception component, the useful reading is therefore a combination of object localization and strict contact accuracy rather than one permissive score.

A memory-bank swap guides grasp selection

The authors then replace the memory bank in the existing RAM affordance-transfer method. An object observation and a pick-up instruction retrieve a related interaction; its contact is transferred to the target, projected into a point cloud and used to select a nearby grasp proposal. In Isaac Sim, success across 346 objects rises from 14.2% to 31.2%, with a grasp required to hold under gravity for at least three seconds. Physical tests use a Franka Panda, an RGB-D camera and AnyGrasp proposals. Ten trials on each of five objects yield eight successful grasps with the original memory and twenty-nine with EgoContact. This measures the whole memory-bank replacement, including coverage and annotations.

A contact hint does not finish the task

The contribution reduces dependence on full robot trials when evaluating contact selection, and supplies useful retrieval material for an existing grasping pipeline. A two-dimensional point still leaves depth interpretation, grasp proposals, collision handling and execution feedback to other components. Results on five physical objects do not establish transfer to every tool or material. The author page labels both code and data coming soon, so a readable paper and example gallery do not establish an available complete download.

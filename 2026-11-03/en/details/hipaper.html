Make the reading set explicit

Literature search, personal collections and questions to an assistant often live in separate places. HiPaper lets researchers revise keywords, inspect clusters and collect papers into named knowledge pockets before requesting synthesis. Its abstract emphasizes editable intermediate artifacts across these stages. The product question is how clearly people can see and change the selected scope. A newcomer might use the collection as a reading queue, while an experienced researcher may already know which terminology is missing. Keyword suggestions should remain easy to reject. A coherent collection is not necessarily a representative collection, particularly when relevant work uses unfamiliar vocabulary.

Linked excerpts have a content boundary

The interface links generated comparisons and graph relationships to explanations and evidence. However, the paper limits its corpus mainly to recent computing venues and abstract-level content. Abstracts help assess relevance but often omit experimental conditions and failure cases. A linked excerpt shows where a statement came from; it does not ensure that the available text supports the inference. A useful reading workflow should make this boundary visible and preserve a direct route to the complete paper. Otherwise the convenience of a grounded-looking answer can encourage a stronger conclusion than the underlying material warrants.

The study does not establish better reviews

Five formative participants informed the prototype, followed by a study with fourteen computing doctoral students. The evaluation lacked a competing-tool control, did not assess final research quality and lost three sets of interaction logs. Its observations can guide interface design, but cannot establish faster or more accurate reviews than conventional search. A follow-up comparison could use the same research question and assess missing key papers, mistaken method descriptions and verification time. Satisfaction with clustering or chat is a separate outcome. It should not substitute for evidence that the final account represents the literature more faithfully.

Challenge the collection with a counterexample

A practical trial can include a supporting paper, a contradictory paper and an irrelevant paper with a similar title. Inspect grouping and comparison, remove one item, and check whether later answers still rely on it. Reopening a saved collection should preserve scope and citation identity. A reference manager, spreadsheet and separate assistant remain useful alternatives because their evidence boundaries can be explicit. HiPaper is a research prototype without confirmed pricing or longitudinal outcomes. Its adoption case depends on reducing organizational effort while preserving the researcher’s ability and willingness to read the complete source and challenge an appealing summary. The collection itself can become a useful review artifact if another researcher can inspect its membership and understand exclusions. That requires preserving the selection decisions, not merely keeping a polished answer whose source set is hard to reconstruct.

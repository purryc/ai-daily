Give an existing text agent speech input and output

CascadeLive separates speech recognition, text reasoning and speech synthesis into replaceable stages. Recognition emits live captions and a completed-turn signal; a normal text model receives the completed transcript and uses its existing instructions and tools; sentence or phrase segments are synthesized into audio. Developers can reuse an existing text agent without rewriting its tools around a dedicated end-to-end speech model. Version 2.13.0 adds Gemini recognition and synthesis implementations and interruption handling. Non-live calls go directly to the inner text model, bypassing both speech transforms. One agent definition can therefore serve typing and speaking, while the modes retain different latency and failure characteristics.

An interruption must stop audio and correct later context

Speech synthesis often runs ahead of the speaker, leaving generated sentences waiting in a playback queue. When the user interrupts, the connection aborts reasoning, signals cancellation to synthesis and tells the client to stop local playback. It then compares elapsed time with the audio timeline, retaining fully played sentences and only complete words from the interrupted sentence, while discarding unplayed text. Subsequent reasoning avoids assuming that the listener heard the rest. This remains a timing-based estimate rather than a measurement at the listener’s ear. Network buffering and device latency can affect the relationship between synthesis, client playback and the conversation history the next turn receives.

Inspectable text comes with serial latency

Exposing text between reasoning and synthesis lets an application inspect or redact it before speech, and swap recognition or voice providers. Recognition, reasoning, sentence buffering and synthesis also add serial delay; model thinking postpones the first audio further. A short silence threshold can cut off a person pausing mid-sentence, while a long threshold creates waiting. Open speakers require echo cancellation to avoid transcribing the agent’s own voice as an interruption. The documentation identifies a stronger limit: if a new utterance finishes while a tool call is pending, message ordering can break that turn and later turns in the session. Interruption support therefore does not establish seamless conversation across every tool workflow.

Ticket routing checks labels against graph edges

The experimental ClassifierNode addresses another recurring construction task: deciding whether a ticket belongs to billing or technical support and forwarding it accordingly. Developers define a Choice or Binary question. The node passes the input and question to a classifier, uses the answer as the route and forwards the original input unchanged. Graph construction checks misspelled routes and labels without destinations, while invalid answers raise an explicit error. TypeSafe Jev is one available classifier; its single-pass speed claim should not be generalized to every task. The node removes label-parsing glue, while the team still defines classification criteria, boundary examples and the fallback for uncertain or incomplete requests.

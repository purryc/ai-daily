A robot to drive before rewriting its behavior

Microduck serves people who want to put learned behavior on physical hardware. A gamepad also lets an owner walk, sit or try the grasping beak before writing control code. The roughly 25-centimeter body uses fifteen motors, with separate camera, inertial and ranging inputs. Its repository separates policy training from execution: MuJoCo and PPO produce an ONNX policy for the onboard controller. Changing movement therefore involves a runnable policy and its control interface, rather than simply asking a general chatbot to learn walking. For a hobbyist, that makes the simulation-to-hardware handoff visible and gives debugging a concrete object: the same requested motion can be compared in both environments.

Position starts with a foot on the floor

Without wheels, the robot cannot count wheel rotations to estimate distance. Joint angles and body orientation instead locate the trunk relative to a point touching the floor. The inertial unit supplies orientation rather than an endlessly integrated acceleration estimate. Because a foot rolls as it steps, the implementation considers multiple sole points and filters momentary contact glitches. Sliding still accumulates error, especially during sharp turns. This distinction matters when designing play: contact odometry can explain the recent walk, but carrying the robot breaks its assumption that a foot anchors it to the floor. Recovery after a person moves it needs information about the surroundings.

Stopping supplies another view of the room

A head-mounted VL53L5CX reduces the nearby scene to 64 distances. During a stop, several observations are combined and compared with an obstacle map; while walking, the system follows leg odometry. A wall alone is ambiguous because several places along it can look alike. Recovery after a carry consequently needs supporting observations from separate stops. In a play session, this could let somebody move the duck from a table to a sofa without leaving its room position attached to the old path. The corner camera and floor markers shown in the engineering setup measure error; they are not external tracking equipment required by the robot.

Knowing one room is not whole-home navigation

The map currently covers one room. The author reports a false match in an unfamiliar room, while autonomous exploration remains unfinished. Developers can use the public recordings to examine sensor inputs and reference positions instead of judging only a selected walking clip. A household buying a finished play companion faces different questions: shipping and the supported behaviors matter before customization. The official press kit dates preorders to August 27, gives an introductory US$399 price before tax and shipping, and targets first deliveries before Christmas. That is a delivery target, not verified general shipment. The open-source claim covers software, excluding mechanical and electronic design files. Availability is still conditional.

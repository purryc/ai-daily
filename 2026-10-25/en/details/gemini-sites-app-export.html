An app with a place for its instructions

An interactive quiz inside a conversation still needs a usable destination. Its audience may need instructions, input units, an explanation of the answers or the origin of a dashboard’s numbers. The export opens a private Sites draft where the author can place that context around the app before sharing. A teacher can distribute a page instead of asking students to locate a conversation; a small team can give a lightweight tool a short operating guide. This is a publishing handoff for an existing Gemini app. It does not establish a database service, an identity system or migration of arbitrary websites. The original announcement is dated October 19; subsequent news coverage does not reset that date.

Drafting and granting access are separate steps

Sites already separates preview, publication and audience selection. Its help page describes previewing layouts, choosing a web address and publishing, then checking who can view the published site. Personal accounts can choose restricted or public access; work and school accounts may offer other choices. For a small trial, the relevant check is the finished site’s audience setting. A draft having started private does not answer who can view the published version. Asking search engines not to list a page is neither guaranteed exclusion nor access control. These are existing Sites controls, not new protections introduced by Gemini export. The author still needs to open the resulting address and inspect what a reader receives.

The second edit raises the maintenance question

The visible r/Bard discussion contains the announcement repost and four other comments. Two different commenters ask closely related questions: whether later Gemini edits synchronize automatically, and whether another export can update the same page. These are questions, not evidence of a failed update. The retrieved page supplies relative ages and no usable vote or total-comment count. For a classroom tool or recurring team dashboard, however, the answer affects upkeep. Creating a fresh page each time would require managing old links and bookmarks; replacing the existing page would require a clear point at which readers receive the revision. The brief official announcement describes a new draft and does not settle either later behavior.

Keep a review step for changes readers will see

For an existing site, Sites offers a side-by-side comparison between the draft and the published version, with annotations for unpublished changes. That review is on by default and can be disabled. With generated content, the pause is useful for checking headings, instructions and units before readers see the revision. It does not inspect the app’s formulas or generated code on the author’s behalf. If a cost calculator gets a new rate, its surrounding worked example should change too; a polished page can otherwise preserve contradictory assumptions. Export reduces the work of placing the app on a website. How subsequent editing returns to Gemini, and how the same public address is preserved, still needs confirmation in use.

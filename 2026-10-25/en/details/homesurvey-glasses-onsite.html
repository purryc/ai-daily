Recording without putting down the clipboard

An onsite moving survey combines looking around with questions about furniture, narrow access and fragile belongings. HomeSurvey’s glasses route records the estimator’s view alongside spoken conditions, leaving hands available to open a cupboard or move a chair. Recognition is not performed entirely inside the glasses. Each room’s clip reaches the paired phone and is then uploaded to the same survey. That remaining step matters: finishing a recording is not the same as updating the estimate. If a room is never uploaded, faster processing cannot recover its contents. For jobs that do not need an onsite visit, the existing customer-recorded phone route remains available.

Keeping an exclusion beside the relevant item

The input includes both furniture imagery and speech such as an instruction to leave a lamp or crate a table. The system drafts an item inventory with estimated volume, weight and handling labels, and maps spoken access details into questionnaire answers. Its public interface shows source imagery beside item information so a reviewer can approve, exclude or adjust the entry without retyping everything. The materials do not disclose a specific model or depth-measurement system, so these video estimates should not be described as instrument measurements. The practical convenience is keeping a customer’s spoken exception beside the recognized item instead of reconstructing that qualification from memory later.

Review happens before the estimate

The Movegistics page places review before a customer estimate: an estimator or sales representative checks the inventory before room details, counts, volume and packing materials feed the lead. Other CRMs can connect through an API, or a team can attach an exported inventory manually; those paths are not the same as the native integration. A useful review starts with unseen areas and spoken exclusions, then checks large objects and access conditions rather than accepting a plausible-looking total. The work removed is repeated entry. Final estimating responsibility remains with a person, and the vendor’s speed and accuracy claims lack enough disclosed sampling detail for independent reproduction here.

The glasses route changes credits and captured material

The official page presents glasses surveys as an IAM October 21–24 addition, while the phone service dates to March. Its introductory package has expired and should not be advertised as still purchasable: afterward, the stated rule uses two credits for glasses footage and one for phone footage, subject to the account’s package. This route also uploads interior scenes, voices and notes. The privacy policy permits processing by service providers and makes retention dependent on contracts and configuration; wearing the capture device does not make processing local. Move-Day Audit remains forthcoming. Willingness to wear glasses and customers’ acceptance of recording also affect adoption for regular onsite estimators.

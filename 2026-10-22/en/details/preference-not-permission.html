Separate suitability from commitment

An agent may correctly infer a preference for wide-toe shoes without knowing whether this particular discount justifies purchasing without confirmation. The paper holds technical credential authority fixed and asks a different question: do the available evidence, reversibility, relative price and urgency justify autonomous commitment? It uses minimally altered fashion and hotel scenarios to compare declining, asking and automatically buying. This makes choosing a suitable item but acting too aggressively a failure in its own right. The submission at 05:15 UTC on October 21 precedes the main window and is included as a three-day supplement.

A population average is not an individual boundary

The authors evaluate 16 instruction models and collect 1,110 labels from 15 annotators across 270 scenarios. Annotators adopt supplied personas, so these are not actual shoppers authorizing transactions based on their own established habits. Two annotators are authors, with a separate robustness analysis excluding them. Shallow models trained on individual labels better match held-out purchase boundaries than shared configurations in this benchmark. That supports collecting explicit choices instead of merely making a prompt more assertive. A cross-domain transfer check also finds substantial degradation, but concerns one cross-domain annotator; it cannot establish how every person’s policy generalizes.

Reduced overreach has a cost

Supplying structured personal policies reduces overreach across all evaluated models, while leaving some purchases that should not have been automatic. More confirmation also accompanies missed opportunities that annotators would have allowed. Evaluation therefore cannot reward either fewer interruptions or inactivity alone. The policy experiment fits rules to the same scenario cells, primarily testing whether models can follow a supplied boundary rather than generalization to unseen people. There is no live payment experiment, longitudinal deployment or test–retest assessment. Data and annotation protocols are planned for release after acceptance. Code execution, cancellation and recovery were not tested here, and refund or rollback behavior cannot be inferred from these results.

[S119]

Recheck when conditions change

A product workflow could recommend an item, expose supporting evidence and transaction conditions, and then use a separate policy to decide whether confirmation is needed. That is our design inference, not a purchase interface delivered by the paper. Users should be able to revise a domain-specific boundary, understand a confirmation request and withdraw an unexecuted decision. Manual shopping, saved carts and per-action approval remain legitimate alternatives. A pilot should separately record unauthorized purchases, missed permitted automation, unnecessary confirmations and recovery time, then change refund terms and actual price. Fewer prompts are not an improvement if they come from silently expanding permission. A useful interface should preserve the distinction between permission to research, permission to prepare a transaction and permission to complete it, even when all three use the same account.

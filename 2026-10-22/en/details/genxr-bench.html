Make embodied action part of software acceptance

A convincing three-dimensional scene is different from an operable XR application. Head and hand motion continually interact with occlusion, rays, coordinates and sensor initialization. GenXR-Bench evaluates this chain through 112 expert-authored tasks: 24 SDK feature implementations, 21 repairs and 67 application-generation tasks. It uses the WebXR framework XR Blocks, enabling headless execution rather than making navigation of a game-engine editor the main variable. For spatial-computing developers, the change is to hold coding agents accountable for interaction constraints instead of successful startup or attractive previews. The paper was submitted at 00:28 UTC on October 21 and is treated as a three-day supplement.

Separate actions from assertions

The tests combine scripted actions with an adaptive actor, then inspect outcomes through programmatic assertions and visual judgments. Pointing a ray is not selection, and a grab requires a complete press, move and release lifecycle. Objects that overlap on screen need not touch in physical space. Held-out acceptance can distinguish those cases instead of trusting an app’s own labels or a fluent completion message. Eight XR experts, blinded to candidate origin and verifier outcomes, assessed 124 applications, with at least one evaluation on Galaxy XR or Quest 3 hardware. Three automated runs per candidate yielded 372 evaluations, reporting 96.5% accuracy against expert judgments and 4.0% flakiness. Neither figure is the coding models’ task success rate.

Failure categories matter beyond a leaderboard

Across ten model configurations, the strongest overall Pass@1 was only 50.9%, with weaker performance on new SDK features. Failure analysis highlights coordinate transforms, asynchronous sensor lifecycles and two-handed semantics. An app can read tracking state before a sensor is ready or treat left- and right-hand events as interchangeable; its static appearance may still look correct. The verifier also has limits when people successfully interact in ways its scripts did not anticipate. The authors explicitly exclude realistic sensor noise, incomplete observations, physiological constraints and accessibility from their idealized simulator. They provide a repository link, but it returned 404 during this check, so complete reproduction cannot be promised.

From automated checks to completion on a device

Existing alternatives are repeated headset testing by a developer and unit tests for computational logic. The first consumes human time; the second misses embodied behavior. This benchmark is better viewed as a regression filter during development than as final user-experience acceptance. A practical comparison should hold requirements and hardware constant, run repeatable assertions, and then introduce lost tracking, occluded hands, different heights and unusual interaction paths. Measure escaped defects and human diagnosis time. If passing applications still require people to adapt their movements to a narrow script, the tests reward conventions rather than task completion. Useful automation should speed diagnosis and improve real-task reliability while making uncovered bodily and environmental conditions explicit.

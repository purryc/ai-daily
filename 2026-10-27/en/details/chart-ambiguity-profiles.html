One pattern can support different takeaways

A chart can contain correct values while encouraging conclusions its author did not anticipate. One reader emphasizes recovery after a low point; another focuses on remaining far below a target. The research starts with the chart image, identifies its title, axes, encodings and interpretive questions, then asks topic-relevant model personas the same question. It produces readings to inspect before revision instead of asking one model to pronounce the design good or bad. For a report author, the useful output is a concrete difference in the time period, group or reference point foregrounded by each interpretation, followed by a decision about whether that difference needs clearer presentation.

Comparing target, pattern and scope

The pipeline retrieves relevant PersonaHub descriptions, selects varied representatives and routes chart interpretation by chart type. Each response retains its overall takeaway and supporting observations, decomposed into target, pattern and scope. Language-model judges compare statements from different personas, separating compatible claims, different but nonconflicting observations, and direct or framing conflicts. An author can inspect the sentences behind a label rather than rely on a score alone. Yet embedding diversity does not establish demographic or ideological coverage, and an apparent disagreement may begin with a model misreading the chart. The number of simulated personas should never be reported as a count of real readers.

What the evaluation actually checks

Nine models were compared on 689 chart-question pairs before statement labeling was studied using 115 human-annotated pairs. Forty supported prompt development and seventy-five strategy evaluation; the selected holistic strategy reached 72 percent agreement on the latter. A final three-model council matched 101 of all 115 pairs, or 87.8 percent; that aggregate is not a fresh held-out score. The case studies cover twenty-four visualizations. Target-pattern-scope extraction and detailed subtypes were not separately validated. Nor does any of this show that the distribution of generated interpretations matches a population of actual viewers. The tested components and the claim about audience understanding therefore need to stay distinct.

Make ambiguity inspectable without erasing disagreement

In an unemployment example, data, personas and question stayed fixed while a reference line, shading and repositioned annotations changed the design. More generated observations then used the same benchmark, but recovery and prolonged shortfall remained competing emphases. That need not indicate a defective chart: public data can support several reasonable readings. In use, an author should revisit the flagged sentences and visible evidence, then ask the intended audience where misunderstanding occurs. Removing inconvenient data to improve agreement would make the communication worse. The method is best treated as a source of review questions; commercial availability, pricing and measured savings in a working design process are not established.

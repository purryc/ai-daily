Controller for HomeKit 10 · Product update · Independent review observation

Controller shares an AI-arranged home dashboard while Apple Home retains device access

Put switches where the household expects them

The person who configured a smart home may know every accessory name, while another household member simply wants the light off or to check a window. SSPAI’s October 26 review observes a device dashboard that AI can rearrange from text and that remains manually editable. The generated result is a control surface whose buttons still need to point to the intended devices. For someone repeatedly explaining menus to relatives, the useful reduction is in finding a room and accessory again. It does not require AI to decide when the household should use its appliances. A prominent control pointing to the wrong room can make a mistake easier rather than making the home simpler.

Draft the layout in language and keep direct editing

The official release says a text request can generate sections and tiles, with visibility conditions such as time or a device value. This adds no new household sensor; it reorganizes accessories and states already connected to Apple Home. For routine use, stable placement matters alongside quick access. A fan control appearing when the temperature rises may be convenient, but moving neighboring buttons repeatedly could disrupt a learned action. A sensible layout would confine changing information to a clear area while keeping frequently used lights and locks predictable. That is a design implication of the interaction, not a measured outcome for households or a claim that the application enforces that layout automatically.

[S23] [S25]

Share the layout and grant device access separately

Home Sharing passes either the whole setup or selected dashboards to other people, while the owner retains configuration changes. Recipients do not have to position the tiles again, but they still need access through Apple Home. The two entry points can make a problem look misleading: a visible dashboard with unresponsive devices may reflect missing access rather than a bad AI layout. A household handoff should check the recipient’s actual account and accessible rooms before relying on the controls. Success on the administrator’s phone is insufficient. Dashboard support is documented for iPhone, iPad and Mac; the existence of Watch and Apple TV versions does not extend identical dashboard functionality to those screens.

[S26] [S25]

Arrange the actual devices rather than generating for its own sake

Compared with continuing to use Apple Home’s existing controls, Controller offers a way for one person’s arrangement work to serve other household members. It also introduces another configuration to maintain. Adding or replacing an accessory means checking automatically filled sections and older controls, especially where names are similar. The official pricing page offers a seven-day trial and directs users to local App Store prices; a free download does not establish that all capabilities remain free. This is a recent review observation, not proof that version 10 first shipped that day. The practical adoption outcome is whether relatives complete their regular actions themselves and how often the configurator still has to explain or repair the setup.

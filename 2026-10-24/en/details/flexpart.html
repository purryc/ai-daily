Start with the part you mean

A reference image of a toy may need separate body, hand, foot and antenna meshes, yet the creator may not want to trace an accurate mask around every piece before beginning. FlexPart supports lightweight point prompts as well as more detailed boxes and masks within one model. Its intended output is separable three-dimensional geometry, not simply differently colored regions on one intact object. That gives downstream editing, animation or scene assembly a more useful starting point. It does not establish physical manufacturability. The creator specifies the intended decomposition, while unseen surfaces and depth remain inferred from a single view. Those are different kinds of control, and adding a precise outline does not remove all ambiguity about the hidden shape.

Follow the input through to the files

The repository documents a local Gradio workflow: upload an image, remove its background, annotate the desired parts with points or boxes, and save a GLB result. Command-line inference can also export individual part files, an assembled object and an optional rotating preview. When several prompt types are supplied, their part counts and ordering must agree. Boxes derive center points, and masks derive both point and box coordinates. This explains why a box-only interaction can still satisfy the model’s internal point requirement. File-level output gives the creator somewhere concrete to inspect a suspicious component. Background removal is another practical handoff to check, especially when a thin antenna or small protrusion matters to the intended shape.

One model receives coarse and precise guidance

The technical contribution combines image features with part-level geometric prompts in a diffusion transformer. During training, stronger box and mask information helps the weaker point representation learn spatial correspondence. The resulting model can accept different prompt detail without retraining for each interaction mode. The paper also shows sensitivity to where a point lands: points near part boundaries perform worse, while additional box or mask information reduces that sensitivity. For an interface designer, the useful implication is progressive clarification. A creator can start with quick marks and supply more detail where a boundary is ambiguous. It would be misleading to present every click as equally informative, or to suggest that two-dimensional annotation completely determines the depth of an occluded part.

Geometry scores and setup costs need context

The part-error metric is computed on matched pairs; unmatched reference or generated parts receive no extra penalty. A lower geometric error therefore does not prove that nothing was omitted or invented. The three-annotator exercise on fifty objects adds evidence about human-provided prompts, not a broad productivity study. Deployment also requires more than the MIT-licensed project code. The documented pipeline combines FlexPart weights with TripoSG components, and its demo uses a separate background-removal model, each retaining upstream terms. Linux, Python and CUDA are part of the setup, with memory use depending on the chosen configuration. This is a research implementation for teams able to maintain local models, rather than a verified turnkey consumer service with measured modeling-time savings.

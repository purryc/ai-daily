Reaching the room is part of cleaning

In a home with raised doorways, low beds and mixed flooring, a cleaning schedule is useful only if the robot can reach the intended rooms and return to its dock. Qrevo Edge 3 Pro combines a lifting chassis with a retractable navigation tower. Its LiDAR rises for an open-space scan and lowers into the body beneath furniture; Roborock lists access down to about 7.95 cm. That hardware changes which surfaces can belong to one cleaning run, potentially reducing the places an owner must finish with a separate vacuum or reach by carrying the robot. Furniture crossbars and the shape of an actual threshold still matter as much as nominal clearance.

A crossing must work in both directions

Vacuum Wars bought the robot and evaluated it over several weeks. It found a repeatable 57 mm single-step crossing; a 64 mm crossing worked once but could not be repeated for the return to the dock. Its carpet test removed 93% of embedded sand, while obstacle avoidance succeeded on 18 of 24 objects. Those results describe different parts of the job. Good debris removal cannot compensate for getting trapped after cleaning, and a strong climbing result does not establish dependable handling of every toy or cable. For an owner, a completed route includes the return journey. The laboratory’s object set and threshold geometry remain the conditions of these observations.

Mapping, sensing and dock work

The mechanisms have distinct jobs. Retractable LiDAR supports navigation, while the camera, structured light and lateral sensing help interpret nearby objects. An extending side brush and spinning pads reach along edges. The dock handles emptying, pad washing, drying, water refill and detergent dispensing, removing repeated preparation between runs. That does not make dirty-water handling or consumable replacement disappear. Mop lifting and removal also serve different transitions between hard flooring and carpets. Room selection, virtual boundaries and schedules let the owner specify where cleaning is needed. Those controls are more useful than treating a maximum-power whole-home run as the answer to every spill or daily maintenance task.

Buying for the rooms you actually have

The US official store showed $1,299.99 against a $1,499.99 list price at observation, a regional offer rather than a permanent universal price. The model is also identified as Saros20 Neo in Europe, so accessories and comparisons need exact model matching instead of borrowing results from Edge 2 or an older Qrevo Edge. The new independent review gives households with tall transitions and carpets a concrete reason to examine this model. A home mainly troubled by small objects left across the floor still needs a tidying routine. Paying for stronger mobility and a more automated dock may help one part of housekeeping while leaving the owner’s most frustrating step untouched.

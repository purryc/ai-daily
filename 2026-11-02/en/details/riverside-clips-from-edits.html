Choosing the source version

An interview may already have had a digression, mistake or unsuitable passage removed. If highlight generation starts again from the raw recording, an editor has to recognize and remove that material a second time. Riverside’s October 28 update lets Clipper Agent work from a chosen edit, including the version currently open in the editor. The generated clips go into the Magic Clips folder. The useful change is the position of source selection in the workflow: it happens before extraction. This connects an earlier editing decision to the later repurposing job instead of asking the person to reconstruct that decision after every new batch of clips.

Agents connect to the production tools

The update sits inside Riverside’s existing division of production tasks. Producer helps prepare a recording, Editor works on the episode, Clipper extracts shorter material and Marketer prepares distribution drafts. Suggestions can appear after recording finishes, reducing repeated explanation of the same source material. The official interface combines agent conversation, transcript, video and timeline. That arrangement makes it possible to connect a request to an affected part of the recording. The vendor describes a focus on conversations and talking-head video. It should not be interpreted as evidence that the system can automatically construct a satisfactory narrative for every kind of film or visual story.

Inherited cuts still need contextual review

Carrying cuts forward addresses source-version consistency, not the meaning of a standalone excerpt. An answer may depend on a qualification heard earlier in the full interview. A shorter version can lose that qualification even when every retained frame came from an approved edit. Names, subtitles, tone and entry or exit points still need review. A practical trial could produce several lengths from one edit and check both excluded intervals and the meaning of retained speech. The useful measurement would be repeated editing and review work removed, rather than clips generated. No account-based generation was performed here, so accuracy and review time per clip remain unknown.

Approval and costs remain separate decisions

The official explanation says posts and other distribution material remain drafts until a person approves them, with manual intervention available along the way. Mobile review is supported, although a smaller screen does not establish equal suitability for every detailed editing decision. Agents are offered across Riverside plans, while the underlying feature entitlements still differ. Operations such as video enhancement continue to consume AI credits, and a fourteen-day trial is not permanent free access. The October 26 agent introduction and November 1 Product Hunt appearance provide context. This item uses the explicit October 28 edited-source feature date rather than relabeling discovery as a first release.

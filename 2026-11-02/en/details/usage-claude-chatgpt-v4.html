Separate the limits before comparing them

A long task can hit a limit before its owner realizes how much of the week has been used. Usage places reported session and weekly readings in a persistent menu-bar view. Version 4 adds Codex readings from ChatGPT accounts and offers Minimal, Detailed and Chart stacks for multiple accounts. The labels matter: percentages from different providers do not represent equal amounts of remaining work, nor can they be pooled into a transferable balance. The practical benefit is seeing account headroom before beginning another task. The display still depends on what each provider reports and should not be interpreted as a promise about how many requests will succeed.

Make the parsed alert rule inspectable

The maker describes natural-language alerts parsed on-device with Apple Intelligence into rules that can be inspected before saving. Conditions can concern a usage threshold, reset timing or approaching a limit, with account selection. This removes some menu navigation, but “twenty percent left” and “twenty percent used” are materially different requests. Showing the interpreted condition gives the user something concrete to correct. The workflow should be evaluated with threshold crossings and delayed refreshes, not merely whether a sentence becomes a rule. There was no authenticated account test here, and a notification should not be assumed to pause an agent already consuming resources or to prevent the service from enforcing its own limits.

[S20]

Account metadata and credentials travel differently

The privacy policy distinguishes Claude sessions stored in Keychain, which may sync when iCloud Keychain is enabled, from ChatGPT access tokens retained on each device without token synchronization. A ChatGPT account therefore requires sign-in on each device. Account metadata and history have separate iCloud paths. Usage requests go directly to the provider, while diagnostic services are also involved. Signing out of ChatGPT deletes the local tokens and requests revocation. These are the developer’s stated boundaries, not an audit. A person deciding whether to use a third-party monitor should consider the maintained session and the diagnostic flow, rather than assuming that a local-looking widget requires no external communication.

[S22]

Distinguish free monitoring from paid multi-account use

One-account menu-bar monitoring, widgets and basic alerts are free, while multiple accounts, full history and additional developer statistics sit in Pro. The inspected US store lists several monthly purchase entries, so the lowest amount should not be advertised as a universal current offer. Requirements also matter: macOS 15 or later, iOS or iPadOS 26.2, and watchOS 26.4 can exclude older devices. The maker’s index dates its version 4 introduction October 29, making this a seven-day update rather than a new release today. A useful trial compares a widget reading against the official account page and checks whether alerts actually reduce unplanned interruptions, rather than judging the utility by chart richness.

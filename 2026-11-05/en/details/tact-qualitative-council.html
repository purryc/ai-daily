TACT attaches source evidence to multi-model coding and puts human reading before theme review

Inspect themes through their supporting passages

A fluent set of themes can hide uncertainty about which responses support it. TACT asks models from three families to discover candidate codes independently, preserving definitions, examples and source identifiers. Programs check quoted evidence before human review. Researchers first read a sample without seeing model suggestions. This is a useful separation of tasks: finding and organizing passages is delegated, while interpretation remains reviewable. A team evaluating the procedure should inspect what it failed to propose, not only whether existing categories look convincing. Reviewing a polished codebook alone makes an omission especially difficult to notice.

Critique should not silently replace evidence

Lite provides independent coding, reconciliation, human approval and agreement checks. Full adds critiques from other model families and uncertainty screening. Critiques and responses remain in the record rather than silently replacing an earlier decision. The resulting review work is more specific than approving a confidence score. A product researcher can ask whether disagreement concerns missing evidence, overlapping definitions or a legitimate difference in meaning. Those cases need different responses. An implementation should also make discarded and uncovered material easy to revisit, so that a minority concern does not disappear simply because it is inconvenient to group.

Keep evaluation and cost denominators visible

The validation corpus contained 256 public Stack Overflow threads, with reference-label evaluation on 192 and a separate comparison against the authors’ own held-out coding. Final-coding F1 was approximately 0.71–0.72. That is not a general accuracy rate for customer insight. Public training-data exposure and the authors’ involvement constrain interpretation. Their model-call totals were about US$35 and US$86 at the stated historical prices; neither includes researcher labor. An adoption comparison should add preparation, reviewing disagreements and correcting the codebook. A more elaborate council is not automatically economical when a small corpus is already manageable by two people.

Look for shared errors on local material

Different models can still share an interpretation error, especially around metaphor or domain terminology. A local pilot could reserve independently coded material and compare missing themes, unsupported quotations, minority concerns and review time. It must also establish which providers may receive the data. If a change of language or customer group causes extensive repair, the delegated task should be narrowed. Traceability makes a conclusion open to examination; it does not make a sample representative of a market. Nor does it convert a complaint into willingness to pay. Those inferences require evidence beyond consistent assignment of qualitative codes.

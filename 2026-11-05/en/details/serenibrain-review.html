Sensor contact comes before interpretation

The user fits contacts against the forehead and temples, then checks the signal in the phone application. The reviewed device uses one EEG channel and replaceable hydrogel contacts. The reviewer initially left protective film on the pads and could not establish a signal. That small failure illustrates the onboarding work required before interpreting any score. A useful interface should identify the contact that needs adjustment and explain when a session is ready to begin. Detailed reporting cannot compensate for unreliable input. The sensor records an electrical signal; that does not mean the application understands what the wearer is thinking.

Feedback during practice, inspection afterward

The official workflow offers guided meditation, free practice and eyes-open focus sessions. Closed-eye use can rely on sound, while visual feedback is available when looking at the screen. Scores, session states and brainwave trends can be reviewed afterward. This sequencing matters: requiring the person to inspect a graph whenever it changes would repeatedly interrupt the activity being measured. Keeping immediate cues simple and moving detailed inspection to the end can reduce those switches. The product makes a practice session easier to revisit; neither the interface nor the sources establishes that a particular session caused a durable improvement in attention.

Export gives the score an inspectable context

The reviewer requested a CSV export containing raw voltage and multiple frequency-band values. This adds an inspection path beyond a single relaxation score, although access to data does not confer expertise in interpreting it. A person can first annotate the practice method, duration and contact problems before comparing sessions. Blinks, facial movement and changing contact can affect forehead readings, and one channel does not represent the whole brain. For a general consumer, identifying intervals that should not be interpreted may be more useful than displaying additional curves. More visible measurements should not quietly become a stronger claim about what those measurements mean.

Purchase and data boundaries

The store currently lists $179.99, iOS and Android support, and no required subscription. These are observed purchasing terms, not a lifetime price promise. The vendor positions the device as consumer wellness equipment rather than diagnosis or treatment. Its August 29 policy says EEG, scores and reports stay on the user’s device, while account and diagnostic information have separate processing provisions. That is a vendor statement, not an independent network audit. Before buying, consider willingness to wear the band and maintain contacts, then examine failure handling, deletion and export. Someone who only needs a timer and audio guidance can use a simpler meditation application.

A tone edit can change the purpose

Making a paragraph more confident can undermine an intentionally cautious position; making an opening more dramatic can change its audience. IntentWeaver places those purposes beside the draft and distinguishes inferred goals from accepted ones. The product question is whether a writer can identify the level affected by an edit: wording, a particular argument or the overall purpose. A useful trial would introduce a revision that improves a sentence locally while weakening an explicit audience constraint. Generic goals such as clarity provide little help unless the interface connects them to specific passages and the author’s actual reason for writing.

Let the author resolve conflicts

The hierarchy links goals with passages that support or undermine them. Pinning a goal triggers checks on its relationships, while conflicts between explicit intentions block regeneration until the writer changes the specification. That pause can support a deliberate tradeoff, but the model cannot determine the author’s priorities. Evaluate whether a warning identifies a concrete tension and leads back to the relevant wording. Repeated vague warnings could train people to unpin constraints merely to continue. Restoration also needs scrutiny: the returned draft and its goals should describe the same version, so an old text is not silently paired with a newer intention.

Keep short tasks separate from sustained writing

Twenty-four participants completed a within-participant comparison, with blinded experts assessing how final texts fulfilled the writers’ stated intentions. The results support further investigation of goal revisiting and drift awareness. Tasks were short everyday posts, however, and each participant used the new interface for roughly 25 minutes. Drift detection partly relied on retrospective replay judgments. That leaves room for hindsight and does not establish benefits for academic writing or durable learning. A longer trial should inspect whether writers notice obsolete goals and maintain a growing structure over several days, rather than assuming that success with a small graph scales automatically.

Match the structure to the writing task

Simple translation, proofreading and fast replies may not justify maintaining a goal hierarchy. A better pilot uses a document whose audience, stance and substantive requirements genuinely interact, retaining initial goals and successive drafts for comparison. Count lost qualifications, interruptions caused by false warnings and the time needed to correct inferred intentions. More graph nodes are not an outcome in themselves. Commercial access and pricing were not confirmed. A complete research description and interface figure support inspection of the idea, but do not establish procurement readiness, collaboration support or dependable behavior on a team’s long-running documents.

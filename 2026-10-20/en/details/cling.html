User and task

Cling studies tasks where people form judgments across webpages, such as moving qualifying papers into a list or filtering restaurants around a selected hotel. It represents page entities and attributes and offers coordination actions for incremental use. The October19 submission at06:37:54Z belongs to the near-three-day review, not a same-day commercial launch. PM inference: this interaction fits people whose criteria are still developing. When curating candidates or furniture, a person may revise priorities while browsing. Automating copying and lookup can preserve that judgment process. Conversely, a stable and frequently repeated task may be better served by a complete workflow; small steps are not automatically the best design for every automation.

Interaction and responsibility

Users arrange pages side by side, select an entity and choose a nearby matching, filtering or transfer action. A binder between pages shows active relations and editable criteria. Target changes have an in-place preview and confirmation; later confirmations may optionally be skipped. Page models update with DOM changes. PM inference: the design challenge is keeping the selected source and affected destination in view together. Confirmation should reflect scope: changing one date and inserting many rows should not rely on an indistinguishable continue action. With ambiguous names or sale prices, the user needs field-level provenance. Acceptance checks should verify that changing a constraint removes obsolete highlights, not merely that new results appear.

Keep the evidence within its limits

With12 participants, a sensitivity analysis using a240-second timeout reported geometric mean durations of83.0 seconds for Cling,171.8 for manual interaction and177.3 for the end-to-end agent. However, structured tasks used locally archived pages and cached schemas. This is prototype evidence, not a live-web guarantee or an available commercial browser feature. PM inference: adoption needs additional evidence about authenticated pages, changing layouts, permissions, extraction delay and recovery. A buying decision should add those costs back instead of comparing only cached interaction time. This review installed no prototype and ran no participant study. Neither commercial pricing nor effectiveness across arbitrary websites was verified, and those gaps should remain visible.

Alternatives and a falsifiable opportunity

For selecting papers and adding them to a list, manual side-by-side browsing preserves direct judgment but repeats transfer work. A dedicated integration reduces transfers after the data structures are connected. An end-to-end agent better fits an explicit completion criterion. The competition is not simply which option has the fewest clicks; it is which makes each transfer easiest to verify. PM inference: test ten unfamiliar sites with same-name authors, missing fields and a mid-task threshold change. Compare wrong-row insertions, correction time, missed eligible items and added waiting. If checking the result requires repeating every lookup, the benefit disappears. The opportunity is to place a small useful action beside a newly formed judgment, without demanding a complete plan in advance.

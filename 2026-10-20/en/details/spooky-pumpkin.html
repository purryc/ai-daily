Scenario and system loop

Naveen’s plastic pumpkin greets a detected face, transcribes a question with quantized Whisper Small, pairs text with a 224-by-224 camera frame for Qwen3-VL-4B instruct, and speaks a short reply through MeloTTS. A lightweight Qualcomm face model triggers the interaction. Arduino VENTUNO Q supplies the Dragonwing IQ8 NPU; a Logitech C270, speaker, M5Stack FlashLight and printed bracket form the physical interface. App Lab connects services, while the STM32 controls lighting.

State, failures and evidence

Lights distinguish waiting, listening, thinking and replying. The microphone opens for listening; cached greetings and fillers reduce audio contention. The author reports missed initial words during ASR startup and stalls when ASR and TTS overlap. Barge-in and short memory remain future work. The tutorial includes code and an Apache-2.0 label, which does not unify model or hardware licensing. These are author demonstrations, without independent success-rate testing or a network audit.

Product analysis: readiness must mean readiness

PM analysis: this is a promising short interaction exhibit or teaching prototype. A visitor may answer immediately after the greeting, before the recognition session is ready. A fluent greeting can therefore create a timing trap. A product should distinguish starting from ready, establish a short turn-taking instruction, and request repetition when no useful question arrives instead of asking the vision model to guess. Lighting is helpful, but first-time visitors may not understand a breathing pattern. Light, readable text and brief speech should describe the same state. Optional start and cancel buttons would provide a definite entry point in noisy surroundings. These are proposed controls rather than features claimed for the maker build. The key design question is whether a visitor can tell when input is accepted without understanding how ASR sessions or shared audio services work.

Alternatives, adoption and a falsifiable test

A motion-triggered recorded voice remains the simplest alternative: it cannot adapt its words to the scene, but its timing is predictable. The vision-language version earns its added models, power, assembly and maintenance only if seeing costumes, props or scene changes makes responses meaningfully better. A falsifiable exhibit trial would compare prerecorded, speech-only generative and audiovisual versions at the same station. Include masks, two simultaneous visitors, a question immediately after greeting and departure during a reply. Record missing-first-word frequency, the distribution of question-end-to-audible-answer delay, restarts and completed conversations. If vision makes the character more entertaining but much less available, narrow its responses or repair turn-taking before adding memory. A successful local demonstration should not be treated as evidence of unattended operation over a full exhibition day, and planned interruption handling should remain visibly separate from present capabilities.
